## Key Facts (Verified Against IRS.gov)

1. Schedule K-1 is not a standalone tax return — it is a supplemental form attached to Form 1065 (partnerships), Form 1120-S (S corporations), or Form 1041 (trusts and estates). SOURCE: IRS Partner's Instructions for Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) — https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065sk1

2. A partnership does not pay income tax at the entity level. It "passes through" profits and losses to partners, who report their allocated share on their individual Form 1040. SOURCE: IRS About Form 1065 — https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1065 and IRS Publication 541 — https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541

3. A domestic LLC with at least two members that does not file Form 8832 is classified as a partnership for federal income tax purposes and must issue Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) to each member. SOURCE: IRS LLC Filing as a Corporation or Partnership — https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/llc-filing-as-a-corporation-or-partnership

4. A single-member LLC is treated as a disregarded entity and the owner reports income on Schedule C — no K-1 is issued. SOURCE: IRS LLC Filing page (same as above).

5. Partnership returns (Form 1065) and S corporation returns (Form 1120-S) are due March 15 (15th day of the 3rd month after tax year-end). Trust and estate returns (Form 1041) are due April 15. SOURCE: IRS Instructions for Form 1041 — https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1041 (confirms April 15, 2026 for calendar-year entities); IRS Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) Instructions — March 15 deadline confirmed.

6. Partners and shareholders do NOT attach Schedule K-1 to their personal Form 1040 — they keep it for records and use it to complete relevant schedules. SOURCE: IRS Partner's Instructions for Schedule K-1 (Form 1065); IRS Instructions for Schedule E (Form 1040) — https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040se

7. Most K-1 partnership/S-corp income is reported on Schedule E (Form 1040), Part II. Interest income flows to Schedule B; capital gains to Schedule D; rental income to Schedule E Part I. SOURCE: IRS Schedule E Instructions — https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1040se.pdf and Partner's Instructions for K-1 (Form 1065).

8. Trust/estate K-1 (Form 1041) beneficiaries report: interest on Form 1040 line 2b + Schedule B; dividends on line 3b + Schedule B; qualified dividends on line 3a; capital gains on Schedule D. SOURCE: IRS Instructions for Schedule K-1 (Form 1041) for Beneficiaries — https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1041sk1

9. The self-employment (SE) tax rate is 15.3%: 12.4% for Social Security + 2.9% for Medicare. The Social Security portion applies only up to the wage base ($168,600 for the 2024 tax year — [NOTE: 2025 wage base is $176,100 per search results; IRS page fetched cited 2024 figure]). SOURCE: IRS Self-Employment Tax page — https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes

10. Self-employed individuals must file Schedule SE if net earnings from self-employment are $400 or more. SOURCE: IRS Self-Employment Tax page (same as above).

11. Self-employed individuals can deduct approximately half of their SE tax (the "employer-equivalent portion") when calculating adjusted gross income. SOURCE: IRS Self-Employment Tax page.

12. An additional 0.9% Medicare tax applies for high earners: over $200,000 (single), $250,000 (married filing jointly), $125,000 (married filing separately). SOURCE: IRS Self-Employment Tax page.

13. Taxpayers must pay quarterly estimated taxes using Form 1040-ES if they expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year. The four 2025 payment deadlines are: April 15, June 16, September 15, and January 15, 2026. SOURCE: IRS Estimated Tax FAQ — https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax/individuals/individuals-2

14. Form 1099-NEC must be issued to nonemployees paid $600 or more during the calendar year for services. The payer must furnish Copy B to the recipient and file Copy A with the IRS by January 31. SOURCE: IRS Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC — https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec

15. Starting with payments made on or after January 1, 2026, the 1099-NEC threshold increases from $600 to $2,000. For the 2025 tax year (filings in 2026), the $600 threshold still applies. SOURCE: IRS 1099-NEC Instructions; confirmed by search results citing the One Big Beautiful Bill.

16. OnlyFans income is treated as self-employment income; sole proprietors report it on Schedule C. This is the default for a solo creator with no separate business entity. SOURCE: IRS Instructions for Schedule C — https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sc (general self-employment rules apply).

17. Schedule C deductible expenses must be "ordinary and necessary" for the business. Eligible categories include: advertising, vehicle expenses (70 cents/mile standard rate in 2025), home office (Form 8829 or simplified method at $5/sq. ft. up to 300 sq. ft.), equipment/depreciation, professional services, contract labor, supplies, phone/internet, insurance, meals (50%). SOURCE: IRS Instructions for Schedule C (Form 1040) — https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sc; IRS Topic 509 — https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc509

18. An LLC can elect S corporation status by filing Form 8832 (entity classification election) followed by Form 2553 (S election). The S-corp then files Form 1120-S and issues Schedule K-1 (Form 1120-S) to shareholders. SOURCE: IRS LLC Filing as a Corporation or Partnership page.

19. K-1 distributions from an S corporation are generally not subject to self-employment tax — only the shareholder's W-2 salary is subject to payroll taxes. The IRS requires S-corp owner-employees to pay a "reasonable salary." ## Schedule K-1 Deep Dive

### What Schedule K-1 Is

Schedule K-1 is the mechanism through which pass-through entities communicate each owner's or beneficiary's share of the entity's income, deductions, credits, and other tax items. The entity itself typically does not pay federal income tax — instead, these items "pass through" to the individual recipients, who report them on their personal Form 1040.

The K-1 is not filed with the recipient's personal return. It is a source document that tells the recipient what to report on their own return and on which schedules.

### Three Variants

**Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) — Partnerships**
- Issued by: partnerships, multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships, joint ventures
- Filed with: Form 1065 (U.S. Return of Partnership Income)
- Received by: each partner (or LLC member)
- Entity deadline: Form 1065 due March 15; six-month extension available to September 15
- Key IRS source: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065sk1

**Schedule K-1 (Form 1120-S) — S Corporations**
- Issued by: S corporations, LLCs that have elected S-corp status
- Filed with: Form 1120-S (U.S. Income Tax Return for an S Corporation)
- Received by: each shareholder
- Entity deadline: Form 1120-S due March 15; six-month extension available to September 15
- Key IRS source: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120ssk

**Schedule K-1 (Form 1041) — Trusts and Estates**
- Issued by: fiduciary of an estate or trust
- Filed with: Form 1041 (U.S. Income Tax Return for Estates and Trusts)
- Received by: each beneficiary
- Entity deadline: Form 1041 due April 15; five-and-a-half-month extension available to September 30
- Key IRS source: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1041; https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1041sk1

### What K-1 Reports (Form 1065 Box-by-Box Overview)

**Income Items (Boxes 1–11):**
- Box 1: Ordinary business income (loss) — flows to Schedule E, Part II
- Box 2: Net rental real estate income (loss) — Schedule E, Part I
- Box 3: Other net rental income (loss)
- Box 5: Interest income — Schedule B
- Box 6a/6b: Ordinary dividends / Qualified dividends — Schedule B
- Box 7: Royalties
- Box 8: Net short-term capital gain (loss) — Schedule D
- Box 9a: Net long-term capital gain (loss) — Schedule D
- Box 10: Net section 1231 gain (loss)
- Box 11: Other income (loss)

**Deduction/Credit Items (Boxes 12–15):**
- Box 12: Section 179 deduction
- Box 13: Other deductions (including charitable contributions)
- Box 15: Credits (low-income housing, research, work opportunity, etc.)

**Self-Employment:**
- Box 14, Code A: Net earnings (loss) from self-employment — Schedule SE

**Other:**
- Box 16: International transactions (Schedule K-3 attachment)
- Box 17: Alternative minimum tax (AMT) items
- Box 18: Tax-exempt income and non-deductible expenses
- Box 19: Distributions
- Box 20: Other information (including qualified business income for Form 8995)

### How to Report K-1 on Form 1040

| K-1 Item | Where It Goes on Form 1040 |
|---|---|
| Ordinary business income (Box 1, passive) | Schedule E, Part II, Column (g) or (f) |
| Ordinary business income (Box 1, nonpassive) | Schedule E, Part II, Column (k) |
| Rental income (Box 2) | Schedule E, Part I |
| Interest income (Box 5) | Schedule B; Form 1040 line 2b |
| Ordinary dividends (Box 6a) | Schedule B; Form 1040 line 3b |
| Qualified dividends (Box 6b) | Form 1040 line 3a |
| Capital gains (Boxes 8, 9a) | Schedule D |
| Self-employment earnings (Box 14) | Schedule SE |
| Section 179 deduction (Box 12) | Form 4562 |
| Charitable contributions (Box 13) | Schedule A (if itemizing) |

Key rule: Partners do NOT attach Schedule K-1 to their Form 1040. Keep it for records.

### Pass-Through Taxation Mechanics

The entity files an informational return (Form 1065, 1120-S, or 1041). It calculates total income, deductions, and credits at the entity level. It then allocates each partner's/shareholder's/beneficiary's share based on their ownership percentage or the trust/partnership agreement terms. The entity issues K-1s communicating those allocations.

The recipient pays tax on their allocated share whether or not they actually received a cash distribution. This is the fundamental rule: tax liability arises from the allocation, not the distribution.

### LLC + K-1 Scenarios

| LLC Type | Default Tax Treatment | Form Filed | K-1 Issued? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-member LLC | Disregarded entity | Schedule C (owner's 1040) | No |
| Multi-member LLC | Partnership | Form 1065 | Yes — K-1 (1065) to each member |
| LLC electing S-corp | S corporation | Form 1120-S | Yes — K-1 (1120-S) to each shareholder |
| LLC electing C-corp | C corporation | Form 1120 | No (pays its own tax) |

### Common K-1 Scenarios

1. **Real estate limited partnerships (RELPs) / real estate investment syndications**: Passive investors receive K-1 (Form 1065) with rental income, depreciation, and potentially passive activity losses. Very common in real estate crowdfunding and syndication.

2. **S corporation small business owners**: Owner-employee receives both a W-2 (for salary) and a Schedule K-1 (1120-S) (for their share of S-corp profits beyond salary).

3. **Family limited partnerships (FLPs)**: Family members receive K-1 (Form 1065) for their share of partnership income.

4. **Trust beneficiaries**: Individuals receiving distributions from a decedent's estate or an irrevocable trust receive K-1 (Form 1041) reporting their share of trust income.

5. **Publicly traded partnerships (PTPs) / master limited partnerships (MLPs)**: Investors receive K-1 (Form 1065) — often issued late (after March 15), which is a known complexity for investors who hold PTP units.

### K-1 vs. W-2 vs. 1099 — Key Differences

| Feature | K-1 | W-2 | 1099-NEC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who issues it | Pass-through entity (partnership, S-corp, trust) | Employer | Business paying $600+ to nonemployee |
| Recipient | Partner, shareholder, or beneficiary | Employee | Independent contractor / freelancer |
| Tax withholding | None withheld | Federal + state income tax withheld; FICA withheld | None withheld |
| SE tax trigger | Generally yes (active partners); generally no (S-corp distributions, trust) | No (employer pays half) | Yes (recipient pays full 15.3%) |
| Deadline to recipient | March 15 (partnerships/S-corps); April 15 (trusts) | January 31 | January 31 |
| Where reported on 1040 | Primarily Schedule E (some items to B, D, SE) | Form 1040 line 1a; W-2 wages | Schedule C |

### Late K-1s — What Recipients Should Do

K-1s from partnerships and S-corps are due March 15. Extensions push the deadline to September 15. This means a K-1 can legally arrive close to the October 15 extension deadline. If you:
- Have not received your K-1 and April 15 is approaching: file Form 4868 for an automatic extension (note: this only extends the filing deadline, not the payment deadline)
- Pay estimated taxes by April 15 to avoid underpayment penalties
- Receive a K-1 after already filing: file an amended return on Form 1040-X

## OnlyFans Tax Obligations

### How OnlyFans Income Is Classified

The IRS treats an OnlyFans creator operating as a solo content business as a sole proprietor. Income is self-employment income, not wage income. There is no employer withholding taxes on the creator's behalf.

A creator is considered "in business" (not a hobby) when their primary purpose is income or profit and they engage with continuity and regularity. The hobby vs. business distinction matters because hobby expenses cannot exceed hobby income, whereas business losses on Schedule C can offset other income.

### Forms Involved for a Sole Proprietor Creator

1. **Form 1099-NEC** (received): OnlyFans issues this when annual earnings exceed $600. Due to creators by January 31. Available for download on the platform's Banking/Payouts page. Even if no 1099 is received (e.g., earnings under $600), all income must still be reported.

2. **Schedule C (Form 1040)**: Reports all business income and deductible expenses. Net profit is the taxable amount.

3. **Schedule SE (Form 1040)**: Calculates self-employment tax (15.3%) on net earnings. Required when net SE earnings are $400 or more.

4. **Form 1040-ES**: Used to calculate and pay quarterly estimated taxes.

### The 1099-NEC Threshold — Important 2025/2026 Note

- For the **2025 tax year** (returns filed in 2026): OnlyFans issues 1099-NEC for earnings over **$600**. [Confirmed by IRS 1099-NEC instructions]
- Starting **January 1, 2026**: The threshold increases to **$2,000** under new legislation (the "One Big Beautiful Bill"). This applies to 2026 tax year filings.
- Regardless of whether a 1099-NEC is issued, creators must report ALL income. The IRS receives data from payment processors independently.

### Self-Employment Tax Calculation

Net profit from Schedule C × 92.35% = SE tax base
SE tax base × 15.3% = SE tax owed

The 92.35% adjustment accounts for the fact that self-employed individuals effectively pay both the employee and employer share. The employer-equivalent half (approximately 50%) of SE tax is then deductible as an above-the-line deduction on Form 1040.

**Example**: Creator earns $50,000 net profit after business deductions.
- SE tax base: $50,000 × 0.9235 = $46,175
- SE tax: $46,175 × 0.153 = $7,065 (approximately)
- Deduction: ~$3,533 off AGI

### Quarterly Estimated Taxes

OnlyFans creators generally must pay quarterly estimated taxes because OnlyFans does not withhold taxes. The trigger is owing $1,000 or more for the year.

2025 payment deadlines (covering income from each period):
- Q1 (Jan. 1 – Mar. 31): Due **April 15, 2025**
- Q2 (Apr. 1 – May 31): Due **June 16, 2025**
- Q3 (Jun. 1 – Aug. 31): Due **September 15, 2025**
- Q4 (Sep. 1 – Dec. 31): Due **January 15, 2026**

Safe harbor rule: Underpayment penalties are avoided if withholding + estimated payments cover at least 90% of the current year's tax OR 100% of the prior year's tax liability (110% if prior year AGI exceeded $150,000). ## K-1 + OnlyFans Connection

### When an OnlyFans Creator Gets a K-1

The default for a solo OnlyFans creator is Schedule C (sole proprietorship) — no K-1 involved. Schedule K-1 enters the picture only when the creator restructures the business:

**Scenario 1: Multi-Member LLC (Two or More Partners)**
If two OnlyFans creators form a joint LLC — e.g., a creator and a business partner/manager — the LLC defaults to partnership tax treatment. The LLC files Form 1065 and issues a Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) to each member showing their share of profits, losses, deductions, and credits. Each member reports their K-1 on Schedule E of their personal Form 1040. Active members also pay SE tax on their distributive share (Box 14 of K-1).

**Scenario 2: LLC Electing S-Corporation Status**
A creator with substantial net profit (general practitioner guidance: $50,000–$80,000+ in net profit) may elect S-corporation status for their LLC. The mechanics:
1. Form 8832: Elect to be treated as a corporation
2. Form 2553: Elect S corporation status
3. Pay yourself a "reasonable salary" (W-2 income subject to payroll taxes)
4. Take remaining profits as a distribution (reported on Schedule K-1, Form 1120-S)
5. K-1 distributions are NOT subject to SE tax (only the W-2 salary is)

This structure can reduce overall SE tax by shifting a portion of compensation out of self-employment earnings into S-corp distributions. [UNVERIFIED at IRS.gov primary source — well-established tax planning strategy confirmed across multiple CPA-authored sources; the IRS's "reasonable salary" requirement is confirmed at https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues]

**Scenario 3: Trust Beneficiary**
A creator who is a beneficiary of a trust (e.g., a family trust, grantor trust, or testamentary trust) may receive a K-1 (Form 1041) if the trust earns investment income or business income distributed to them. This is less common for younger creators but relevant for estate planning.

### The Tax Impact of Business Structure on Creator Tax Liability

| Structure | K-1 Type | SE Tax on Profits | Complexity | Typical Threshold to Consider |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietor | None (Schedule C) | 15.3% on all net profit | Low | Default / any income level |
| Single-member LLC | None (disregarded entity) | 15.3% on all net profit | Low | Liability protection purpose only |
| Multi-member LLC | K-1 (Form 1065) | 15.3% on active share | Medium | Two or more owners |
| S-corp (LLC + election) | K-1 (Form 1120-S) | Only on W-2 salary | High | $50,000–$80,000+ net profit |

Key insight for creators: The act of forming a single-member LLC alone does NOT trigger K-1 filing requirements and does NOT reduce SE tax. SE tax reduction requires either an S-corp election (K-1 from 1120-S) or multi-member LLC partnership structure (K-1 from 1065).

## Content Gaps & Opportunities

### Gap 1: The K-1 + Creator Connection Nobody Explains
Every competitor in the "what is a schedule k-1" SERP talks about real estate partnerships and family trusts. Zero mention of content creators. There is a meaningful underserved audience of OnlyFans creators (and their accountants) who have graduated to multi-member LLCs or S-corps and now receive K-1s and have no idea what to do with them. A section explaining "here's when YOUR business generates a K-1" would be genuinely novel.

### Gap 2: Accurate K-1 Deadlines
Multiple competitors (including TurboTax) incorrectly state or imply K-1s arrive by January 31. The correct deadlines are:
- Partnerships and S-corps: March 15 (with possible extension to September 15)
- Trusts and estates: April 15 (with possible extension to September 30)
This is a factual error in competitor content and an opportunity to earn trust with accurate IRS-sourced dates.

### Gap 3: Concrete Numerical Examples
No competitor article for "what is a schedule k-1" includes a worked example showing how $100,000 of partnership income flows from Form 1065 → K-1 → Schedule E → Form 1040. A clear numerical walkthrough would dramatically improve educational value and likely improve time-on-page.

### Gap 4: SE Tax Treatment Differences by K-1 Type
The distinction that partnership K-1 income (active partners) IS subject to SE tax, but S-corp K-1 distributions are NOT, is poorly explained in most competitor content. This is a high-value insight for creators choosing between a multi-member LLC and an S-corp election.

### Gap 5: The 2025 1099-NEC Threshold Change for 2026
The increase from $600 to $2,000 (effective January 1, 2026) is relevant, underreported, and timely. An article covering OnlyFans taxes that mentions this change would be more current than most competitors.

### Gap 6: Record-Keeping for Deductions
No OnlyFans tax competitor article adequately covers documentation requirements (receipts, logs, bank records). Creators face disproportionate audit risk due to the personal-vs-business overlap of many potential deductions (home, clothing, equipment). A practical record-keeping section would fill a real user need.

### Gap 7: The Hobby vs. Business Line
The IRS uses a nine-factor test (IRC Section 183) to distinguish a business from a hobby. A creator who fails this test loses deduction rights.
