# What Is a Schedule K-1? A Complete Guide to K-1 Tax Forms

A Schedule K-1 is a tax form that partnerships, S corporations, trusts, and estates use to report each partner's, shareholder's, or beneficiary's share of income, deductions, and credits. The entity itself does not pay federal income taxes on its profits. Instead, those profits pass through to the individual partners, shareholders, or beneficiaries, who then report them on their personal tax returns.

More than 40 million Schedule K-1 forms are issued to taxpayers each year. If you own a stake in a partnership, hold shares in an S corporation, receive distributions from a trust or estate, or run an LLC with multiple members, you will likely receive a K-1. Even OnlyFans creators who form business partnerships or elect S corporation status need to understand how Schedule K-1 taxes work.

This guide covers the three types of K-1 tax forms, what they report, how to file them on your tax return, and when content creators and other self-employed business owners need one.

## What Is a Schedule K-1 Tax Form?

A Schedule K-1 is not a standalone tax return. It is a supplemental form that pass-through entities file with the IRS alongside their entity-level return. The K-1 tells each partner, shareholder, or beneficiary exactly how much income, losses, deductions, and credits they are responsible for reporting on their personal income tax return.

Pass-through entities include partnerships, S corporations, and trusts or estates. These entities file informational tax returns but do not pay federal income taxes at the entity level. The tax liability passes through to the individuals who receive Schedule K-1 forms.

## Who Files Schedule K-1 Tax Forms?

Three types of entities issue Schedule K-1 forms, each with a different parent form.

### Partnerships and Business Partnerships (Form 1065)

Partnerships file [Form 1065](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1065) with the IRS and issue a Schedule K-1 to each partner. This includes general partnerships, limited partnerships, multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships, and joint ventures. The individual partners then report their share of partnership income, deductions, and credits from their K-1 on their personal tax returns.

### S Corporations (Form 1120-S)

An S corporation files [Form 1120-S](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1120-s) and issues a Schedule K-1 to each shareholder. Shareholders report their share of S corp income on their individual tax returns. An LLC can elect S corporation status by filing Form 2553, which then requires K-1 reporting.

### Trusts and Estates (Form 1041)

Trusts and estates file [Form 1041](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1041) and issue a Schedule K-1 to each beneficiary. Estate beneficiaries and trust beneficiaries receive their K-1 showing their share of the entity's income from interest, dividends, capital gains, and other earnings.

## How Does Pass-Through Taxation Work?

Pass-through entities do not pay income taxes at the entity level. Instead, the entity calculates its total income, deductions, and credits, then allocates each partner's or shareholder's share based on their ownership percentage or the partnership agreement terms.

Here is the key rule: you owe taxes on your allocated share of income whether or not you received a cash distribution. Receiving a K-1 means taxes are owed based on the allocation, not based on money you actually received. Your tax liability comes from the taxable income reported on your Schedule K-1. For example, if a partnership earns $200,000 in profit and you own 25%, your K-1 reports $50,000 of income. You pay taxes on that $50,000 even if the partnership kept the money in the business.

## What Does a Schedule K-1 Report?

A K-1 form reports your share of the entity's income, deductions, and credits across multiple categories. For a partnership K-1 (Form 1065), the major items include:

| K-1 Box | Item | Where It Goes on Form 1040 |
|---------|------|---------------------------|
| Box 1 | Ordinary business income | Schedule E, Part II |
| Box 2 | Net rental real estate income | Schedule E, Part I |
| Box 5 | Interest income | Schedule B |
| Box 6a | Ordinary dividends | Schedule B |
| Box 8–9 | Capital gains and losses | Schedule D |
| Box 12 | Section 179 deductions | Form 4562 |
| Box 13 | Other deductions (charitable contributions) | Schedule A |
| Box 14 | Self-employment earnings | Schedule SE |
| Box 15 | Tax credits | Various credit forms |

S corporation K-1 forms and trust/estate K-1 forms follow a similar structure with minor differences in box numbering and applicable items.

### How to Determine Your Taxable Income From a K-1

To determine how a K-1 affects your taxable income, start with Box 1 (ordinary business income) and add any other income items such as interest, dividends, and capital gains reported in the remaining boxes. Subtract any deductions listed on the K-1, including Section 179 deductions and charitable contributions. The result is your net income from that entity. The IRS requires you to report this amount on the forms indicated in the table above, and it flows into your adjusted gross income on Form 1040. If you receive K-1 forms from multiple entities, calculate each one separately and combine the totals. For example, a partner who receives $30,000 of ordinary income and $5,000 of interest from one partnership adds $35,000 to their taxable income for the year.

## How Do You Report a K-1 on Your Tax Return?

You do not attach your Schedule K-1 to your Form 1040. Keep it with your records and use the information to complete the relevant schedules on your personal income tax return.

Most K-1 income from partnerships and S corporations goes on [Schedule E](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040se), Part II. Interest and dividends go on Schedule B. Capital gains go on Schedule D. Self-employment earnings from an active partnership go on Schedule SE, where you calculate your self-employment tax.

Transfer each K-1 line item to the correct schedule. If you receive K-1 forms from multiple entities, report each one separately on Schedule E.

## When Are Schedule K-1 Forms Due?

Schedule K-1 deadlines depend on the entity type and the tax year:

- **Partnerships and S corporations**: K-1 forms are due **March 15** for calendar year entities. A six-month extension pushes the deadline to **September 15**.
- **Trusts and estates**: K-1 forms are due **April 15** for calendar year entities. An extension pushes the deadline to **September 30**.

If you expect to receive a K-1 but it has not arrived by April 15, file [Form 4868](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-4868) for an automatic six-month extension on your personal tax return. Pay estimated taxes by April 15 to avoid underpayment penalties. If you already filed and then receive a late K-1, file an amended return on Form 1040-X.

## How Do LLCs and K-1 Forms Work Together?

A limited liability company (LLC) does not have its own IRS tax classification. Whether your LLC triggers a Schedule K-1 depends on how the IRS [classifies your LLC](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/llc-filing-as-a-corporation-or-partnership):

| LLC Structure | Tax Treatment | K-1 Required? | Income Reported On |
|--------------|---------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Single-member LLC | Disregarded entity | No | Schedule C |
| Multi-member LLC | Partnership (default) | Yes — K-1 (Form 1065) | Schedule E |
| LLC electing S corp | S corporation | Yes — K-1 (Form 1120-S) | Schedule E + W-2 |
| LLC electing C corp | C corporation | No | Form 1120 (entity pays taxes) |

A single-member LLC does not generate a K-1. The owner reports all business income on Schedule C. A multi-member LLC defaults to partnership status and must file Form 1065 with K-1 forms for each member.

## What About OnlyFans Taxes and Schedule K-1?

If you are making money on OnlyFans, understanding your OnlyFans taxes is essential. OnlyFans creators who operate as sole proprietors report their OnlyFans income on Schedule C — no K-1 is involved. OnlyFans issues a [1099-NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec) to creators who earn $600 or more during the calendar year. Creators must report all OnlyFans income even if they do not receive a 1099.

Schedule K-1 enters the picture when OnlyFans creators restructure their business into a partnership or S corporation.

### When OnlyFans Creators Receive a K-1

OnlyFans creators get a K-1 in two common scenarios:

1. **Multi-member LLC**: Two or more creators (or a creator and a manager) form an LLC. The LLC files Form 1065 and issues a K-1 to each member. Active members pay self-employment tax on their share of the profits.
2. **S corporation election**: A creator with $50,000 or more in net profit may elect S corp status for their LLC. The S corp pays the creator a reasonable W-2 salary (subject to payroll taxes) and distributes remaining profits via a Schedule K-1. Those K-1 distributions are not subject to self-employment tax, which can reduce the creator's overall tax liability.

### Self-Employment Tax for OnlyFans Creators

OnlyFans creators pay [self-employment tax](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes) of 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on net earnings over $400. You can deduct half of your SE tax when calculating adjusted gross income.

**Example**: A creator earns $50,000 net profit. SE tax base: $50,000 x 92.35% = $46,175. SE tax owed: $46,175 x 15.3% = approximately $7,065. The deductible portion is about $3,533.

All self-employment income above $400 is subject to SE tax, regardless of whether it comes from OnlyFans, freelancing, or other sources. OnlyFans creators must pay quarterly estimated taxes if they expect to owe $1,000 or more for the tax year. Use [IRS Form 1040-ES](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax/individuals/individuals-2) to calculate and pay quarterly. The 2025 deadlines are April 15, June 16, September 15, and January 15 (2026).

### Common Tax Deductions and Write-Offs for OnlyFans Creators

OnlyFans creators can claim tax deductions — also called tax write-offs — for ordinary and necessary business expenses on Schedule C:

- **Equipment**: cameras, lighting, microphones, computers, editing software
- **Home office deduction**: $5 per square foot (up to 300 sq. ft.) using the simplified method, or actual expenses via Form 8829
- **Business operations**: props, costumes, sets, studio rental, OnlyFans platform fees
- **Professional services**: CPA fees, legal fees, marketing, contractor payments
- **Communication**: internet and phone bills (prorated for business use)

Each tax deduction must be for a legitimate business purpose. The IRS does not allow personal expenses to qualify — only costs directly related to your content creation business. Keep detailed records and receipts for every deduction to support your claims if the IRS questions them.

### How to Stay Compliant With K-1 Tax Obligations

Meeting your tax obligations as a K-1 recipient requires attention to deadlines and accurate reporting. Make all required tax payments on time, including quarterly estimated payments if your K-1 income is significant. Report every line item from your K-1 on the correct IRS schedule — omitting income or misreporting deductions can trigger penalties. If you receive wages from an S corporation alongside a K-1, ensure both are reported accurately. Keep copies of all K-1 forms you receive for at least three years in case the IRS requests documentation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to file a Schedule K-1 with my personal tax return?**

No. You do not attach your K-1 to your Form 1040. Keep the K-1 for your records and use the information to complete Schedule E, Schedule B, Schedule D, and other relevant tax forms on your personal income tax return. The entity that issued the K-1 files it separately with the IRS.

**What happens if I receive my K-1 late?**

File Form 4868 for an automatic extension on your personal tax return. Pay your estimated taxes by April 15 to avoid penalties. Partnerships and S corporations can extend their K-1 deadline to September 15, which means you might not receive your K-1 until close to the October 15 filing deadline for individual tax returns.

**Do OnlyFans creators need a Schedule K-1?**

Only if they operate through a partnership or S corporation. Solo OnlyFans creators report income on Schedule C and pay self-employment taxes directly. A K-1 applies when two or more partners form an LLC or when a creator elects S corporation status for their business. The S corp structure can lower self-employment tax by shifting some income from self-employment earnings to K-1 distributions.

**Is Schedule K-1 income subject to self-employment tax?**

It depends on the entity type. Active partners in a partnership pay self-employment tax on their K-1 income via [Schedule SE](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes). S corporation shareholders do not pay SE tax on K-1 distributions — only their W-2 salary is subject to payroll taxes. Trust and estate beneficiaries generally do not pay SE tax on K-1 income.

**What is the difference between Schedule K-1 from a partnership and an S corporation?**

A partnership K-1 (Form 1065) reports each partner's share of income, and active partners pay self-employment tax on their earnings. An S corporation K-1 (Form 1120-S) reports each shareholder's share of income, but distributions are not subject to self-employment tax. S corp shareholders must receive a reasonable W-2 salary, and the remaining profits pass through on the K-1. This difference in SE tax treatment is why many business owners with higher profits elect S corporation status.
