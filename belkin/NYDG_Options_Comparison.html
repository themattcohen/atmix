Key Structure: Phased Acquisition (Not 100% Upfront)

This is a phased buy-in, not a single purchase. Belkin acquires 40% initially, with call options to purchase additional tranches over 6-10 years.

Phase 1: 40% ownership at closing | Phase 2: Call options for remaining 60% over Years 3-10

The Core Strategy

Dr. Colbert cares about the headline enterprise value - the number he can say his practice sold for. We care about the after-tax present value - what we actually pay in today's dollars after tax benefits.

By structuring with phased acquisitions, tax-deductible payments, extended timelines, and 0% interest periods, we can offer a $10M headline while paying an effective ~$5-6M after-tax. That's 50-60 cents on the dollar.

1. The Phased Structure Explained

Following the model in the existing LOI, this is a management buyout (MBO) where Belkin acquires ownership in phases, not all at once. This is critical for understanding the true economics.

Phase 1: Initial 40% Purchase
At Closing
40% Ownership

Belkin acquires 40% of the practice at closing through:

  • Down Payment: 10% of the 40% purchase price (cash at closing)
  • Seller Note: 90% financed over 7-10 years
  • 0% Interest Period: First 3-5 years
  • Balloon Payment: Remaining principal at end of note term

Example (Option C at $10M valuation): 40% = $4,000,000 purchase price. Down payment of $400,000, seller note of $3,600,000.

Phase 2: Path to 100% Ownership
Years 3-10+
+60% via Call Options

Belkin has call options (right but not obligation) to acquire additional tranches at the original enterprise valuation:

Option Window Tranche Cumulative Terms
Years 3-4 10% 50% Same structure as Phase 1
Years 5-6 10% 60% Same structure
Years 7-8 20% 80% Same structure
Year 10 / Retirement Final 20% 100% Mandatory buyout

Key Benefit: The valuation is LOCKED at the original enterprise value. If the practice grows, Belkin captures 100% of the upside on future tranches.

Tax-Deductible Overlay Payments

In addition to the equity purchase, we structure tax-deductible payments that run alongside the phased acquisition. These are NOT part of the equity purchase - they're separate service agreements:

Consulting Fees

$100K-$150K/year for 5-6 years

For advisory services, patient introductions, strategic guidance. 100% deductible to the practice in year paid.

Non-Compete Payment

$100K-$150K/year for 5 years

For agreeing not to practice within 10 miles. Amortized over 15 years (Section 197).

Transition Services

$100K-$150K/year for 3-5 years

For patient handoffs, staff training, knowledge transfer. 100% deductible in year paid.

Why This Matters

Tax savings of 40%

$2.4M in deductible payments = ~$960K tax savings. Colbert receives the full amount; Belkin's net cost is 60%.

3. Deal Mechanics & Tax Optimization

Six mechanisms work together to reduce the true cost while maximizing Colbert's headline number:

📊

Mechanic #1: Phased Acquisition (40% Now, 60% Later)

How It Works

Instead of buying 100% at closing, Belkin acquires 40% initially. The remaining 60% is purchased via call options over the next 6-10 years at the original valuation. Each subsequent tranche has its own seller note.

Example: Option C ($10M Enterprise Value) Phase 1 (Closing): 40% = $4,000,000
Year 4-5: +10% = $1,000,000
Year 6-7: +10% = $1,000,000
Year 8-9: +20% = $2,000,000
Year 10+: +20% = $2,000,000
// Total: $10,000,000 but paid over 12+ years
Net Impact

Time value of money: $2M paid in Year 10 is worth only ~$926K in today's dollars (at 8% discount). The later the payment, the lower the present value cost to Belkin.

💼

Mechanic #2: Tax-Deductible Consulting Fees

How It Works

Colbert provides advisory services during transition - patient introductions, strategic guidance, brand management. For Belkin: 100% deductible business expense in year paid. For Colbert: Ordinary income (higher tax rate than capital gains, but spread over years).

Example: Option C Consulting Consulting: $125,000/year x 6 years = $750,000
Tax savings @ 40%: $750,000 x 0.40 = $300,000
Effective cost to Belkin: $450,000 (60%)
Net Impact

Colbert receives $750K. Belkin's after-tax cost is $450K. Immediate tax benefit vs. 15-year amortization if it were part of purchase price.

🚫

Mechanic #3: Non-Compete Payment

How It Works

Payment to Colbert for agreeing not to practice dermatology within 10 miles for 5 years. Tax treatment: Amortized over 15 years under Section 197 (slower benefit than consulting, but well-documented for IRS purposes).

Example: Option C Non-Compete Non-Compete: $140,000/year x 5 years = $700,000
Amortization: $700,000 / 15 years = $46,667/year deduction
Annual tax savings: $46,667 x 0.40 = $18,667/year
// PV of 15-year tax savings: ~$140,000
Net Impact

Effective cost: $700K - $140K tax benefit = $560K. Slightly less efficient than consulting due to slower amortization, but highly defensible allocation.

🤝

Mechanic #4: Transition Services Agreement

How It Works

Payment for specific deliverables: patient introductions, referral source handoffs, staff training, EHR system guidance, vendor relationships. 100% deductible as business expense in year paid.

Example: Option C Transition Services Transition: $140,000/year x 5 years = $700,000
Tax savings @ 40%: $700,000 x 0.40 = $280,000
Effective cost to Belkin: $420,000 (60%)
Net Impact

Combined with consulting: $1,450,000 in deductible services, effective cost of ~$870,000. Colbert gets the full amount; Belkin pays 60%.

📉

Mechanic #5: Zero-Interest Periods on Seller Notes

How It Works

Each seller note carries 0% interest for the first 3-5 years, then steps up to AFR (~4.5%). Legal under IRC Section 1274 for seller-financed acquisitions. Belkin gets interest-free financing during the critical stabilization period.

Example: Option C Phase 1 Note ($3.6M) Years 1-5 @ 0%: All payments go to principal reduction
Years 6-10 @ 4.5%: Interest begins accruing
Interest avoided in Years 1-5: ~$650,000
// vs. market rate financing at 7-8%
Net Impact

~$650K+ in interest savings over the life of the note. Plus, payments in Years 6-10 have lower present value than Years 1-5.

📅

Mechanic #6: Extended Amortization with Balloon

How It Works

Notes are amortized over 20-25 years but due in 7-10 years, creating a balloon payment. This keeps annual payments manageable while deferring a large portion to the end when it's worth less in today's dollars.

Example: Option C Phase 1 Note Comparison 10-year full amortization: $360,000/year
25-year amortization: $144,000/year + $2.6M balloon at Year 10
Annual cash flow savings: $216,000/year for 10 years
Net Impact

The $2.6M balloon at Year 10 is worth only ~$1.2M in present value (at 8% discount). You effectively pay 46 cents on the dollar for the balloon.

5. Present Value Calculations

The PV Formula

PV = Future Payment / (1 + r)^n

Where r = discount rate (8%) and n = years from now

Example: $100,000 paid in Year 5 = $100,000 / (1.08)^5 = $68,058 in today's dollars

8. Recommendation

Lead with Option C ($10M Enterprise Value)

Opening Position

"David, we've looked hard at the numbers. The practice had an exceptional 2025 - $13.7 million in revenue, 27% growth. Your brand and the team you've built deserve recognition. We're prepared to value the practice at $10 million."

Structure the Pitch

  • Phase 1: "I'll acquire 40% now - $4 million - with a $400K down payment and seller financing."
  • Path to 100%: "Over the next 6-10 years, I'll acquire the remaining 60% at the same $10M valuation."
  • Consulting: "We'll also pay you $125K/year for 6 years for advisory services - that's $750K additional."
  • Non-Compete & Transition: "Plus $140K/year each for non-compete and transition services."
  • Total: "All in, you're receiving over $12 million for your life's work."

Key Talking Points

  • Tax benefit: "The installment structure saves you roughly $400K+ in taxes vs. a cash deal."
  • Income stream: "You'll receive $400K-550K/year for the first five years."
  • Premium: "This is well above what any outside buyer would pay - they'd need employment guarantees from me."
  • Involvement: "The consulting arrangement keeps you earning and engaged as long as you'd like."

If He Pushes Back on Terms

  • Reduce Phase 1 note term from 10 to 8 years
  • Increase Y1-3 annual payments slightly
  • Reduce 0% period from 5 to 4 years

Hold Firm On

  • Phased structure (40% now, 60% via options)
  • Fixed valuation for all tranches ($10M)
  • Extended amortization with balloons
  • Offset rights (payments from distributions)

Fallback Position

If he rejects the structure, move to Option B ($8M) as a "concession": "If you need a simpler structure with a shorter timeline, we can do $8 million with a 7-year Phase 1 term."
