# FBAR UK Bank Accounts: What US Persons Must Report

Tens of thousands of Americans hold bank accounts in the United Kingdom. Some are expats working in London or Manchester. Others are dual US-UK citizens who grew up with Barclays or Lloyds accounts. Others inherited ISAs or building society accounts from British relatives.

These accounts require FBAR filing. FBAR UK bank accounts — covered by the Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FinCEN Form 114) — must be reported to FinCEN. Under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), any US person with a financial interest in or signature authority over foreign accounts must file. The threshold is $10,000 in total value at any point during the tax year.

UK accounts carry a specific risk. The United States and the United Kingdom signed a FATCA Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) in 2014. Under this agreement, HMRC collects financial data from UK banks and sends that data to the IRS every year. If you skip the FBAR, the IRS may already have records showing the accounts you failed to report.

## Which FBAR UK Bank Accounts Are Reportable?

The FBAR UK bank accounts rule casts a wide net. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), "financial account" covers bank accounts, securities accounts, and other accounts held at foreign financial institutions. Most UK accounts held by US persons must be reported. This includes current accounts, savings accounts, ISAs, building society accounts, NS&I products, pensions, and brokerage accounts.

Here is a breakdown of the most common UK account types and their FBAR status.

### Current Accounts and Savings Accounts

Every UK current account (checking account) and savings account at a UK bank is reportable. This includes accounts at:

- Barclays
- HSBC UK
- Lloyds Banking Group
- NatWest
- Santander UK
- Metro Bank
- Monzo, Starling, and other digital banks

The bank type does not matter. High-street banks, challenger banks, and online-only banks all qualify as foreign financial institutions.

### Individual Savings Accounts (ISAs)

ISAs receive tax-free treatment under UK law. However, the ISA wrapper has no effect on US reporting obligations. Specifically, all ISA types are reportable on the FBAR:

- **Cash ISAs**: Report the maximum cash balance during the year.
- **Stocks and Shares ISAs**: Report the peak portfolio value, including all holdings.
- **Lifetime ISAs (LISAs)**: Report the full account value, including government bonuses.
- **Innovative Finance ISAs**: Report the total value of peer-to-peer lending holdings.

A Cash ISA holding £18,000 converts to about $22,680 at a 1.26 GBP/USD rate — exceeding the $10,000 FBAR threshold on its own.

US persons in the UK who hold both a Cash ISA and a Stocks and Shares ISA must report each one separately on the FBAR, listing the institution, account number, and maximum value for each.

### Building Society Accounts

UK building societies — Nationwide, Yorkshire, Coventry, Skipton, and others — are financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act. Any savings account, fixed-rate bond, or regular saver at a building society is a foreign account. Additionally, these accounts often hold £50,000 or more ($63,000+ USD), far above the $10,000 threshold.

### NS&I (National Savings & Investments)

NS&I is backed by HM Treasury, but its accounts are foreign financial accounts for FBAR purposes. Reportable NS&I products include:

- **Premium Bonds**: Report the face value of your bond holdings, not the prize value.
- **Direct Saver**: Report the maximum balance during the year.
- **Income Bonds**: Report the maximum balance.
- **Green Savings Bonds**: Report the face value of the holding.

NS&I Premium Bonds are commonly overlooked. For instance, dual US-UK citizens frequently hold bonds purchased years ago without realizing they trigger a filing obligation.

### UK Pension Accounts

UK pension accounts are one of the more complex areas of FBAR reporting, as the IRS has not issued definitive guidance on every pension type.

**Self-Invested Personal Pensions (SIPPs)**: Reportable. Because you direct the investment choices, a SIPP is a financial account. Report the total value including all underlying investments.

**Workplace Pensions (Defined Contribution)**: Reportable if you can choose or change your investments. In particular, report the full account value.

**Defined Benefit Pensions (Final Salary)**: Treatment is unclear. Tax practitioners typically exclude them from the FBAR because there is no account balance you can direct — consult a qualified professional for your situation.

**State Pension**: Not reportable. The UK State Pension is a government benefit, not a financial account.

### UK Brokerage and Investment Accounts

Any account at a UK broker or investment platform where you hold securities is reportable. Common UK platforms include:

- Hargreaves Lansdown
- AJ Bell
- Interactive Investor
- Vanguard UK
- Freetrade

Report the peak portfolio value during the year. This includes stocks, bonds, funds, ETFs, and any cash balance within the brokerage account. If you hold both a general investment account and an ISA at the same platform, report each account separately.

## How Do You Convert GBP to USD for FBAR Reporting?

To convert GBP to USD for FBAR reporting, use the US Treasury's official end-of-year exchange rate. Apply this rate to the peak GBP balance of each UK account during the year. Do not use the spot rate on the date of the peak balance or an annual average rate. The December 31 Treasury rate is the only accepted method for FBAR currency conversion.

For the 2025 FBAR, use the Treasury's December 31, 2025 rate from the [Treasury Exchange Rates page](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/treasury-reporting-rates-of-exchange). The December 31, 2024 rate was 1 GBP = 1.2535 USD — verify the current year's rate before filing.

### How the Conversion Works

1. Find the maximum GBP balance of each UK account during 2025.
2. Look up the Treasury's December 31, 2025 rate.
3. Multiply each balance by that rate.
4. Sum all converted values to get the aggregate maximum.

### Conversion Example

Sarah holds three UK accounts:

- Lloyds current account: peak balance of £8,500
- Nationwide ISA: peak balance of £25,000
- NS&I Premium Bonds: £10,000 face value

Using a Treasury rate of 1 GBP = 1.26 USD:

- Lloyds: £8,500 × 1.26 = $10,710
- Nationwide ISA: £25,000 × 1.26 = $31,500
- NS&I Premium Bonds: £10,000 × 1.26 = $12,600

Aggregate maximum value: **$54,810**. Sarah must file an FBAR reporting all three accounts. For more detail on calculating maximum values, see our guide on [how to calculate maximum account value for your FBAR](/blog/how-to-calculate-maximum-account-value-fbar).

## Why Does the US-UK FATCA Agreement Mean the IRS Already Knows About Your Accounts?

The US-UK FATCA Agreement means the IRS already knows about your UK accounts. HMRC shares account data with the IRS each year. Consequently, UK banks report US-person account balances and income to HMRC, which then sends the data to the IRS. As a result, no audit is required — the IRS already holds records of your UK accounts before you file anything.

### What UK Banks Report to HMRC

Under the [Model 1 FATCA Intergovernmental Agreement](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/tax-policy/treaties/Pages/FATCA-Archive.aspx) signed in 2012 and updated in 2014, UK financial institutions must report the following for each account held by a US person:

- Account holder name, address, and US taxpayer identification number (SSN or ITIN)
- Account number
- Account balance or value at year-end
- Interest, dividends, and other income paid to the account
- Gross proceeds from sales of financial assets

### What This Means for FBAR Non-Filers

If you hold UK accounts and do not file the FBAR, the IRS has two sources of information pointing to a violation:

1. **FATCA data from HMRC** showing the accounts exist and their balances.
2. **The absence of your FBAR** in FinCEN's BSA E-Filing system.

Matching these two datasets is straightforward for the IRS. The penalty for a non-willful failure to file is up to **$16,117 per violation** under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)(i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/5321) (2026 inflation-adjusted amount). However, the Supreme Court ruled in *United States v. Bittner* (2023) that non-willful penalties apply per report, not per account. A single missed year is one violation.

Willful violations carry far harsher penalties. The maximum is the greater of **$129,210** or **50% of the account balance** under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The DOJ has prosecuted willful FBAR violations as crimes. Criminal penalties reach **$250,000** and five years in prison under [31 USC 5322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5322).

The FATCA IGA removes any reasonable expectation of privacy for UK accounts. Therefore, filing your FBAR is not optional when the IRS already has the data to verify compliance.

## Common Scenarios for US Persons With UK Accounts

US persons with UK accounts fall into three groups: expats working in the UK, dual citizens who have held accounts since childhood, and US residents who inherited UK accounts. Each case triggers FBAR filing. The examples below show how the aggregate threshold applies.

### Scenario 1: American Expat Working in London

James is a US citizen who moved to London in 2022 for work. He opened a Barclays current account, a Vanguard UK Stocks and Shares ISA, and enrolled in his employer's workplace pension. His account values in 2025:

- Barclays current account: peak £12,000 ($15,120)
- Vanguard UK ISA: peak £45,000 ($56,700)
- Workplace pension (defined contribution): balance £28,000 ($35,280)

Aggregate maximum: **$107,100**. James must file an FBAR for 2025 under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), reporting all three accounts. He should also consider [Form 8938 (FATCA)](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938), which has a higher threshold for expats but may still apply.

James also holds a Chase checking account in the US. Domestic accounts do not appear on the FBAR.

### Scenario 2: Dual US-UK Citizen

Emily is a dual US-UK citizen who has lived in the UK since childhood. She has a Lloyds savings account, NS&I Premium Bonds, and a Cash ISA at Nationwide — all opened when she was a teenager.

- Lloyds savings: peak £6,200 ($7,812)
- NS&I Premium Bonds: £15,000 face value ($18,900)
- Nationwide Cash ISA: peak £9,500 ($11,970)

Aggregate maximum: **$38,682**. Emily must file an FBAR. Dual citizens in Emily's position frequently have never filed because they did not know about the obligation.

If Emily has missed prior years, the [Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures) are the most common remedy. For a detailed overview of penalty exposure, see our article on [FBAR penalties and what happens if you do not file](/blog/fbar-penalties-what-happens-if-you-dont-file).

### Scenario 3: US Person Who Inherited UK Accounts

David is a US citizen living in Ohio. His British grandmother passed away in 2024 and left him £40,000 in a Skipton Building Society account and £20,000 in NS&I Income Bonds.

- Skipton Building Society: peak £40,000 ($50,400)
- NS&I Income Bonds: peak £20,000 ($25,200)

Aggregate maximum: **$75,600**. David must file an FBAR for 2025 under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350) — the first full calendar year he holds the accounts. The FBAR obligation began the moment David had a financial interest in the accounts.

Inherited accounts catch US persons off guard. Moreover, the inheritance itself may not be taxable in the US, but gifts or bequests over $100,000 from a foreign person may require a Form 3520 filing. The ongoing FBAR obligation is separate and applies every year the accounts remain open.

## How to Report Specific UK Account Types

To report UK accounts on the FBAR, you provide the bank name, account number, account type, and peak value in USD. The process is similar for ISAs, building society accounts, NS&I products, and UK pensions. However, each type has specific nuances. The sections below cover the most common types step by step.

### Reporting ISAs

For an ISA, enter:

- **Financial Institution**: The ISA provider (e.g., "Nationwide Building Society" or "Hargreaves Lansdown")
- **Account Number**: Your ISA account number or client reference
- **Account Type**: "Other" with description "Individual Savings Account (ISA)" — or "Bank" for a Cash ISA
- **Maximum Value**: The peak value of the ISA during the year, converted to USD

Report the ISA wrapper account. In other words, you do not list individual stocks or funds held within a Stocks and Shares ISA — the FBAR tracks the account, not the underlying assets.

### Reporting NS&I Premium Bonds

Premium Bonds have no fluctuating balance — you hold them at face value. Report the total face value converted to USD as the maximum value.

- **Financial Institution**: "National Savings & Investments (NS&I)"
- **Account Number**: Your NS&I holder's number
- **Maximum Value**: Total face value of Premium Bonds held, converted to USD

Prize winnings do not increase the FBAR value — they are separate income events.

### Reporting Building Society Accounts

Report building society accounts identically to standard bank accounts: enter the society name, your account or roll number, "Bank" for savings accounts (or "Other" for fixed-rate bonds), and the peak balance converted to USD.

### Reporting UK Pensions (SIPPs)

For a SIPP, enter the pension provider name (e.g., "AJ Bell"), your SIPP account reference, account type "Other — Self-Invested Personal Pension (SIPP)", and the peak total pension value converted to USD.

## When Is the FBAR Deadline, and What Happens If You Miss It?

The FBAR for 2025 is due on **April 15, 2026**. If you miss that date, an automatic extension pushes the deadline to **October 15, 2026** under [31 CFR 1010.306(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306). You do not need to request this extension — it applies to all filers. FinCEN does not charge a late fee between April 15 and October 15. After October 15, however, penalties may apply.

## The FBAR and Form 8938: Two Separate Filings

US persons with UK accounts often need to file both the FBAR and [Form 8938](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938). These are two separate filings with different thresholds, recipients, and penalties.

| | FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) | Form 8938 |
|---|---|---|
| **Filed with** | FinCEN (via BSA E-Filing) | IRS (attached to tax return) |
| **Threshold (domestic filers)** | $10,000 aggregate | $50,000 on last day or $75,000 at any time |
| **Threshold (expats, filing jointly)** | $10,000 aggregate | $400,000 on last day or $600,000 at any time |
| **Penalties** | Up to $16,117 non-willful; up to 50% willful | Up to $10,000 for failure to file |

Expats with UK accounts commonly exceed both thresholds. Furthermore, filing one does not satisfy the other — you must file both reports separately. For a full comparison, see our article on [FBAR vs. Form 8938 differences](/blog/fbar-vs-fatca-form-8938-differences).

## What Options Do First-Time Filers and Late Filers Have?

If you have UK accounts and have never filed an FBAR, you are not alone. The IRS offers three main paths to compliance, depending on whether your non-filing was non-willful or willful. Most late filers qualify for a penalty-reduction program; accordingly, acting sooner matters.

Here are the main options:

- **Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures**: For non-willful non-filers, this program lets you file the last three years of tax returns and six years of FBARs with reduced or zero penalties. See [IRS Streamlined Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures).
- **Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures**: If you have no unreported income, you may file past-due FBARs without penalties. See [IRS Delinquent FBAR Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/delinquent-fbar-submission-procedures).
- **Voluntary Disclosure Practice**: For willful violations, this is the formal path. Penalties will apply, but criminal prosecution becomes far less likely.

Additionally, green card holders with UK accounts should review our guide on [FBAR requirements for green card holders](/blog/fbar-green-card-holders).

## Frequently Asked Questions

These FAQs cover the most common points of confusion about FBAR filing for UK bank accounts. Topics include closed accounts, the $10,000 threshold, ISA tax treatment, joint accounts, and the US-UK tax treaty.

**Do I report a UK account I closed partway through the year?**

Yes. If you held the account at any point during the calendar year and your aggregate foreign account values exceeded $10,000, you must report the closed account. Use the maximum value the account reached before you closed it.

**My UK bank account holds less than $10,000. Do I still file?**

That depends on the aggregate. Sum the maximum values of all your foreign accounts worldwide — not only UK accounts. Suppose you have a €5,000 account in France and a £4,000 account in the UK. The combined value may push you over the $10,000 threshold.

See our guide on [how to calculate maximum account value](/blog/how-to-calculate-maximum-account-value-fbar) for a detailed walkthrough.

**Are ISA gains taxable in the United States?**

ISAs are tax-free in the UK. The US does not recognize the ISA tax wrapper. Therefore, interest, dividends, and capital gains within an ISA are taxable on your US income tax return. This is a US income tax question separate from the FBAR, but it often surprises US persons holding UK ISAs.

**Does the US-UK tax treaty protect me from FBAR penalties?**

No. The [US-UK Income Tax Treaty](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-kingdom-tax-treaty-documents) covers income tax. The FBAR is a Bank Secrecy Act obligation under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314), not an income tax provision. The treaty does not override BSA reporting rules. Consequently, FBAR penalties apply regardless of any treaty position.

**Do joint account holders both need to file an FBAR for UK accounts?**

Yes. Each US person with a financial interest in or signature authority over a foreign account must file separately. If you and your spouse both have access to a UK joint account, both of you may need to file. See our article on [FBAR joint account reporting rules](/blog/fbar-joint-account-reporting-rules) for details.
