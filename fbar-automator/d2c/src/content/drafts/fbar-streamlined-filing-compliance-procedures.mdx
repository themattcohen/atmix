# Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures FBAR: How to Catch Up on Missed Filings

The IRS streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program lets taxpayers who missed prior-year FBARs catch up with reduced penalties. You must certify that your failures were non-willful. Without this program, a willful FBAR violation costs the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance per year under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321).

The streamlined program replaces that exposure with a 5% Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty — or no penalty at all for qualifying expats. This distinction makes the streamlined filing compliance procedures one of the most important options for US persons who failed to file the Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts.

The FBAR, formally FinCEN Form 114, covers any US person who held foreign financial accounts with an aggregate value over $10,000 at any point during the calendar year, under [31 CFR 1010.306](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306). This includes US citizens, US residents, and green card holders.

Taxpayers who missed prior-year filings under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314) can use the streamlined procedures, the Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures, or the IRS Voluntary Disclosure Program. Each path carries different FBAR penalties, eligibility rules, and levels of criminal protection.

This article covers who qualifies for the streamlined filing compliance procedures, how SDOP and SFOP differ, what you must submit, the Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty calculation, certification statement requirements, common mistakes, and the risks.

## What Are the Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures?

The streamlined filing compliance procedures are an IRS program that allows US persons to come into compliance with their FBAR reporting and tax return obligations under a reduced-penalty framework. The Internal Revenue Service introduced this program to encourage voluntary compliance among taxpayers whose failures were non-willful.

The program addresses two separate obligations. First, it covers the FBAR filing requirement under the Bank Secrecy Act at [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314). The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) administers FBAR filings through the BSA E-Filing system.

Second, the streamlined procedures address unreported foreign income on your federal tax returns. You must file amended tax returns for the three most recent tax years. You must also file FinCEN Form 114 for the six most recent calendar years.

The streamlined program does not erase the past filings. However, it brings them current under more favorable penalty terms than a standard FBAR enforcement action would produce. Fixing only the FBARs without amending your tax returns will not satisfy the IRS streamlined filing compliance procedures program rules.

### Who Is a US Person for FBAR Purposes?

Under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), a US person includes any US citizen, US resident, or entity organized under US law. Green card holders qualify as US persons regardless of where they live.

This definition is broader than many taxpayers realize. A US citizen living abroad for decades still has FBAR reporting obligations. A green card holder who has never filed a US tax return still must report foreign financial accounts to FinCEN.

The $10,000 aggregate value threshold applies across all foreign financial accounts held by the US person during the calendar year. If the combined maximum balances exceed $10,000 at any point, the FBAR filing requirement applies for that entire year.

## How to Demonstrate Non-Willful Conduct

### What Is Non-Willful Conduct?

Non-willful conduct is failure that results from negligence, inadvertence, or mistake under the Bank Secrecy Act framework. It does not include intentional disregard of known legal duties. The Internal Revenue Service evaluates each certification statement individually.

Courts have interpreted non-willful conduct to include:

- Failing to know that FBAR reporting applied to foreign financial accounts
- Misunderstanding which accounts crossed the $10,000 aggregate value threshold under [31 CFR 1010.306](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306)
- Relying on a tax preparer who never asked about foreign accounts
- Being a dual citizen unaware of US person filing obligations under the Bank Secrecy Act
- Inheriting foreign financial accounts without understanding the FBAR filing requirement
- Maintaining childhood accounts opened by parents in a foreign country

Non-willful conduct does not include hiding accounts on purpose, structuring transactions to avoid reporting, or ignoring clear IRS warnings about foreign account reporting.

### Writing a Strong Non-Willful Certification Narrative

Your certification statement must explain specifically why your failures were non-willful. The IRS rejects vague or conclusory statements. A bare assertion that you "did not know about FBAR" is not sufficient.

Describe the facts chronologically. Explain how you opened each foreign financial account. Identify what tax advice you received and from whom. State whether your tax return preparer ever asked about foreign accounts.

If you are a US citizen who grew up abroad, explain when you first learned about FBAR filing obligations. If you relied on professional advice, identify the advisor and describe what they told you about reporting foreign financial accounts.

The certification statement is signed under penalty of perjury. False statements can result in criminal prosecution. Only include facts you can verify. Do not exaggerate your ignorance or fabricate details about professional advice.

### What Penalties Does the Streamlined Program Replace?

The standard non-willful FBAR penalty is up to $16,117 per violation (2025 figure) under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The Supreme Court held in *Bittner v. United States*, 598 U.S. 85 (2023), that this penalty applies once per year — not once per account.

The willful FBAR penalty is the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance per year under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The streamlined program replaces these FBAR penalties with either the 5% SDOP penalty or no penalty under SFOP.

For a US person with six years of missed FBAR filings, the cumulative non-willful penalty exposure could reach $96,702 (6 x $16,117). The streamlined procedures reduce that to a single 5% calculation or zero for qualifying expats.

## How Do the Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures FBAR Tracks Differ?

The streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program offers two tracks. SDOP (Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures) covers US residents. SFOP (Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures) covers qualifying expats. The key difference is the penalty.

SDOP charges 5% of the highest aggregate value as the Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty. SFOP charges nothing for taxpayers who meet the non-residency test.

| Feature | SDOP (Domestic) | SFOP (Foreign) |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| Who qualifies | US residents | Taxpayers living abroad |
| FBAR/offshore penalty | 5% of highest aggregate value | No penalty |
| Non-residency test | Not required | 330+ days abroad in at least 1 covered year |
| Certification form | Form 14654 | Form 14653 |
| Returns to amend | 3 most recent tax years | 3 most recent tax years |
| FBARs to file | 6 most recent calendar years | 6 most recent calendar years |
| Tax owed | Tax + interest + 5% penalty | Tax + interest only |
| Willful penalty avoided | Up to $100,000+ per year per [31 USC 5321](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321) | Up to $100,000+ per year |

See the [IRS Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures) for current guidance on both tracks.

### Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures (SDOP) Eligibility

To qualify for SDOP, you must meet all of the following criteria. The Internal Revenue Service will deny your streamlined filing if any of these conditions is not satisfied.

You must be a US citizen, US resident, or green card holder who filed a US tax return for at least one of the three most recent tax years. You must not be a resident outside the United States under the IRS non-residency test.

You must not be under IRS audit, examination, or criminal investigation for any of the years covered by your streamlined submission. If the IRS has already contacted you about your foreign financial accounts, you cannot use SDOP.

Your failure to report foreign financial accounts and pay all tax due must have been non-willful conduct. You must certify this on Form 14654 under penalty of perjury.

You must not have previously filed under the streamlined filing compliance procedures for the same years. The IRS does not allow repeat submissions for years already covered.

### Title 26 Miscellaneous Offshore Penalty Under SDOP

Under SDOP, the IRS replaces the standard FBAR penalty with a single 5% Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty. The Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures penalty calculation is straightforward.

You find the year with the highest aggregate value across all foreign financial accounts and assets during the six-year lookback period. You multiply that balance by 5%. You pay that amount once — not per year and not per account.

For example, if your highest aggregate value was $200,000, the SDOP penalty is $10,000. That is well below the $100,000-per-year willful penalty under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321).

For a $400,000 peak balance, the Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty is $20,000. For a $100,000 peak balance, the penalty is $5,000.

The penalty base includes foreign financial accounts reportable on FinCEN Form 114 and foreign financial assets reportable on Form 8938. Assets you reported on Form 8938 but not on the FBAR may be excluded from the penalty base in some cases. Consult a qualified tax professional before excluding any asset.

### Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures (SFOP) Eligibility

To qualify for SFOP, you must meet the IRS non-residency test in addition to the general eligibility criteria. SFOP offers the most favorable terms of the streamlined filing compliance procedures.

For US citizens and green card holders, you must not have had a US abode during any of the three most recent tax years you are amending. You must also have been physically outside the United States for at least 330 full days in any one or more of those three calendar years.

This non-residency test mirrors the physical presence test for the foreign earned income exclusion under IRC Section 911. A US citizen living in London who was present in the US for fewer than 35 days in a calendar year would meet this test.

For US residents who are not US citizens, you must not have met the substantial presence test under [26 USC 7701(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/7701) for any one or more of the three most recent tax years.

### SFOP Zero Penalty for Qualifying Expats

Under SFOP, there is no FBAR penalty and no Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty on unreported income. You pay only the tax owed on unreported foreign income from your foreign financial accounts plus statutory interest.

You must still certify non-willful conduct under SFOP on Form 14653. The absence of a penalty does not make the certification statement optional. A false certification can expose you to perjury charges. It can also turn what would have been a civil matter into a criminal referral.

The SFOP track saves qualifying US persons significant money compared to SDOP. A US citizen abroad with a $500,000 peak balance would pay $25,000 under SDOP. Under SFOP, the same US person pays only unpaid tax and interest — no offshore penalty.

## What Documents Must You Submit for the Streamlined Filing Procedures?

Both SDOP and SFOP require the same four-part document package. The streamlined filing compliance procedures require all four elements in one coordinated submission to the Internal Revenue Service. Missing any element can cause the IRS to treat your package as a routine delinquent filing — and you lose the penalty reduction.

### Required Documents for Both Tracks

Submit all of the following together:

1. **Amended tax returns** — File amended Forms 1040-X for the three most recent tax years whose due dates have passed, per [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures). Include Schedule B for foreign interest and dividends. Include Form 8938 if the foreign financial asset thresholds apply under [26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D) ($50,000 for domestic filers). Include Form 1116 for foreign tax credits.
2. **Late FBARs** — File FinCEN Form 114 for each of the six most recent calendar years in which you held a reportable foreign financial account. Use the [BSA E-Filing system](https://bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov/) at the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network. Mark each FBAR as a late filing.
3. **Certification statement** — Complete and sign Form 14654 (for SDOP) or Form 14653 (for SFOP). Explain in detail why your failures were non-willful conduct. A bare statement typically fails. Describe how you opened the accounts, what advice you received, and why your failures were mistakes.
4. **Payment** — For SDOP, include the 5% Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty with your amended tax returns. For both tracks, pay all tax owed and applicable interest using EFTPS.

Write "Streamlined Foreign Offshore" or "Streamlined Domestic Offshore" on top of each amended return. Mail everything together to the IRS address on the certification form. Use certified mail and keep proof of delivery.

### Certification Statement Requirements: Form 14654 vs Form 14653

The certification statement is the most important document in your streamlined filing compliance procedures submission. The IRS uses it to determine whether your conduct was non-willful.

**Form 14654** is the certification for Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures. It requires your personal information, a list of all foreign financial accounts, and a detailed narrative explaining your non-willful conduct. You must also calculate and report the Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty.

**Form 14653** is the certification for Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures. It requires the same personal information and narrative. It also requires documentation proving you meet the non-residency test for at least one of the three covered tax years.

Both forms require a signature under penalty of perjury. Both require you to list every foreign financial account held during the six calendar years covered by your FBAR filings. Omitting an account from your certification statement while including it on your FinCEN Form 114 creates an inconsistency that the IRS will scrutinize.

### What to Include in Your Amended Tax Returns

Each amended tax return must include all previously unreported foreign income. This includes interest earned on foreign financial accounts, dividends from foreign investments, and rental income from foreign property.

Attach Schedule B to report foreign interest and dividends. Check "Yes" on Part III, which asks whether you have a financial interest in or signature authority over foreign financial accounts.

If your foreign financial assets exceed the $50,000 threshold ($200,000 for expats), attach Form 8938 (Statement of Specified Foreign Financial Assets) as required under [26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D). Claim foreign tax credits on Form 1116 to avoid double taxation.

## What Are the Risks of the Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures?

The streamlined filing compliance procedures are not a guaranteed amnesty. The Internal Revenue Service can reject your certification, assess full statutory FBAR penalties, or refer your case to criminal investigators. You should understand three specific risks before you submit.

### Risk 1: IRS Rejection of the Non-Willful Certification

The IRS can review your certification statement and decide your conduct was willful. If that happens, the IRS can assess the full statutory FBAR penalty under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). That penalty is the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance per year.

The Internal Revenue Service has five years from each violation to assess FBAR penalties under [31 USC 5321(b)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). A $500,000 foreign financial account balance triggers a $250,000 willful penalty per year.

Ten unreported calendar years at that balance could push exposure well above $1,000,000. This is why the certification statement must be thorough and accurate.

### Risk 2: Criminal Referral

The IRS can refer your case to the Department of Justice. This happens when your submission reveals facts suggesting willful tax evasion or deliberate FBAR violations under the Bank Secrecy Act.

This risk is low for taxpayers with genuinely non-willful failures. It is real for anyone who signed tax returns without disclosing foreign financial accounts while knowing those accounts existed. Criminal penalties for Bank Secrecy Act violations can include up to five years in prison under [31 USC 5322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5322).

### Risk 3: Audit Before Acceptance

If the IRS opens an examination of your tax returns before processing your streamlined submission, you lose access to the program for those years. The program excludes taxpayers already under examination. This is why timely submission matters.

## Streamlined vs Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures

The Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures are a separate IRS option for taxpayers who missed FBAR filings but have no unreported foreign income. Understanding the difference helps you choose the right path.

### When to Use Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures

The Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures apply only when your foreign financial accounts did not generate any unreported income. You properly reported all income on your tax returns. You simply missed the FinCEN Form 114 filings.

Under the delinquent procedures, you file the late FBARs through the BSA E-Filing system with a reasonable cause statement. There is no penalty. There are no amended tax returns to file.

### When Streamlined Is Required Instead

If your foreign financial accounts earned interest, dividends, or other income that you did not report on your tax return, you cannot use the Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures. You need the streamlined filing compliance procedures or the Voluntary Disclosure Program instead.

The streamlined procedures require both late FBARs and amended tax returns. The delinquent procedures require only the late FBARs. This is the key distinction.

## Streamlined vs Voluntary Disclosure Program

The Voluntary Disclosure Program provides criminal protection that the streamlined filing compliance procedures do not. The two programs serve fundamentally different situations.

### When to Choose Voluntary Disclosure

If you cannot certify non-willful conduct with confidence, the Voluntary Disclosure Program is the safer path. It provides protection from criminal prosecution in exchange for full cooperation and payment of negotiated penalties.

The Voluntary Disclosure Program is appropriate when you knew about the FBAR filing requirement and chose not to file. It is also appropriate when you are uncertain whether your conduct was willful or non-willful.

### When Streamlined Is the Better Option

For taxpayers with genuinely non-willful conduct, the streamlined filing compliance procedures offer lower penalties. SDOP charges only the 5% Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty. SFOP charges no penalty at all.

The Voluntary Disclosure Program penalties are typically higher. However, the criminal protection makes VDP essential for anyone with willful conduct.

## Common Mistakes in Streamlined Filings

Taxpayers who use the streamlined filing compliance procedures often make errors that delay processing or cause rejection. Avoid these mistakes.

### Filing FBARs Before Amended Returns

The IRS expects a coordinated submission. Filing your FinCEN Form 114 through the BSA E-Filing system weeks before mailing your amended tax returns and certification statement creates a disconnected package. File the FBARs and mail the paper package within the same timeframe.

### Submitting a Weak Certification Statement

A one-paragraph certification that says "I did not know about FBAR" is insufficient. The Internal Revenue Service expects a detailed narrative explaining the specific facts of your situation. Describe when you opened each foreign financial account, what tax advice you received, and why you did not know about the reporting requirement.

### Using the Wrong Certification Form

SDOP requires Form 14654. SFOP requires Form 14653. Using the wrong form invalidates your streamlined submission. The IRS will not automatically convert your filing to the correct track.

### Miscalculating the Title 26 Miscellaneous Offshore Penalty

The SDOP penalty is based on the single highest aggregate value across all foreign financial accounts during the six-year lookback period. Taxpayers sometimes calculate the penalty on the wrong year or forget to include all accounts in the aggregate value calculation.

### Omitting Foreign Financial Accounts

Every foreign financial account must appear on both your FinCEN Form 114 filings and your certification statement. Omitting an account from one document while listing it on another creates an inconsistency that triggers IRS scrutiny.

### Forgetting Form 8938

If your foreign financial assets exceed the reporting threshold under [26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D), you must attach Form 8938 to each amended tax return. The FBAR and Form 8938 are separate requirements with different thresholds. The FBAR threshold is $10,000 aggregate value. The Form 8938 threshold is $50,000 for domestic filers.

## How Do You Submit a Streamlined FBAR Package?

Submitting the streamlined filing compliance procedures package requires a specific sequence. Do not file FBARs before the amended returns. Do not pay the penalty without the certification form. Follow these eight steps in order.

1. **Identify the covered years.** Find the three most recent tax years with past due dates per IRS instructions. Find the six most recent calendar years for FBAR purposes.
2. **Gather foreign account records.** Collect account statements for all six FBAR years. You need the highest balance in each calendar year, the account number, the institution name and address, and any income credited. Use Treasury exchange rates from [fiscal.treasury.gov](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/reports-statements/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/current.html) to convert foreign currency balances at the December 31 rate for each year.
3. **Amend your tax returns.** Prepare amended Forms 1040-X for the three covered tax years. Report unreported foreign income and claim foreign tax credits on Form 1116. Attach Schedule B and Form 8938 if the $50,000 threshold under [26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D) applies.
4. **File the FBARs online.** Log into the BSA E-Filing system at [bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov](https://bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov/) and file FinCEN Form 114 for each of the six calendar years. Select the correct calendar year. Save your confirmation numbers.
5. **Draft your non-willful certification.** Write a detailed narrative for your certification statement. Describe how you opened each foreign financial account, what advice you received, and why you did not know about the FBAR filing obligation.
6. **Complete Form 14654 or Form 14653.** Transfer your narrative to the correct IRS form. Sign under penalty of perjury. For SDOP, complete Form 14654. For SFOP, complete Form 14653.
7. **Calculate and pay the SDOP penalty.** For SDOP, find the calendar year with the highest aggregate value. Multiply by 5% to determine the Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty. For example, $400,000 x 5% = $20,000. Include that payment plus all tax owed and interest.
8. **Mail the complete package.** Send amended tax returns, signed certification statement, and penalty payment to the IRS address on the certification form. Use certified mail and keep proof of delivery.

## What Happens After You Submit Your Streamlined Package?

After you mail your streamlined filing compliance procedures package to the Internal Revenue Service and file your FinCEN Form 114 forms through the BSA E-Filing system, the IRS begins processing your submission.

### IRS Processing Timeline

The IRS does not provide a guaranteed processing timeline for streamlined submissions. Based on publicly available practitioner reports, processing typically takes six to twelve months. Some cases take longer.

During this period, the IRS reviews your amended tax returns, verifies your certification statement, and confirms receipt of the Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty payment for SDOP filers. The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network processes your FBAR filings separately through its own system.

### What to Expect During Review

The IRS may accept your streamlined submission without further contact. This is the most common outcome for taxpayers with straightforward non-willful conduct situations.

In some cases, the Internal Revenue Service may request additional documentation. This does not mean your submission was rejected. Respond promptly to any IRS correspondence.

If the IRS determines that your conduct was willful rather than non-willful, it can reject your streamlined filing and assess full statutory FBAR penalties. You should consult a tax attorney immediately if you receive a rejection notice.

### Keeping Records After Submission

Retain copies of everything you submitted for at least seven years. Keep your certified mail receipt, FinCEN Form 114 confirmation numbers, copies of all amended tax returns, and your signed certification statement.

These records protect you if the IRS later questions your streamlined submission. They also document your good faith effort to come into compliance with FBAR reporting requirements under the Bank Secrecy Act.

## Frequently Asked Questions

These questions cover the most common issues taxpayers face when using the IRS streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program. The answers apply to both SDOP and SFOP unless stated otherwise.

**What is the IRS streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program?**

The streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program is an IRS initiative that allows US taxpayers to catch up on missed Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts filings under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314) with reduced penalties. You must certify non-willful conduct and file three years of amended tax returns.

You must also file six years of FBARs through the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network BSA E-Filing system. Domestic filers pay the 5% SDOP Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty. Qualifying expats under SFOP pay no penalty at all.

**Can I use the streamlined procedures if my foreign financial accounts had no income?**

Yes. The streamlined filing compliance procedures apply when you missed FBARs, regardless of whether the accounts generated taxable income. If there is no unreported income, you may qualify for the [Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures](/blog/fbar-delinquent-filing-procedures) instead — those carry no penalty.

However, if your foreign financial accounts earned any foreign interest or dividends, you need the streamlined procedures or the Voluntary Disclosure Program. The Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures alone are not sufficient when unreported income exists.

**What if I cannot certify non-willful conduct with confidence?**

Do not use the streamlined program. A false certification statement can result in the full willful FBAR penalty under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). That penalty is the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance per year.

You also face criminal exposure for perjury. The IRS Voluntary Disclosure Program offers criminal protection and negotiated terms for taxpayers who cannot certify non-willful conduct. See [FBAR Willful vs Non-Willful Penalties](/blog/fbar-willful-vs-non-willful-penalties) for how to assess which path fits your situation.

**How does the Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures penalty calculation work?**

The SDOP penalty is 5% of the highest aggregate value across all foreign financial accounts and assets during the six FBAR calendar years. You pay the Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty once — not per year.

If your accounts peaked at $300,000 in year three of the lookback, the SDOP penalty is $15,000 total. That is 5% of $300,000, calculated under [31 CFR 1010.306](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306) reporting rules. You do not multiply $15,000 by six.

**What if the IRS opens an audit after I file my streamlined submission?**

If the Internal Revenue Service opens an examination after you file but before they process your submission, your access to the streamlined filing compliance procedures ends for those years. The IRS then treats your package as a delinquent or amended filing.

It can assess full statutory FBAR penalties. Contact a tax attorney right away if the IRS contacts you after you file a streamlined package.

**How does the streamlined program interact with the $16,117 non-willful FBAR penalty?**

The standard non-willful FBAR penalty is up to $16,117 per year (2025 figure) under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The streamlined program replaces that per-year penalty with either the 5% SDOP Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty or no penalty under SFOP.

For most US persons, the streamlined result is far lower than the cumulative standard FBAR penalties. This is especially true for those with multiple calendar years of unreported Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts filings.

**Can I use the streamlined procedures for FBAR penalties already assessed?**

No. The streamlined program covers only calendar years with no prior IRS penalty assessment. If the Internal Revenue Service assessed FBAR penalties for a prior year, that year is not eligible.

However, you may have other options. These include the [Reasonable Cause Defense](/blog/fbar-reasonable-cause-defense) or a collection due process appeal. The streamlined filing compliance procedures are not available for years where penalties have already been assessed.

**Do I need a tax attorney or CPA for the streamlined filing compliance procedures?**

The IRS does not require professional representation. However, the streamlined procedures involve complex interactions between FBAR reporting under the Bank Secrecy Act and federal income tax obligations. The certification statement must be carefully drafted.

Errors in the amended tax returns, the Title 26 miscellaneous offshore penalty calculation, or the certification statement can result in rejection. Many taxpayers benefit from working with a tax professional experienced in FBAR compliance and the streamlined filing compliance procedures.
