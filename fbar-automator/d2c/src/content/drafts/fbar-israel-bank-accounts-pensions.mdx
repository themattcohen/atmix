# FBAR Israel Bank Accounts: Reporting Israeli Pensions and Accounts

An estimated 200,000 to 300,000 US citizens also hold Israeli citizenship. Dual citizens typically hold Israeli bank accounts, provident funds, training funds, and managers' insurance policies. Whether you live in Tel Aviv or in New Jersey, your Israeli financial accounts likely trigger FBAR filing.

The Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FinCEN Form 114) requires any US person with a financial interest in or signature authority over foreign financial accounts to file when the aggregate value exceeds $10,000. Under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), this duty covers Israeli bank accounts, savings funds, pension accounts, and insurance-linked savings products.

FBAR Israel bank accounts also fall under FATCA reporting. Israel signed a Model 1 FATCA treaty with the US in 2014. This is an Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA). Israeli banks report account data to the Israel Tax Authority (ITA), which then shares that data with the IRS. As a result, the Internal Revenue Service holds records of your Israeli accounts before you file FinCEN Form 114.

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) administers the Bank Secrecy Act and processes all FBAR filings. Every US person — including US citizens, US residents, and green card holders — must file the FBAR when their foreign bank accounts cross the $10,000 threshold. Dual US-Israeli citizens are no exception. The FBAR reporting obligation applies regardless of where you live or how long you have held the accounts.

## Which FBAR Israel Bank Accounts and Financial Products Must You Report?

All standard Israeli bank accounts are reportable under the FBAR. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), a "financial account" covers bank accounts, securities accounts, and similar accounts at foreign banks. Israeli bank accounts at Bank Leumi, Bank Hapoalim, Israel Discount Bank, and Mizrahi-Tefahot all qualify. They trigger FBAR filing requirements.

Israeli pension and savings products are more complex. However, most are reportable. The table below summarizes the most common Israeli financial products and their FBAR status.

| Standard bank account (checking/savings) | חשבון בנק | Yes |
| Foreign currency account | חשבון מט"ח | Yes |
| Savings plan (tochniut chisachon) | תוכנית חיסכון | Yes |
| Provident fund | קופת גמל (kupat gemel) | Yes |
| Training fund / education fund | קרן השתלמות (keren hishtalmut) | Yes |

### Types of Israeli Bank Accounts: Shekel, Foreign Currency, and Savings Plans

Israeli banks offer several account types. Each one counts as a foreign financial account for FBAR purposes. Understanding the different types helps you avoid leaving an account off your filing.

**Shekel current accounts** (חשבון עו"ש) are the most common Israeli bank accounts. Nearly every Israeli resident holds one. The account is denominated in the New Israeli Shekel (ILS). All deposits, salary payments, and bill withdrawals flow through this account. For FBAR reporting, you must convert the maximum ILS balance to USD using the Treasury's year-end exchange rate.

**Foreign currency accounts** (חשבון מט"ח) hold balances in USD, EUR, GBP, or other currencies. Many dual citizens keep a USD-denominated account at their Israeli bank. Even though the balance is already in dollars, it is still a foreign bank account held at a foreign financial institution. You must report the maximum account value on the FBAR.

**Savings plans** (תוכנית חיסכון, or tochniut chisachon) are fixed-term deposit products offered by Israeli banks. You deposit a lump sum or make monthly payments over a set period — typically one to five years. The bank pays interest. These savings plans are foreign financial accounts under the Bank Secrecy Act. Report the maximum value during the calendar year, including accrued interest.

**Linked deposit accounts** (פיקדון צמוד) are deposits tied to the Consumer Price Index (CPI) or to the exchange rate. Israeli banks offer CPI-linked and dollar-linked deposits. These are reportable on the FBAR as foreign financial accounts. The maximum value is the peak balance during the year, converted to USD at the Treasury rate.

### Israeli Bank Accounts (Bank Leumi, Hapoalim, Discount, Mizrahi)

The four major Israeli banks — Bank Leumi, Bank Hapoalim, Israel Discount Bank, and Mizrahi-Tefahot — are foreign financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA). Every account you hold at these banks is a foreign financial account. This covers deposit accounts, savings accounts, and investment accounts.

Israeli current accounts (חשבון עו"ש) and savings accounts (חשבון חיסכון) are reportable. Dual US-Israeli citizens often opened these accounts using a Teudat Zehut (Israeli ID number) before learning about their US tax duties. In either case, the account currency — New Israeli Shekel (NIS/ILS) or US dollar — does not change the FBAR filing duty.

Smaller Israeli banks and credit unions also trigger the FBAR. Accounts at Bank Yahav, Bank Otsar Ha-Hayal, Bank Massad, and Israel Post Bank are all foreign bank accounts. The size of the bank does not matter. Any account at any Israeli financial institution is a foreign financial account under the Bank Secrecy Act.

### Kupat Gemel (Provident Fund) FBAR Reporting

A kupat gemel (קופת גמל) is a provident fund for long-term savings. Employers and employees each add pre-tax NIS amounts each month. The fund invests those payments. You can withdraw at age 60. You may also get a lump sum under certain conditions.

Kupot gemel are reportable on the FBAR. The IRS treats account-style foreign pension plans as foreign financial accounts when the holder has a financial interest in a specific fund balance. So you must report the maximum NIS balance during the calendar year and convert that balance to USD using the Treasury's year-end exchange rate.

There are several types of kupot gemel. A kupat gemel le-hashtala (provident fund for investment) allows withdrawal after a set period. A kupat gemel le-kitzba (provident fund for annuity) pays out as a monthly pension at retirement. Both types are reportable as foreign financial accounts on the FBAR. The distinction matters for Israeli tax treatment but not for FBAR reporting purposes.

### Keren Hishtalmut (Education Fund) on FBAR

A keren hishtalmut (קרן השתלמות) is an employer-matched savings fund. Employers add about 7.5% of salary. Employees add 2.5%, up to a set cap. The fund locks for six years. After that, the money comes out tax-free under Israeli law.

The keren hishtalmut is reportable for FBAR purposes. The Israeli tax exemption has no effect on the FBAR duty. Dual citizens who built up keren hishtalmut balances through years of Israeli employment often never reported them. This account type creates years of potential FBAR violations for many US-Israeli dual citizens.

Self-employed individuals in Israel can also open a keren hishtalmut. The self-employed version has different contribution limits and a shorter lock-in period (three years instead of six). For FBAR reporting, the self-employed keren hishtalmut follows the same rules. Report the maximum account value during the calendar year. The education fund is a foreign financial account regardless of the contributor type.

### Bituach Menahalim (Managers' Insurance)

Bituach menahalim (ביטוח מנהלים) combines pension savings, life insurance, and sometimes disability coverage. Employers and employees both contribute. The policy includes a pension component that builds a cash value over time.

Report the savings and pension component of the bituach menahalim. The life insurance portion with a cash surrender value also requires its own FBAR entry. This matters because FBAR rules under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350) cover life insurance and annuity contracts with cash value.

### Israeli Pension Funds and FBAR Requirements

Israel's pension system includes several types of pension funds. The most common is the keren pensia (קרן פנסיה), a comprehensive pension fund that covers retirement income, disability insurance, and survivor benefits. Major Israeli pension fund managers include Meitav, Menora Mivtachim, Migdal, Harel, and the Histadrut-affiliated funds.

A keren pensia is a foreign financial account for FBAR purposes. You hold a financial interest in a specific pension balance. That financial interest triggers FBAR reporting. The maximum account value is the highest balance during the calendar year, before any deductions for insurance components. Convert the ILS balance to USD using the Treasury rate.

Israeli pension funds send annual statements (דוח שנתי) each year, usually by March or April. The statement shows your total accumulated savings (צבירה), which is the figure you need for FBAR reporting. If the statement shows only the ILS value, you must convert to USD yourself.

### Pensia Tzomachet (Growth Pension)

A pensia tzomachet (פנסיה צומחת) is a set-contribution pension fund — the most common pension type for Israeli workers hired after 2008. Monthly payments from both employer and employee go into a personal account. You hold a financial interest in a specific balance. That makes this pension reportable on the FBAR.

The pensia tzomachet replaced older pension models in Israel. Under the 2008 mandatory pension law, all Israeli employees must have a pension fund. For dual citizens, this means FBAR reporting begins from the first month of Israeli employment. Many US citizens who made aliyah do not realize that their Israeli pension fund creates an immediate FBAR filing obligation.

### Israeli Brokerage and Investment Accounts

Israeli brokerage accounts (חשבון ניירות ערך) held at Bank Leumi, Bank Hapoalim, Israel Discount Bank, or any other Israeli financial institution are foreign financial accounts. If you trade stocks, bonds, mutual funds (קרנות נאמנות), or ETFs through an Israeli brokerage, the account is reportable on the FBAR.

Israeli investment management accounts — where a portfolio manager invests on your behalf — also qualify. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), a securities account maintained with a financial institution is a foreign financial account. The maximum account value is the highest total value during the calendar year, including all securities at market price.

Gemel IRA accounts (individual retirement accounts offered by Israeli investment houses) are also reportable. These function like provident funds and hold a specific balance in which you have a financial interest. Report the peak ILS value, converted to USD.

### Tsvira Tzva'it (Army Service Savings)

Israeli required military service generates savings credits called tsvira tzva'it (צבירה צבאית). These credits build up during service. After service ends, they convert into pension rights or cash payments. If the savings take the form of a financial account with a reportable balance, you must include them on the FBAR.

### Bituach Leumi (National Insurance Institute) — Is It Reportable?

Bituach Leumi (ביטוח לאומי) is the Israeli National Insurance Institute — Israel's social security system. Israeli residents pay monthly contributions, and the system pays retirement pensions, disability benefits, and unemployment insurance.

Bituach Leumi is generally **not** reportable on the FBAR. The payments you receive are government social security benefits, not a balance held in a financial account. You do not have a financial interest in a specific account at a foreign financial institution. FinCEN has not classified government social security programs as foreign financial accounts under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350).

However, if you receive Bituach Leumi payments into an Israeli bank account, that bank account is reportable. The Bituach Leumi benefit itself is not a foreign financial account. The Israeli bank account receiving the benefit is.

### Israeli Mortgage Offset Accounts

Some Israeli mortgage products include an offset account (חשבון קיזוז) linked to the mortgage. The bank offsets the balance in the savings account against the outstanding mortgage to reduce interest charges. The offset account holds a cash balance.

If you hold an Israeli mortgage offset account with a positive cash balance, that balance is a foreign financial account. Include it when calculating your aggregate maximum value for the FBAR $10,000 threshold. Report the maximum balance during the calendar year, converted from ILS to USD.

Not all Israeli mortgages include offset accounts. Standard Israeli mortgages (mashkanta) at Bank Leumi, Bank Hapoalim, or other Israeli banks do not create a separate reportable account. The mortgage liability itself is not a foreign financial account. Only an associated savings or offset account with a positive balance triggers FBAR reporting.

## US-Israel Tax Treaty and FBAR Obligations

The Israel-US Tax Treaty does not exempt you from FBAR reporting. The treaty covers income tax — it prevents double taxation on income earned in Israel and the US. The FBAR, however, is a Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) rule under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314), not an income tax rule. BSA duties apply on their own. They are apart from all income tax treaties.

Dual US-Israeli citizens regularly confuse the two. The treaty reduces income tax on Israeli pension distributions in some cases. However, it has no effect on your duty to report the pension account on the FBAR each year. A non-willful failure to file results in a penalty of up to $16,117 per violation under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). That penalty applies regardless of any treaty protection.

### What the Israel-US Tax Treaty Actually Covers

The [US-Israel Income Tax Treaty](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/israel-tax-treaty-documents), signed in 1994, covers:

- Taxation of business income, dividends, and royalties
- Treatment of Israeli pension distributions for US tax purposes
- Relief from double taxation through credits and offsets
- Exchange of data between the IRS and the ITA

None of these rules override the BSA duty to file FinCEN Form 114 for foreign financial accounts.

The treaty includes a pension article that may allow deferral of US income tax on certain Israeli pension contributions. For example, under certain conditions, contributions to a keren pensia or keren hishtalmut may be treated similarly to US retirement plan contributions for income tax purposes. This is an income tax benefit. It does not reduce or eliminate the FBAR filing requirement for those same accounts.

### FATCA and the Israel Model 1 IGA

Israel signed a Model 1 FATCA treaty agreement (IGA) in 2014. Under the IGA, Israeli financial institutions — including banks, insurance firms, and pension managers — report US-person account data to the ITA. The ITA then sends that data to the IRS.

Israeli banks, pension funds, and insurance firms therefore already share your account details with the US government each year. If you do not file the FBAR but hold Israeli accounts above the $10,000 threshold, the IRS can match FATCA data against FinCEN records to find non-filers.

Bank Leumi, Bank Hapoalim, Israel Discount Bank, and Mizrahi-Tefahot all comply with FATCA. They request a W-9 form and a self-certification of US person status when you open an account. If you provided US citizenship information to your Israeli bank, the bank reports your account data to the ITA, which forwards it to the Internal Revenue Service.

## How to Calculate Maximum Account Value for Israeli Accounts

The FBAR requires you to report the maximum account value of each foreign financial account during the calendar year. For Israeli bank accounts and pension funds denominated in New Israeli Shekel (ILS), this means finding the highest balance and converting it to USD.

The maximum account value is not the year-end balance. It is the highest balance at any point during the calendar year. For a checking account, this could be the day after a large deposit. For a pension fund, it is typically the last statement of the year if the fund grew steadily. Review all monthly or quarterly statements to find the peak.

### ILS to USD Currency Conversion for FBAR

To convert ILS to USD for the FBAR, use the US Treasury's year-end rate. This is the December 31 rate from the [Treasury Bureau of the Fiscal Service](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/reports-statements/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/current.html). Apply that rate to the peak NIS balance of each account during the year. Do not use spot rates, annual averages, or bank rates.

The December 31, 2024 Treasury rate for the NIS was about $0.27 per NIS (about 3.67 NIS per dollar). You must check the current year's rate on the Treasury's [exchange rates page](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/reports-statements/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/current.html) before filing your FBAR.

The currency conversion applies to every Israeli account denominated in New Israeli Shekel. If you hold a USD-denominated account at an Israeli bank, report the maximum USD value directly — no conversion is needed. For accounts in EUR or GBP at Israeli banks, use the Treasury's year-end exchange rate for that currency.

### Step-by-Step NIS Conversion

1. Find the maximum NIS balance of each Israeli account during the calendar year.
2. Look up the Treasury's December 31 exchange rate for the NIS.
3. Multiply each maximum NIS balance by the Treasury rate.
4. Sum all converted USD values to get the aggregate maximum.
5. If the aggregate exceeds $10,000, file the FBAR for all foreign accounts.

For more detail, see our guide on [how to calculate maximum account value for your FBAR](/blog/how-to-calculate-maximum-account-value-fbar).

### Finding the Peak Balance on Israeli Bank Statements

Israeli banks provide monthly statements (דפי חשבון) through online banking portals. Bank Leumi uses the Leumi Digital platform. Bank Hapoalim uses the Poalim B'Online system. Israel Discount Bank provides statements through Discount Online.

To find the maximum account value, download all 12 monthly statements for the calendar year. Look for the highest closing balance on any statement. For accounts with large deposits followed by withdrawals, the peak may occur mid-month. Daily transaction records in the online portal show intra-month peaks.

For pension funds and kupot gemel, the fund manager sends an annual statement showing the account value at year-end and sometimes quarterly values. If quarterly values are available, use the highest one. If only the year-end value is available, use that as your best estimate of the maximum account value.

## Getting Account Statements From Israeli Banks

Gathering account statements from Israeli banks is one of the harder parts of FBAR filing for dual citizens. Israeli bank websites are primarily in Hebrew, and some features require Israeli phone numbers for two-factor authentication.

### Bank Leumi Account Statements

Bank Leumi offers online access through Leumi Digital (digital.leumi.co.il). Log in with your Israeli ID and password. Navigate to "דפי חשבון" (account statements) to download monthly statements as PDF files. You can request statements for any date range.

If you no longer have online access, contact Bank Leumi's international department directly. Bank Leumi has an English-speaking international banking division that handles requests from US-based customers. You can also visit a branch in person during a trip to Israel. Request statements showing all accounts and their maximum balances during the calendar year.

### Bank Hapoalim Account Statements

Bank Hapoalim provides statements through the Poalim B'Online portal (online.bankhapoalim.co.il). The statements page shows monthly account summaries. Download each month's statement to identify the peak balance.

Bank Hapoalim also has a US presence through Bank Hapoalim B.M. branches. If you cannot access the Israeli portal, contact the US branches for assistance in obtaining your Israeli account records.

### Israel Discount Bank and Mizrahi-Tefahot Statements

Israel Discount Bank offers the Discount Online portal. Mizrahi-Tefahot provides online banking at mizrahi-tefahot.co.il. Both banks supply monthly statements in PDF format through their online systems.

For all Israeli banks, you may need to authorize someone in Israel to obtain statements on your behalf. Israeli banks accept a power of attorney (ייפוי כוח) that allows a designated person to request account information. This is useful for US residents who cannot travel to Israel.

## Common FBAR Mistakes US-Israeli Dual Citizens Make

Dual US-Israeli citizens make several recurring FBAR mistakes. Understanding these mistakes helps you avoid them and file correctly.

**Mistake 1: Not knowing about the FBAR at all.** Many US citizens who made aliyah or who were born to American parents in Israel do not know that the FBAR exists. The FBAR filing requirement applies to every US person, regardless of where they live. The Internal Revenue Service and the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network do not send filing reminders. You must file on your own.

**Mistake 2: Reporting only bank accounts and ignoring pension funds.** Israeli pension accounts — keren pensia, kupat gemel, keren hishtalmut, and bituach menahalim — are all foreign financial accounts. Dual citizens who file the FBAR sometimes list only their Israeli bank accounts and leave off their pension and provident fund balances. This results in an incomplete filing.

**Mistake 3: Using the wrong exchange rate for currency conversion.** Some filers use the spot rate from xe.com, the Bank of Israel rate, or an annual average. The FBAR requires the US Treasury's year-end exchange rate. Using the wrong rate leads to incorrect account values and potential issues during an audit.

**Mistake 4: Reporting the year-end balance instead of the maximum account value.** The FBAR asks for the maximum value during the calendar year, not the December 31 balance. For accounts with large inflows and outflows, the maximum can be much higher than the year-end balance. Always review all monthly statements to identify the peak.

**Mistake 5: Failing to aggregate across all foreign accounts.** The $10,000 threshold applies to the aggregate value of all foreign financial accounts, not each account individually. A dual citizen with a Bank Leumi account worth $4,000 and a keren hishtalmut worth $7,000 exceeds the threshold. That filer must report both accounts on the FBAR — not just the one above $10,000.

**Mistake 6: Assuming the Israel-US Tax Treaty exempts FBAR filing.** As explained above, the tax treaty covers income tax. It does not exempt you from FBAR reporting under the Bank Secrecy Act. This is one of the most common misunderstandings among US-Israeli dual citizens.

**Mistake 7: Missing the FBAR deadline.** The FBAR is due April 15 with an automatic extension to October 15. It is a separate filing from your income tax return. Many dual citizens file their US tax return but forget the FBAR. Late filing can trigger FBAR penalties even if no tax is owed.

## Real-World Scenarios for US-Israeli Dual Citizens

These scenarios show how FBAR reporting works for US-Israeli dual citizens. Each uses real NIS amounts and the rough Treasury rate of $0.27 per NIS. Always use the actual year-end Treasury rate when you file your FBAR Form online.

### Scenario 1: Dual Citizen Working in Israel

Yael is a dual US-Israeli citizen working as a software engineer in Tel Aviv. She holds these accounts, with peak balances during 2025:

- Bank Hapoalim checking account: ₪45,000 ($12,150)
- Keren hishtalmut (education fund): ₪280,000 ($75,600)
- Pensia tzomachet (growth pension): ₪520,000 ($140,400)
- Bituach menahalim (managers' insurance, savings component): ₪85,000 ($22,950)

Aggregate maximum value: **$251,100**. Yael must file an FBAR for 2025 under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), reporting all four accounts. She should also check [Form 8938](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938) thresholds. Expats filing separately face a $200,000 threshold on the last day of the year or $300,000 at any time, under [26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D).

Yael's keren hishtalmut is locked for three more years under Israeli law. The lock-in period does not change her FBAR obligation. She must report the account each year she holds it.

### Scenario 2: US Resident With Israeli Pension Accounts

David is a US citizen who lived in Israel for twelve years. He moved back to the United States in 2018 and left his Israeli accounts open. During 2025, his peak balances were:

- Bank Leumi savings account: ₪22,000 ($5,940)
- Kupat gemel (provident fund): ₪410,000 ($110,700)
- Pensia tzomachet: ₪760,000 ($205,200)

Aggregate maximum value: **$321,840**. David must file an FBAR for 2025 under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350). Even though he lives in the US, his Israeli accounts remain foreign financial accounts. His aggregate far exceeds the $10,000 threshold under [31 CFR 1010.306(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306).

David has not filed FBARs since leaving Israel in 2018. This creates seven years of potential violations. If his non-filing was non-willful, the IRS Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures allow him to file six years of past-due FBARs with a 5% standard offshore penalty. This is far less than the standard $16,117 per-violation penalty under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). See the [IRS Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures) page for full details.

### Scenario 3: US Citizen With Israeli Army Service Savings

Amir is a 28-year-old dual citizen who served in the Israeli army (IDF) for three years. He built up tsvira tzva'it (army service savings) during his service. He also opened a Bank Discount savings account after his release. During 2025, his peak balances were:

- Bank Discount savings account: ₪18,000 ($4,860)
- Tsvira tzva'it savings account: ₪62,000 ($16,740)

Aggregate maximum value: **$21,600**. Amir must file an FBAR. His tsvira tzva'it came from military service, but it is a financial account under Israeli banking law. Therefore, it is also a foreign financial account under the BSA. Amir is a first-time FBAR filer. He should review our [FBAR first-time filer guide](/blog/fbar-first-time-filer-guide) for step-by-step instructions.

### Scenario 4: US Citizen With Israeli Brokerage Account

Rachel is a US citizen and Israeli resident who holds a brokerage account at Bank Leumi. She trades Israeli stocks and mutual funds. During 2025, her peak account values were:

- Bank Leumi brokerage account: ₪320,000 ($86,400)
- Bank Leumi shekel checking account: ₪15,000 ($4,050)
- Keren pensia (pension fund): ₪180,000 ($48,600)

Aggregate maximum value: **$139,050**. Rachel must file an FBAR for all three accounts. The brokerage account is a foreign financial account because it is a securities account maintained at a foreign financial institution. Rachel must report the maximum portfolio value during the calendar year, including unrealized gains on her Israeli stock positions.

## Penalties for Not Reporting Israeli Accounts

Penalties for failing to report Israeli accounts on the FBAR can be high. The IRS and FinCEN enforce the Bank Secrecy Act under [31 USC 5321](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). That law sets two tiers of fines based on whether the failure was willful.

A non-willful violation carries a penalty of up to **$16,117 per violation** (2026 inflation-adjusted amount) under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The Supreme Court held in *United States v. Bittner* (2023) that non-willful penalties apply per FBAR report, not per account. A single year of missed filing is one violation, regardless of the number of Israeli accounts you held.

A willful violation carries the greater of **$129,210** or **50% of the account balance** per violation under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). Criminal penalties under [31 USC 5322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5322) can reach $250,000 and five years in prison.

Non-willful FBAR penalties are the most common for dual citizens who simply did not know about the reporting requirement. A US citizen living in Israel who failed to file FBARs for five years faces a maximum non-willful penalty of $16,117 per year — up to $80,585 total. Willful penalties for the same period could reach into the hundreds of thousands of dollars depending on the account balances.

For a full breakdown of penalty exposure, see our article on [FBAR penalties and what happens if you do not file](/blog/fbar-penalties-what-happens-if-you-dont-file).

### Compliance Options for Late Filers

| Program | Who Qualifies | Penalty Relief |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| Streamlined Foreign Offshore | Non-willful, living outside US | 0% standard offshore penalty |
| Streamlined Domestic Offshore | Non-willful, living in US | 5% standard offshore penalty |
| Delinquent FBAR Procedures | No unreported income | No penalty if IRS not yet contacted |
| Voluntary Disclosure | Willful or near-willful | Reduced criminal exposure |

Many US-Israeli dual citizens living in Israel qualify for the Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures. Under this program, you file three years of delinquent tax returns and six years of delinquent FBARs. The miscellaneous offshore penalty is zero. This is the most favorable option for non-willful dual citizens living in Israel.

Dual citizens who moved back to the US qualify for the Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures instead. The penalty is 5% of the highest aggregate foreign account balance over the six-year look-back period.

## FBAR vs. Form 8938: Two Separate Filing Requirements for Israeli Accounts

US persons with Israeli accounts often need to file both the FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) and Form 8938. These are two separate forms under two different laws. The FBAR goes to FinCEN under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314). Form 8938 goes to the IRS with your federal tax return under [26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D). Filing one does not satisfy the other. Each form has its own penalties.

Dual US-Israeli citizens with large pension balances often exceed both thresholds. The FBAR threshold is $10,000 aggregate. The Form 8938 threshold is $50,000 on the last day of the year or $75,000 at any time during the year for US residents. Expats face higher thresholds. The comparison below shows the key differences.

| | FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) | Form 8938 |
|---|---|---|
| **Filed with** | FinCEN via BSA E-Filing | IRS with tax return |
| **Threshold (US resident, single)** | $10,000 aggregate | $50,000 on last day or $75,000 any time |
| **Threshold (expat, filing jointly)** | $10,000 aggregate | $400,000 on last day or $600,000 any time |
| **Pension accounts** | Reportable (most types) | Reportable (most types) |
| **Non-filing penalty** | Up to $16,117 (non-willful) | Up to $10,000 |

For a full look at these two forms, see our article on [FBAR vs. Form 8938 differences](/blog/fbar-vs-fatca-form-8938-differences).

## Frequently Asked Questions About FBAR Israel Bank Accounts

The questions below cover the most common FBAR Israel bank accounts issues for US persons. They address Israeli pension accounts, the Israel-US Tax Treaty, NIS-to-USD conversion, and options for late filers. If your Israeli bank accounts had a combined peak value above $10,000 at any point during the year, you need to file FinCEN Form 114.

**Do I need to report my Israeli kupat gemel on the FBAR?**

Yes. A kupat gemel (provident fund) is a foreign financial account under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350). You have a financial interest in a specific account balance. Therefore, you must report it. Report the maximum NIS balance during the year, converted to USD using the Treasury's year-end exchange rate. The Israeli tax exemption on the fund has no effect on your US FBAR obligation.

**Does the Israel-US Tax Treaty exempt my Israeli pension from FBAR reporting?**

No. The Israel-US Tax Treaty covers income tax, not BSA reporting. The FBAR is a Bank Secrecy Act requirement under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314). It applies independently of all income tax treaties. Your Israeli pension accounts must appear on the FBAR every year the aggregate value of all your foreign accounts exceeds $10,000. For more on foreign pension reporting, see our guide on [FBAR foreign pension and retirement accounts](/blog/fbar-foreign-pension-retirement-accounts).

**Is my keren hishtalmut reportable even though I cannot withdraw from it yet?**

Yes. The FBAR filing duty does not depend on whether you can access funds. Under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), you must report any foreign financial account in which you have a financial interest. A keren hishtalmut during its lock-in period still holds your money in an account. That financial interest triggers the reporting duty. Lock-in periods and withdrawal limits have no effect on FBAR filing.

**Which exchange rate do I use to convert NIS to USD?**

Use the US Treasury's year-end exchange rate — the rate published by the Bureau of the Fiscal Service as of December 31 of the tax year. Find it on the [Treasury reporting rates page](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/reports-statements/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/current.html). Do not use a bank rate, a market rate from the date of the peak balance, or an annual average. For more on conversion methods, see our guide on [FBAR exchange rates and the Treasury Department](/blog/fbar-exchange-rates-treasury-department).

**What happens if I never filed FBARs for my Israeli accounts?**

The right path depends on whether your non-filing was willful or non-willful. Dual citizens who did not know about the FBAR qualify as non-willful filers in most cases. Non-willful filers living outside the US can use the Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures to file six years of past FBARs with no miscellaneous penalty. Those in the US use the Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures with a 5% penalty under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). Act before the IRS contacts you — penalties rise sharply once an audit begins.

**Does my bituach menahalim life insurance policy need to go on the FBAR?**

Yes, for the savings and pension component. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), life insurance and annuity contracts with a cash value are foreign financial accounts. Bituach menahalim policies build cash value through the pension and savings components. Report the cash surrender value or the total savings balance as the maximum account value. For more on life insurance policy reporting, see our article on [FBAR foreign life insurance policies](/blog/fbar-foreign-life-insurance-policies).

**Is my Bituach Leumi (National Insurance) reportable on the FBAR?**

No. Bituach Leumi is a government social security program, not a financial account at a foreign financial institution. You do not have a financial interest in a specific account balance. However, if your Bituach Leumi payments are deposited into an Israeli bank account, that bank account is reportable. The benefit itself is not a foreign financial account. The bank account receiving it is.

**Do I need to report Israeli accounts that I have signature authority over but do not own?**

Yes. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), a US person must report any foreign financial account over which they have signature authority, even if they do not have a financial interest in the account. If you are an authorized signer on a family member's Israeli bank account, a business account at an Israeli bank, or a corporate account, you must include it on your FBAR. Report the maximum account value of the account during the calendar year. For more on signature authority, see our guide on [FBAR for green card holders](/blog/fbar-green-card-holders).
