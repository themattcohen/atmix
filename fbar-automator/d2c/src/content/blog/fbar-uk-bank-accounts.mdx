# FBAR and UK Bank Accounts: What US Persons Must Report

Tens of thousands of Americans hold bank accounts in the United Kingdom. Some are expats working in London or Manchester. Others are dual US-UK citizens who grew up with Barclays or Lloyds accounts. Many inherited ISAs or building society accounts from British relatives.

All of these accounts carry FBAR filing requirements. The FBAR — formally the Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FinCEN Form 114) — is filed with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network. Under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), any United States person with a financial interest in or signature authority over foreign financial accounts must file. The threshold is $10,000 in aggregate value at any time during the tax year.

UK accounts present a specific risk that accounts in many other countries do not. The United States and the United Kingdom signed a FATCA Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) in 2014. Under this agreement, HMRC collects financial data from UK banks and sends that data to the IRS every year. If you skip the FBAR, the IRS may already have records showing the accounts you failed to report.

## Which UK Accounts Must You Report on the FBAR?

The FBAR casts a wide net. The definition of "financial account" under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350) covers bank accounts, securities accounts, and other financial accounts maintained with a foreign financial institution.

Here is a breakdown of the most common UK account types and their FBAR status.

### Current Accounts and Savings Accounts

Every UK current account (checking account) and savings account at a UK bank is reportable. This includes accounts at:

- Barclays
- HSBC UK
- Lloyds Banking Group
- NatWest
- Santander UK
- Metro Bank
- Monzo, Starling, and other digital banks

The type of bank does not matter. High-street banks, challenger banks, and online-only banks all qualify as foreign financial institutions.

### Individual Savings Accounts (ISAs)

ISAs receive tax-free treatment under UK law. The ISA wrapper has no effect on US reporting obligations. All ISA types are reportable on the FBAR:

- **Cash ISAs**: Report the maximum cash balance during the year.
- **Stocks and Shares ISAs**: Report the peak portfolio value, including all holdings.
- **Lifetime ISAs (LISAs)**: Report the full account value, including government bonuses.
- **Innovative Finance ISAs**: Report the total value of peer-to-peer lending holdings.

The 2025-26 ISA allowance is £20,000. A Cash ISA holding £18,000 converts to about $22,680 at a 1.26 GBP/USD rate. That single account exceeds the $10,000 FBAR threshold on its own.

Many US persons in the UK hold both a Cash ISA and a Stocks and Shares ISA. You must report each one separately on the FBAR, listing the institution, account number, and maximum value for each.

### Building Society Accounts

UK building societies — Nationwide, Yorkshire, Coventry, Skipton, and others — are financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act. Any savings account, fixed-rate bond, or regular saver held at a building society is a foreign financial account.

Building society accounts often hold £50,000 or more ($63,000+ USD). These balances alone exceed the $10,000 aggregate threshold by a wide margin.

### NS&I (National Savings & Investments)

HM Treasury backs NS&I. Despite being a government entity, NS&I accounts are foreign financial accounts for FBAR purposes. Reportable NS&I products include:

- **Premium Bonds**: Report the face value of your bond holdings, not the prize value. If you hold £30,000 in Premium Bonds, report $37,800 (at a 1.26 rate). Prizes are separate income — the FBAR tracks the account value.
- **Direct Saver**: Report the maximum balance during the year.
- **Income Bonds**: Report the maximum balance.
- **Green Savings Bonds**: Report the face value of the holding.

NS&I Premium Bonds are one of the most commonly overlooked FBAR-reportable assets. Many US-UK dual citizens hold bonds purchased years ago and do not realize they trigger a filing obligation.

### UK Pension Accounts

UK pension accounts present one of the more complex areas of FBAR reporting. The IRS has not issued definitive guidance on every UK pension type. Here is the general treatment.

**Self-Invested Personal Pensions (SIPPs)**: A SIPP gives you control over investment choices. You select the funds, stocks, or bonds within the pension wrapper. Because you direct the assets, a SIPP is reportable on the FBAR. Report the total value of the SIPP, including all underlying investments.

**Workplace Pensions (Defined Contribution)**: If your employer contributes to a defined contribution pension and you have the ability to choose or change your investments, the account is reportable. Report the full account value.

**Defined Benefit Pensions (Final Salary)**: A defined benefit pension pays a set amount at retirement based on salary and years of service. You do not hold an account with a balance you can direct. The IRS position on whether defined benefit pensions are reportable is unclear. Many practitioners exclude them from the FBAR because there is no "account" in the traditional sense.

Consult a qualified tax professional for your specific situation.

**State Pension**: The UK State Pension is a government benefit, not a financial account. You do not report the UK State Pension on the FBAR.

### UK Brokerage and Investment Accounts

Any account at a UK broker or investment platform where you hold securities is reportable. Common UK platforms include:

- Hargreaves Lansdown
- AJ Bell
- Interactive Investor
- Vanguard UK
- Freetrade

Report the peak portfolio value during the year. This includes stocks, bonds, funds, ETFs, and any cash balance held within the brokerage account. If you hold both a general investment account and an ISA at the same platform, report each account separately.

## Converting GBP to USD: The Treasury Exchange Rate

The FBAR uses the US Treasury Department's end-of-year exchange rate for currency conversion. You do not use the rate on the day of the maximum value. You do not use an annual average rate.

For the 2025 FBAR (filed in 2026), you will use the Treasury's December 31, 2025 rate for converting GBP to USD. The Treasury publishes these rates on its [Exchange Rates page](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/treasury-reporting-rates-of-exchange).

### How the Conversion Works

1. Find the maximum value of each UK account in GBP during 2025.
2. Look up the Treasury's December 31, 2025 GBP-to-USD rate.
3. Multiply each GBP maximum value by that rate.
4. Sum all converted values to determine the aggregate maximum.

For context, the December 31, 2024 Treasury rate was about 1 GBP = 1.2535 USD. Rates fluctuate yearly. Always verify the current year's rate on the Treasury website.

### Conversion Example

Sarah holds three UK accounts:

- Lloyds current account: peak balance of £8,500
- Nationwide ISA: peak balance of £25,000
- NS&I Premium Bonds: £10,000 face value

Using a Treasury rate of 1 GBP = 1.26 USD:

- Lloyds: £8,500 × 1.26 = $10,710
- Nationwide ISA: £25,000 × 1.26 = $31,500
- NS&I Premium Bonds: £10,000 × 1.26 = $12,600

Aggregate maximum value: **$54,810**. Sarah must file an FBAR reporting all three accounts. For more detail on calculating maximum values, see our guide on [how to calculate maximum account value for your FBAR](/blog/how-to-calculate-maximum-account-value-fbar).

## The US-UK FATCA IGA: Why the IRS Already Knows About Your Accounts

The United States and the United Kingdom signed a [Model 1 FATCA Intergovernmental Agreement](https://www.treasury.gov/resource-center/tax-policy/treaties/Pages/FATCA-Archive.aspx) on September 12, 2012, with an updated version signed in 2014. Under this agreement, UK financial institutions report account data on US persons to HMRC. HMRC then transmits that data to the IRS through an automatic exchange of information process.

### What UK Banks Report to HMRC

UK financial institutions covered by the IGA must report the following for each account held by a US person:

- Account holder name, address, and US taxpayer identification number (SSN or ITIN)
- Account number
- Account balance or value at year-end
- Interest, dividends, and other income paid to the account
- Gross proceeds from sales of financial assets

This means HMRC sends the IRS a record of your UK bank accounts, your balances, and your income — every year. The data flows automatically. You do not need to trigger an audit for the IRS to receive this information.

### What This Means for FBAR Non-Filers

If you hold UK accounts and do not file the FBAR, the IRS has two sources of information pointing to a violation:

1. **FATCA data from HMRC** showing the accounts exist and their balances.
2. **The absence of your FBAR** in FinCEN's BSA E-Filing system.

Matching these two datasets is straightforward for the IRS. The penalty for a non-willful failure to file is up to **$16,117 per violation** under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)(i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/5321) (2026 inflation-adjusted amount). The Supreme Court ruled in *United States v. Bittner* (2023) that non-willful penalties apply per report, not per account. A single missed year is one violation.

Willful violations carry far harsher penalties. The maximum is the greater of **$129,210** or **50% of the account balance** at the time of the violation under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/5321). The Department of Justice has prosecuted willful FBAR violations as criminal offenses. Criminal penalties reach **$250,000** and five years imprisonment under [31 USC 5322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5322).

The FATCA IGA removes any reasonable expectation of privacy for UK accounts. Filing your FBAR is not optional when the IRS already has the data to verify compliance.

## Common Scenarios for US Persons With UK Accounts

### Scenario 1: American Expat Working in London

James is a US citizen who moved to London in 2022 for work. He opened a Barclays current account, a Vanguard UK Stocks and Shares ISA, and enrolled in his employer's workplace pension. His account values in 2025:

- Barclays current account: peak £12,000 ($15,120)
- Vanguard UK ISA: peak £45,000 ($56,700)
- Workplace pension (defined contribution): balance £28,000 ($35,280)

Aggregate maximum: **$107,100**. James must file an FBAR for 2025 under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), reporting all three accounts. He should also consider [Form 8938 (FATCA)](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938), which has a higher threshold for expats but may still apply.

James also keeps a Chase checking account in the US. Domestic accounts do not appear on the FBAR. He reports only the UK accounts.

### Scenario 2: Dual US-UK Citizen

Emily was born in the United States but has lived in the UK since childhood. She holds British and American citizenship. She has a Lloyds savings account, NS&I Premium Bonds, and a Cash ISA at Nationwide — all opened when she was a teenager.

- Lloyds savings: peak £6,200 ($7,812)
- NS&I Premium Bonds: £15,000 face value ($18,900)
- Nationwide Cash ISA: peak £9,500 ($11,970)

Aggregate maximum: **$38,682**. Emily must file an FBAR. Many dual citizens in Emily's position have never filed because they did not know about the obligation.

If Emily has missed prior years, she should consider the [Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures). A qualified tax professional can advise on the best path to compliance.

For a detailed overview of penalty exposure from missed filings, see our article on [FBAR penalties and what happens if you do not file](/blog/fbar-penalties-what-happens-if-you-dont-file).

### Scenario 3: US Person Who Inherited UK Accounts

David is a US citizen living in Ohio. His British grandmother passed away in 2024 and left him £40,000 in a Skipton Building Society account and £20,000 in NS&I Income Bonds.

- Skipton Building Society: peak £40,000 ($50,400)
- NS&I Income Bonds: peak £20,000 ($25,200)

Aggregate maximum: **$75,600**. David must file an FBAR for 2025 under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350) (the first full calendar year he holds the accounts). The FBAR obligation began the moment David had a financial interest in the accounts.

Inherited accounts catch many people off guard. The inheritance itself may not be taxable in the US. However, gifts or bequests over $100,000 from a foreign person may require a Form 3520 filing. The ongoing FBAR obligation is separate and applies every year the accounts remain open.

## How to Report Specific UK Account Types

### Reporting ISAs

The FBAR asks for the financial institution, account number, account type, and maximum value. For an ISA:

- **Financial Institution**: The ISA provider (e.g., "Nationwide Building Society" or "Hargreaves Lansdown")
- **Account Number**: Your ISA account number or client reference
- **Account Type**: Select "Other" and describe as "Individual Savings Account (ISA)" or select "Bank" for a Cash ISA
- **Maximum Value**: The peak value of the ISA during the year, converted to USD

Report the ISA wrapper account. You do not list individual stocks or funds held within a Stocks and Shares ISA. The FBAR tracks the account, not the underlying assets.

### Reporting NS&I Premium Bonds

Premium Bonds do not have a traditional "balance" that fluctuates. You buy bonds at face value and hold them. Report the face value of your Premium Bond holding as the maximum account value.

- **Financial Institution**: "National Savings & Investments (NS&I)"
- **Account Number**: Your NS&I holder's number
- **Maximum Value**: Total face value of Premium Bonds held, converted to USD

If you bought £30,000 in Premium Bonds and held them all year, the maximum value is £30,000 (about $37,800 at a 1.26 rate). Prize winnings do not increase the reported FBAR value — they are separate income events.

### Reporting Building Society Accounts

Report building society accounts the same way you report bank accounts.

- **Financial Institution**: Name of the building society (e.g., "Nationwide Building Society")
- **Account Number**: Your account or roll number
- **Account Type**: Select "Bank" for savings accounts, "Other" for fixed-rate bonds
- **Maximum Value**: Peak balance during the year, converted to USD

### Reporting UK Pensions (SIPPs)

For a SIPP or other reportable pension:

- **Financial Institution**: The pension provider (e.g., "AJ Bell" or "Fidelity International")
- **Account Number**: Your SIPP account reference
- **Account Type**: Select "Other" and describe as "Self-Invested Personal Pension (SIPP)"
- **Maximum Value**: The peak total value of the pension during the year, converted to USD

## FBAR Deadlines and Extensions

The FBAR for calendar year 2025 is due on **April 15, 2026**. If you miss April 15, an automatic extension pushes the deadline to **October 15, 2026** under [31 CFR 1010.306(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306). You do not need to request this extension — the extension applies to all filers by default.

FinCEN does not charge a late filing fee between April 15 and October 15 because the extension is automatic. After October 15, penalties may apply.

## The FBAR and Form 8938: Two Separate Filings

US persons with UK accounts often need to file both the FBAR and [Form 8938](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938) (Statement of Specified Foreign Financial Assets). These are two different filings with different thresholds, different recipients, and different penalties.

| | FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) | Form 8938 |
|---|---|---|
| **Filed with** | FinCEN (via BSA E-Filing) | IRS (attached to tax return) |
| **Threshold (domestic filers)** | $10,000 aggregate | $50,000 on last day or $75,000 at any time |
| **Threshold (expats, filing jointly)** | $10,000 aggregate | $400,000 on last day or $600,000 at any time |
| **Penalties** | Up to $16,117 non-willful; up to 50% willful | Up to $10,000 for failure to file |

Many expats with UK accounts exceed both thresholds. Filing one does not satisfy the other. You must file both reports separately.

## First-Time Filers and Late Filers

If you have UK accounts and have never filed an FBAR, you are not alone. The IRS offers several paths to come into compliance:

- **Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures**: For non-willful non-filers, this program lets you file the last three years of tax returns and six years of FBARs with reduced or zero penalties. See [IRS Streamlined Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures).
- **Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures**: If you have no unreported income, you may file past-due FBARs without penalties. See [IRS Delinquent FBAR Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/delinquent-fbar-submission-procedures).
- **Voluntary Disclosure Practice**: For willful violations, this is the formal path. Penalties will apply, but criminal prosecution becomes far less likely.

If you are a first-time filer getting started, our [FBAR first-time filer guide](/blog/fbar-first-time-filer-guide) walks through the entire process step by step.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I report a UK account I closed partway through the year?**

Yes. If you held the account at any point during the calendar year and your aggregate foreign account values exceeded $10,000, you must report the closed account. Use the maximum value the account reached before you closed it.

**My UK bank account holds less than $10,000. Do I still file?**

That depends on the aggregate. Sum the maximum values of all your foreign accounts worldwide — not only UK accounts. Suppose you have a €5,000 account in France and a £4,000 account in the UK. The combined value may push you over the $10,000 threshold.

See our guide on [how to calculate maximum account value](/blog/how-to-calculate-maximum-account-value-fbar) for a detailed walkthrough.

**Are ISA gains taxable in the United States?**

ISAs are tax-free in the UK. The US does not recognize the ISA tax wrapper. Interest, dividends, and capital gains within an ISA are taxable on your US income tax return. This is a US income tax question separate from the FBAR, but it often surprises US persons holding UK ISAs.

**Does the US-UK tax treaty protect me from FBAR penalties?**

No. The [US-UK Income Tax Treaty](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-kingdom-tax-treaty-documents) covers income tax. The FBAR is a Bank Secrecy Act obligation under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314), not an income tax provision.

The treaty does not override BSA reporting rules. FBAR penalties apply regardless of any treaty position.
