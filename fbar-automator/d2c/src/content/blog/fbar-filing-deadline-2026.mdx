The **FBAR filing deadline 2026** is **April 15, 2026**. If your foreign financial accounts totaled more than $10,000 at any point during 2025, you must file FinCEN Form 114 — the Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts — by that date. You also get an automatic extension to October 15, 2026. No form required to claim it.

This guide covers every key date, the penalty structure if you miss it, and your options if you have prior years to catch up on.

## What Is the FBAR Filing Deadline 2026?

The 2026 FBAR deadline is **April 15, 2026** for the 2025 calendar year. The FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) goes to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network ([FinCEN](https://bsaefiling.fincen.gov/)). It is separate from your federal income tax return. It does not go to the IRS. Additionally, the penalties for missing it are separate from any tax return penalties.

## Does the FBAR Deadline Match the Tax Return Deadline?

Yes, both deadlines fall on April 15 — but the filings are independent. Filing a tax extension (Form 4868) does not extend your FBAR. The FBAR has its own automatic extension to October 15, 2026. That extension applies whether or not you filed Form 4868. Think of them as two separate timers that happen to start at the same date.

## How Does the Automatic Extension to October 15, 2026 Work?

If you miss April 15, you automatically receive an extension to **October 15, 2026**. No request. No form. No action required. The extension exists under [31 CFR 1010.306(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306). Congress added it through the Surface Transportation and Veterans Health Care Choice Improvement Act of 2015. Therefore, if you file your FBAR by October 15, 2026, it counts as timely filed with no penalty.

### This Is NOT the Same as a Tax Return Extension

Filing Form 4868 extends your income tax return. It does not extend your FBAR. The FBAR extension is automatic and separate. In 2026, October 15 is a Thursday. No deadline adjustment is needed for weekends or holidays. If you file both your 1040 and FBAR by October 15, both are timely filed.

## What Are the Key FBAR Filing Dates for 2026?

For the 2025 reporting year, the FBAR filing deadline 2026 starts on April 15. You get an automatic extension to October 15 with no action required. The reporting period covers January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2025. Treasury exchange rates for December 31, 2025 are typically published in mid-January 2026. Use those rates to convert foreign account balances into US dollars.

Here is your complete timeline for 2025 calendar year FBAR reporting:

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| **January 1, 2025** | Start of the 2025 reporting period |
| **December 31, 2025** | End of the 2025 reporting period |
| **January 1, 2026** | Earliest date to file your 2025 FBAR |
| **Mid-January 2026** | Treasury publishes December 31, 2025 exchange rates |
| **April 15, 2026** | FBAR filing deadline |
| **October 15, 2026** | Automatic extended deadline — final date to file without penalty |

The FBAR covers accounts you held at any point during the calendar year. Even a brief ownership during 2025 triggers the requirement if your total exceeded $10,000. Additionally, joint account holders must each file separately. See our guide on [joint account reporting rules](/blog/fbar-joint-account-reporting-rules) for details.

## What Happens If You Miss the FBAR Deadline?

Missing both the April 15 and October 15 deadlines triggers potential penalties under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The severity depends on whether the IRS considers the failure "willful" or "non-willful." Non-willful penalties reach up to $12,909 per violation. Willful penalties can reach 50% of your account balance per year. Criminal charges are also possible in serious cases.

### Non-Willful Penalties

Non-willful violations cover cases where you forgot, didn't know, or had a reasonable explanation. The penalty is up to **$12,909 per violation** (the 2026 inflation-adjusted figure under 31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)(i)).

Each account in each year can count as a separate violation. Three unreported accounts for 2025 means up to **$38,727** in potential penalties. However, the IRS has discretion. First-time violators with smaller balances often see reduced assessments. The [IRS Internal Revenue Manual (IRM 4.26.16)](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-026-016) outlines how agents weigh account size, compliance history, and cooperation when setting penalty amounts.

### Willful Penalties

Willful violations involve knowing about the requirement and choosing not to file. The penalty is the greater of:

- **$100,000**, or
- **50% of the account balance** at the time of the violation

For a $300,000 account, that means a **$150,000 willful penalty — per year, per account**. Over multiple years, this can exceed the total account value. Moreover, "willfulness" includes reckless disregard. If your accountant mentioned FBAR and you still didn't file, courts have found that sufficient for willfulness. See [fbar-penalties-what-happens-if-you-dont-file](/blog/fbar-penalties-what-happens-if-you-dont-file) for a full breakdown.

### Criminal Penalties

Under [31 USC 5322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5322), willful failure to file carries criminal penalties of up to **$250,000** in fines and **5 years** imprisonment. Criminal cases involve large undisclosed accounts, tax evasion, or fraud. Between 2009 and 2024, the Department of Justice prosecuted hundreds of such cases. *United States v. Zwerner* resulted in a $2.24 million jury verdict on accounts totaling $1.69 million.

## What Is the Reasonable Cause Defense?

The reasonable cause defense under 31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)(ii) can eliminate non-willful penalties. To qualify, you must show two things. First, you had reasonable cause for the failure. Second, any reportable income from the account appeared on your tax return. Qualifying circumstances include reliance on professional advice, illness, or a natural disaster. However, simply not knowing about FBAR is usually insufficient. This is especially true if you used a tax preparer who could have asked about foreign accounts.

What does NOT qualify:

- "I didn't know" — insufficient on its own when a preparer was involved
- "My accounts are small" — no de minimis exception above the $10,000 threshold
- "I pay foreign taxes" — foreign tax payments don't eliminate US reporting obligations

## How Do You File Your FBAR for 2025?

The FBAR for the 2025 tax year goes through [FinCEN's BSA E-Filing system](https://bsaefiling.fincen.gov/). Paper filing ended in 2013. You need three categories of information. First, your personal details: name, SSN or ITIN, and address. Second, account details for each foreign account: institution name, account number, type, maximum value in 2025, and country. Third, currency conversion rates from the [Treasury's fiscal data portal](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/). Most straightforward filings take 15-20 minutes.

### What Information Do You Need?

1. **Personal information**: Full legal name, SSN or ITIN, date of birth, current US address
2. **For each foreign account**:
   - Name and address of the financial institution
   - Account number
   - Type of account (bank, securities, or other)
   - Maximum value during 2025 in US dollars
   - Country where the account is held
3. **Exchange rates**: Treasury Department rates for December 31, 2025

For help calculating the maximum account value, see our guide: [how to calculate maximum account value for FBAR](/blog/how-to-calculate-maximum-account-value-fbar).

## What Should You Do If You've Missed Prior Years?

If you missed FBARs for 2024, 2023, or earlier, you have three options depending on your situation. The right choice depends on whether you also had unreported foreign income and whether your failure was willful. Filing late without following the correct procedure can create more exposure, not less.

### Option 1: Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures

**Best for**: Filers who reported all foreign income on their tax returns but missed the FBAR.

**How it works**:
- File delinquent FBARs through BSA E-Filing
- Include a statement explaining why the filings are late
- The IRS generally does not assess penalties if income reporting was correct

**Cost**: Free (no penalty if you qualify)

This is the simplest path. If you had a foreign savings account earning $200 in interest, reported that $200 on Schedule B, but forgot the FBAR, the Delinquent FBAR procedures apply.

### Option 2: Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures

**Best for**: Filers with both unreported foreign accounts AND unreported foreign income, where the failure was non-willful.

**How it works**:
- File the last **3 years** of amended or delinquent tax returns
- File the last **6 years** of delinquent FBARs
- Pay a **5% miscellaneous offshore penalty** on the highest aggregate foreign account balance (Streamlined Domestic)
- **No penalty** if you qualify as a foreign resident (Streamlined Foreign)
- Certify under penalty of perjury that the failure was non-willful

**Example**: If your highest foreign account balance over 6 years was $200,000, the Streamlined Domestic penalty is $10,000. That covers all years and accounts — far less than $12,909 per violation assessed outside the program.

First-time filers should also read our [first-time filer guide](/blog/fbar-first-time-filer-guide) before deciding on a path.

### Option 3: Voluntary Disclosure (Willful Non-Filers)

If you knew about the FBAR and chose not to file, the IRS Voluntary Disclosure Practice is the right path. You make full disclosure to IRS Criminal Investigation. This typically results in civil penalties rather than criminal prosecution. Penalties are higher than Streamlined — about 75% of tax due plus additional charges. However, you avoid criminal charges. This path requires a tax attorney. It is not a situation for self-filing.

## What Is the Statute of Limitations for FBAR Penalties?

The IRS has **6 years** from the violation date to assess FBAR penalties under [31 USC 5321(b)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The violation date is the filing deadline. That is April 15 or October 15, depending on which applies. For your 2025 FBAR, the statute runs until April 15, 2032. Six years is among the longest windows in US tax enforcement. By comparison, regular income tax audits have a 3-year window. The 6-year tax window only applies when you understate income by 25% or more.

## Why Should You File Early Rather Than Waiting Until October?

The automatic extension gives you until October 15. However, filing early is better for four reasons. First, you reduce the chance of forgetting — October is seven months from April. Second, your account details are fresher and easier to find. Third, filing early gives you time to fix errors before the final deadline. Fourth, a confirmed BSA E-Filing submission removes one item from your compliance list. Therefore, earlier is always better when you have the information ready.

**When is the FBAR filing deadline for 2026?**

The FBAR filing deadline in 2026 is April 15, 2026. This covers the 2025 calendar year. If you miss April 15, you automatically receive an extension to October 15, 2026. No form or request is needed. Both dates apply to the Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FinCEN Form 114). Filing by either date avoids penalties under 31 USC 5321.

**Do I need to file the FBAR if I have accounts in the UK?**

Yes. US persons must file the FBAR if their foreign accounts exceed $10,000 in aggregate at any point during the year, regardless of country. UK bank accounts are subject to the same rules as accounts in any other country. See our guide on [FBAR for UK bank accounts](/blog/fbar-uk-bank-accounts) for specifics on common UK institutions.

**What is the penalty for missing the FBAR deadline?**

The non-willful FBAR penalty is up to $12,909 per violation in 2026. Each account per year can be a separate violation. Willful violations carry the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance — per year, per account. Criminal charges under 31 USC 5322 are possible for the most serious cases.

**Does filing a tax extension also extend the FBAR deadline?**

No. Filing Form 4868 to extend your income tax return does not affect your FBAR deadline. The FBAR has a separate automatic extension to October 15. This extension exists independently of any tax return extension. The two filings are completely separate.

**Can I file the FBAR for free?**

**What accounts must I report on the FBAR?**

You must report bank accounts, securities accounts, and certain other financial accounts held at foreign financial institutions.
