# Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures FBAR: How to Catch Up on Missed Filings

The IRS streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program lets taxpayers who missed prior-year FBARs catch up with reduced penalties. You must certify that your failures were non-willful. Without this program, a willful FBAR violation costs the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance per year under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The streamlined program replaces that exposure with a 5% miscellaneous offshore penalty — or no penalty at all for qualifying expats.

The Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR), formally FinCEN Form 114, covers any U.S. person who held foreign financial accounts with an aggregate balance over $10,000 at any point during the calendar year, under [31 CFR 1010.306](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306). Taxpayers who missed prior-year filings under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314) can use the streamlined procedures, the Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures, or the IRS Voluntary Disclosure Program.

This article covers who qualifies, how SDOP and SFOP differ, what you must submit, the Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures penalty calculation, and the risks.

## What Does Streamlined Compliance Mean for FBAR Filers?

Streamlined compliance means filing all missing FBARs and amended tax returns under a reduced-penalty framework. You certify that your prior failures were non-willful. The IRS then waives the standard willful FBAR penalty under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). That penalty can reach $100,000 per year. Instead, the IRS applies a lower miscellaneous offshore penalty or no penalty. The program does not erase the past filings. However, it brings them current under more favorable penalty terms than a standard FBAR enforcement action would produce.

Additionally, the program covers both the FBAR obligation under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314) and unreported foreign income on your tax returns. You must address both in your submission. Fixing only the FBARs without amending your tax returns will not satisfy the IRS program rules.

### What Is Non-Willful Conduct?

Non-willful conduct is failure that results from negligence, inadvertence, or mistake. It does not include intentional disregard of known legal duties. Courts have interpreted non-willful to include:

- Failing to know FBAR reporting applied to foreign accounts
- Misunderstanding which accounts crossed the $10,000 aggregate threshold under [31 CFR 1010.306](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306)
- Relying on a tax preparer who never asked about foreign accounts
- Being a dual citizen unaware of U.S. BSA filing obligations

Non-willful conduct does not include hiding accounts on purpose, structuring transactions to avoid reporting, or ignoring clear IRS warnings about foreign account reporting.

### What Penalties Does the Streamlined Program Replace?

The standard non-willful FBAR penalty is up to $16,117 per year (2025 figure) under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The Supreme Court held in *Bittner v. United States*, 598 U.S. 85 (2023), that this penalty applies once per year — not once per account. The streamlined program replaces this annual penalty with either the 5% SDOP penalty or no penalty under SFOP.

## How Do the Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures Fbar Tracks Differ?

The streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program offers two tracks. SDOP (Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures) covers U.S. residents. SFOP (Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures) covers qualifying expats. The key difference is the penalty: SDOP charges 5% of the highest aggregate balance, while SFOP charges nothing for taxpayers who meet the non-residency test.

| Feature | SDOP (Domestic) | SFOP (Foreign) |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| Who qualifies | U.S. residents | Taxpayers living abroad |
| FBAR/offshore penalty | 5% of highest aggregate balance | No penalty |
| Non-residency test | Not required | 330+ days abroad in at least 1 covered year |
| Certification form | Form 14654 | Form 14653 |
| Returns to amend | 3 most recent tax years | 3 most recent tax years |
| FBARs to file | 6 most recent years | 6 most recent years |
| Tax owed | Tax + interest + 5% penalty | Tax + interest only |
| Willful penalty avoided | Up to $100,000+ per year per [31 USC 5321](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321) | Up to $100,000+ per year |

See the [IRS Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures) for current guidance on both tracks.

### Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures (SDOP) — Penalty Calculation

Under SDOP, the IRS replaces the standard FBAR penalty with a single 5% miscellaneous offshore penalty. The Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures penalty calculation is straightforward. You find the year with the highest aggregate balance across all foreign accounts and assets during the six-year lookback. You multiply that balance by 5%. You pay that amount once — not per year.

For example, if your highest aggregate balance was $200,000, the SDOP penalty is $10,000. That is well below the $100,000-per-year willful penalty under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). For a $400,000 peak balance, the SDOP penalty is $20,000.

To use SDOP, you must not be a resident outside the United States under the IRS residency test. Furthermore, you must not be under IRS audit, examination, or criminal investigation. Assets you reported on Form 8938 but not on the FBAR may be excluded from the penalty base in some cases. Consult a qualified tax professional before excluding any asset.

### Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures (SFOP) — Zero Penalty for Qualifying Expats

SFOP offers the most favorable terms. There is no FBAR penalty and no miscellaneous offshore penalty on unreported income. You pay only the tax owed on unreported foreign income plus statutory interest.

To qualify for SFOP, you must meet the IRS non-residency test. For U.S. citizens and green card holders, you must not have had a U.S. abode. You must also have been physically outside the United States for at least 330 full days. This requirement applies in any one or more of the three tax years you are amending. This test mirrors the foreign earned income exclusion under IRC Section 911.

You must still certify non-willful conduct under SFOP. Therefore, the absence of a penalty does not make the certification optional. A false certification can expose you to perjury charges. It can also turn what would have been a civil matter into a criminal referral.

## What Documents Must You Submit for the Streamlined Filing Procedures?

Both SDOP and SFOP require the same four-part document package. The streamlined filing compliance procedures require all four elements in one coordinated submission to the IRS. Missing any element can cause the IRS to treat your package as a routine delinquent filing — and you lose the penalty reduction.

### Required Documents for Both Tracks

Submit all of the following together:

1. **Amended tax returns** — File amended Forms 1040-X for the three most recent tax years whose due dates have passed, per [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures). Include Schedule B for foreign interest and dividends. Include Form 8938 if the foreign financial asset thresholds apply under [26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D) ($50,000 for domestic filers). Include Form 1116 for foreign tax credits.
2. **Late FBARs** — File FinCEN Form 114 for each of the six most recent calendar years in which you held a reportable foreign financial account. Use the [BSA E-Filing system](https://bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov/) at FinCEN.gov. Mark each FBAR as a late filing.
3. **Certification statement** — Complete and sign Form 14654 (for SDOP) or Form 14653 (for SFOP). Explain in detail why your failures were non-willful. A bare statement typically fails. Describe how you opened the accounts, what advice you received, and why your failures were mistakes.
4. **Payment** — For SDOP, include the 5% miscellaneous offshore penalty with your amended returns. For both tracks, pay all tax owed and applicable interest using EFTPS.

Write "Streamlined Foreign Offshore" or "Streamlined Domestic Offshore" on top of each amended return. Mail everything together to the IRS address on the certification form. Use certified mail and keep proof of delivery.

## What Are the Risks of the Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures?

The streamlined filing compliance procedures are not a guaranteed amnesty. The IRS can reject your certification, assess full statutory penalties, or refer your case to criminal investigators. You should understand three specific risks before you submit.

### Risk 1: IRS Rejection of the Non-Willful Certification

The IRS can review your certification and decide your conduct was willful. If that happens, the IRS can assess the full statutory FBAR penalty under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). That penalty is the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance per year. The IRS has five years from each violation to assess FBAR penalties under [31 USC 5321(b)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321).

A $500,000 account balance triggers a $250,000 willful penalty per year under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). Ten unreported years at that balance could push exposure well above $1,000,000.

### Risk 2: Criminal Referral

The IRS can refer your case to the Department of Justice. This happens when your submission reveals facts suggesting willful tax evasion or deliberate FBAR violations under the Bank Secrecy Act. This risk is low for taxpayers with genuinely non-willful failures. It is real for anyone who signed tax returns without disclosing foreign accounts while knowing those accounts existed.

### Risk 3: Audit Before Acceptance

If the IRS opens an examination of your returns before processing your streamlined submission, you lose access to the program for those years. The program excludes taxpayers already under examination.

## How Does the Streamlined Program Compare to Other FBAR Options?

If you missed FBARs, you have three main paths. The wrong one costs more in penalties or creates criminal exposure. Here is how the streamlined filing compliance procedures compare to alternatives.

| Option | Best For | Penalty | Criminal Protection |
|--------|----------|---------|---------------------|
| Streamlined Domestic (SDOP) | U.S. residents, non-willful | 5% of highest balance | No immunity |
| Streamlined Foreign (SFOP) | Expats, non-willful | None | No immunity |
| [Delinquent FBAR Procedures](/blog/fbar-delinquent-filing-procedures) | Late filer, no unreported income | None | None |
| [Voluntary Disclosure Program](/blog/fbar-voluntary-disclosure-practice) | Willful or uncertain | Negotiated | Yes — criminal protection |

The Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures apply only when you have no unreported foreign income. However, if your accounts earned interest or dividends you did not report, you need streamlined or VDP instead. The Voluntary Disclosure Program provides criminal protection that the streamlined program does not. For taxpayers who cannot certify non-willful conduct with confidence, VDP is the safer path. ## How Do You Submit a Streamlined FBAR Package?

Submitting the streamlined filing compliance procedures package requires a specific sequence. Do not file FBARs before the amended returns. Do not pay the penalty without the certification form. Follow these eight steps in order.

1. **Identify the covered years.** Find the three most recent tax years with past due dates per IRS instructions. Find the six most recent calendar years for FBAR purposes.
2. **Gather foreign account records.** Collect account statements for all six FBAR years. You need the highest balance in each year, the account number, the institution name and address, and any income credited. Use Treasury exchange rates from [fiscal.treasury.gov](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/reports-statements/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/current.html) to convert foreign currency balances at the December 31 rate for each year.
3. **Amend your tax returns.** Prepare amended Forms 1040-X for the three covered years. Report unreported foreign income and claim foreign tax credits on Form 1116. Attach Schedule B and Form 8938 if the $50,000 threshold under [26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D) applies.
4. **File the FBARs online.** Log into the BSA E-Filing system at [bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov](https://bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov/) and file FinCEN Form 114 for each of the six years. Select the correct calendar year. Save your confirmation numbers.
5. **Draft your non-willful certification.** Write a detailed narrative. Describe how you opened each account, what advice you received, and why you did not know about the FBAR obligation.
6. **Complete Form 14654 or 14653.** Transfer your narrative to the IRS form. Sign under penalty of perjury.
7. **Calculate and pay the SDOP penalty.** For SDOP, find the year with the highest aggregate balance. Multiply by 5%. For example, $400,000 × 5% = $20,000. Include that payment plus all tax owed and interest.
8. **Mail the complete package.** Send amended returns, signed certification, and penalty payment to the IRS address on the certification form. Use certified mail.

## Frequently Asked Questions

These questions cover the most common issues taxpayers face when using the IRS streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program. The answers apply to both SDOP and SFOP unless stated otherwise.

**What is the IRS streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program?**

The streamlined filing compliance procedures fbar program is an IRS initiative that allows U.S. taxpayers to catch up on missed Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts filings under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314) with reduced penalties. You must certify non-willful conduct and file three years of amended returns. You must also file six years of FBARs. Domestic filers pay the 5% SDOP miscellaneous offshore penalty. Qualifying expats under SFOP pay no penalty at all.

**Can I use the streamlined procedures if my accounts had no income?**

Yes. The streamlined filing compliance procedures apply when you missed FBARs, regardless of whether the accounts generated taxable income. If there is no unreported income, you may qualify for the [Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures](/blog/fbar-delinquent-filing-procedures) instead — those carry no penalty. But if your accounts earned any foreign interest, you need streamlined or VDP, not the delinquent procedures alone.

**What if I cannot certify non-willful conduct with confidence?**

Do not use the streamlined program. A false certification can result in the full willful FBAR penalty under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). That penalty is the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance per year under [31 USC 5321](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). You also face criminal exposure for perjury. The IRS Voluntary Disclosure Program offers criminal protection and negotiated terms. See [FBAR Willful vs Non-Willful Penalties](/blog/fbar-willful-vs-non-willful-penalties) for how to assess which path fits your situation.

**How does the Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures penalty calculation work?**

The SDOP penalty is 5% of the highest aggregate balance across all foreign accounts and assets during the six FBAR years. You pay it once — not per year. If your accounts peaked at $300,000 in year three of the lookback, the SDOP penalty is $15,000 total. That is 5% of $300,000, calculated under [31 CFR 1010.306](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306) reporting rules. You do not multiply $15,000 by six.

**What if the IRS opens an audit after I file my streamlined submission?**

If the IRS opens an examination after you file but before they process your submission, your access to the program ends for those years. The IRS then treats your package as a delinquent or amended filing. It can assess full statutory penalties. Contact a tax attorney right away if the IRS contacts you after you file a streamlined package.

**How does the streamlined program interact with the $16,117 non-willful FBAR penalty?**

The standard non-willful FBAR penalty is up to $16,117 per year (2025 figure) under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). The streamlined program replaces that per-year penalty with either the 5% SDOP miscellaneous offshore penalty or no penalty under SFOP. For most taxpayers, the streamlined result is far lower than the cumulative standard penalties. This is especially true for those with multiple years of unreported Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts filings.

**Can I use the streamlined procedures for FBAR penalties already assessed?**

No. The streamlined program covers only years with no prior IRS penalty assessment. If the IRS assessed FBAR penalties for a prior year, that year is not eligible. However, you may have other options. These include the [Reasonable Cause Defense](/blog/fbar-reasonable-cause-defense) or a collection due process appeal. Streamlined is not available for assessed years.
