# Calculate Maximum Account Value FBAR: Step-by-Step Instructions

To calculate maximum account value FBAR rules require, find the highest balance in each foreign account at any point during the year. The **Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts** uses this peak value. It is not your December 31 balance. It is not an average. This guide shows you each step with real examples. You will also learn the FBAR maximum account value exchange rate rules and the most common mistakes to avoid.

## What Is Maximum Account Value in FBAR?

The maximum account value is the largest balance in a foreign financial account at any point during the year. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(g)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), you report the peak value — not the year-end balance. This number tells you whether you cross the $10,000 aggregate threshold under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314). If you get it wrong, you either file when you do not need to or miss a required filing.

For example, if your account held $15,000 in June but only $3,000 in December, your maximum value is $15,000. Many filers make the mistake of using the December balance. However, the FBAR instructions state you must use the peak amount.

## Which Foreign Accounts Must You Include?

Before you calculate maximum account value FBAR filing requires, build a full account list. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), a "financial account" covers bank accounts, brokerage accounts, insurance policies with cash value, mutual funds, certain foreign pensions, and crypto exchange accounts at foreign platforms. Every account counts — even dormant ones. That old savings account in your home country with $2,500 must be on the list.

| Account Type | Reportable? | Maximum Value Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Foreign bank savings or checking | Yes | Highest closing or mid-period balance |
| Foreign brokerage or securities | Yes | Highest portfolio value during year |
| Foreign life insurance (cash value) | Yes | Highest cash surrender value |
| Foreign mutual fund | Yes | Highest net asset value |
| Foreign pension (some types) | Varies | Peak account balance |
| Foreign crypto exchange account | Yes | Highest total value |
| US-based accounts | No | Not counted |

### Example: Finding Your Foreign Accounts

Sarah is a dual US-UK citizen in New York. She holds a Barclays savings account in London, a Stocks and Shares ISA with Hargreaves Lansdown, and a joint checking account at HSBC UK shared with her mother. Her Chase account in New York is domestic. Therefore, it does not count. Sarah has three foreign accounts to check.

## How Do You Find the Peak Balance for Each Account?

For each foreign financial account, find the highest balance at any point during the year. Review every monthly or quarterly statement. Note the highest figure you see. If your bank gives daily history, check for mid-month peaks. For instance, a large wire received on one day and sent out two days later may be higher than any month-end balance.

Consider these rules by account type:

- **Bank accounts**: Review all 12 monthly statements. A peak on March 3 from a wire transfer matters even if the month-end balance is much lower.
- **Securities accounts**: Use the highest portfolio value during the year. If stocks were worth $120,000 in January and fell to $85,000 by December, the maximum value is $120,000.
- **Joint accounts**: Per [FinCEN guidance](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/bank-secrecy-act-fbar-guidance), each US person reports the **full value** of the joint account. Do not split it in half. Both US-person holders must file separate FBARs.

### Example: Sarah's Peak Values

After reviewing her 2025 account statements, Sarah finds these peaks:

1. **Barclays savings**: Monthly balances ranged from $32,000 to $45,000. The peak was $45,000 in July.
2. **Hargreaves Lansdown ISA**: Portfolio peaked at $28,500 in November.
3. **HSBC joint checking**: The account hit $8,200 in September. Sarah reports the full $8,200 — not half.

## How Do You Convert Foreign Currency to US Dollars?

To convert foreign currency, use the [Treasury Department exchange rate](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/reports-statements/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/current.html) for December 31 of the report year. Apply this year-end rate to each peak balance. Even when an account peaked in July, use the December 31 rate. In other words, FinCEN requires this one rate for all accounts, no matter when the peak happened. This FBAR maximum account value exchange rate rule trips up more filers than any other.

### Example: Converting Sarah's Accounts

For 2025, assume the Treasury December 31 rate for British pounds is 1 GBP = 1.27 USD:

1. **Barclays savings**: 45,000 GBP x 1.27 = **$57,150**
2. **Hargreaves Lansdown ISA**: 28,500 GBP x 1.27 = **$36,195**
3. **HSBC joint checking**: 8,200 GBP x 1.27 = **$10,414**

Each account now has a US dollar value. Consequently, Sarah can add them up.

## Does Your Total Exceed the $10,000 Filing Threshold?

Add all converted peak values together. Under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314), filing is required when the total exceeds $10,000 at any point during the year. Once you cross that line, you must report every foreign account — even those with small balances.

### Example: Sarah's Total

Sarah's combined peak values:

- Barclays savings: $57,150
- Hargreaves Lansdown ISA: $36,195
- HSBC joint checking: $10,414
- **Total: $103,759**

Sarah exceeds the $10,000 threshold. As a result, she must file an FBAR for all three accounts. Even the HSBC joint account alone at $10,414 would trigger filing. Two accounts at $6,000 and $5,000 would also require filing — the $11,000 total exceeds the limit.

## How Do You Handle Accounts in Multiple Countries?

When accounts are in different countries, follow the same steps for each one. Find the peak balance. Then convert using the December 31 Treasury rate for that currency. The only difference is that you work with a separate exchange rate for each country. Look up each rate on the Treasury website and apply it to the right account.

### Example: Raj's Three-Country Calculation

Raj holds accounts in India, the UK, and Canada. Using December 31, 2025 Treasury rates:

| Account | Peak Balance | Rate | USD Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| State Bank of India savings | 2,500,000 INR | / 83.50 | **$29,940** |
| Barclays UK current | 12,000 GBP | x 1.27 | **$15,240** |
| TD Canada Trust TFSA | 35,000 CAD | x 0.74 | **$25,900** |
| ICICI Bank fixed deposit | 1,800,000 INR | / 83.50 | **$21,557** |
| **Total** | | | **$92,637** |

Raj must file an FBAR for all four accounts. The Canadian TFSA is reportable. Even though it is tax-free in Canada, it remains a foreign financial account for US reporting purposes.

## How Much Money Can a US Citizen Have in a Foreign Bank?

No legal limit exists on how much money a US citizen can hold in a foreign bank. You can keep any amount abroad. However, once your total foreign account balances exceed $10,000 at any point during the year, you must file an FBAR under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314). If your foreign assets exceed $50,000 at year-end (or $75,000 at any point), you may need to file [Form 8938 with the IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/comparison-of-form-8938-and-fbar-requirements). ## What Are the Most Common Mistakes When You Calculate Maximum Account Value FBAR?

Six mistakes come up often in FBAR filings. Using the wrong balance, the wrong rate, or the wrong share of a joint account are the top errors. Catching these mistakes can prevent non-willful fines of [$12,909 per violation](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/fincen-updates-civil-penalty-amounts) under 31 USC 5321(a)(5). For a deeper look at fines, see our [FBAR penalties guide](/blog/fbar-penalties-what-happens-if-you-dont-file).

**Mistake 1: Using the year-end balance.** Your December 31 balance might be $7,000. However, if the account held $15,000 in June, your maximum value is $15,000.

**Mistake 2: Using the wrong exchange rate.** FinCEN requires the Treasury rate for December 31 of the report year. Therefore, do not use a Google rate, your bank's rate, or the rate from the peak date.

**Mistake 3: Reporting half of a joint account.** If you share an account, report the **full** value. Both US-person holders must file separate FBARs with the full balance. See our [joint account reporting rules guide](/blog/fbar-joint-account-reporting-rules).

**Mistake 4: Forgetting closed accounts.** If you closed an account in March but it held $25,000 at its peak, you still must report it.

**Mistake 5: Skipping small accounts after crossing the threshold.** Once you exceed $10,000 in total, report every foreign account — even one with $50.

**Mistake 6: Not knowing what counts as "foreign."** An account is foreign based on where the bank is located — not where you access it. An HSBC account opened in London is foreign even if you manage it from Texas. For UK-specific rules, see our guide on [FBAR for UK bank accounts](/blog/fbar-uk-bank-accounts).

## What If the FBAR Maximum Account Value Is Unknown?

When exact records are not available, use a good-faith estimate based on what you have. FinCEN accepts reasonable estimates when documents are limited. For foreign life insurance, use the peak cash surrender value from policy letters. For precious metals accounts, use the highest market value based on daily spot prices during the year. Meanwhile, foreign real estate held in your own name is not reportable. However, real estate held through a foreign entity may require reporting that entity's accounts. Write down your method and keep all records.

Per the FBAR instructions on [FinCEN's website](https://www.fincen.gov/report-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts), filers who cannot get exact figures should use the best data they can find. Document the source of each estimate.

## FAQ

This FAQ covers the most common questions about how to calculate maximum account value FBAR. The answers follow FinCEN guidance and the Bank Secrecy Act under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314) and [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350).

**How do I calculate maximum account value for FBAR?**
Find the highest balance in each foreign account at any point during the year. Then convert each peak to US dollars using the Treasury December 31 exchange rate. Finally, add all the converted peaks together. If the total exceeds $10,000, you must file an FBAR and report every foreign account. This process applies whether you hold one account or dozens.

**What exchange rate do I use for FBAR maximum value?**
Use the Treasury Department rate for December 31 of the report year. Apply this rate even if the account peaked months earlier. Find the rates at [fiscal.treasury.gov](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/reports-statements/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/current.html). If the Treasury does not list a rate for your currency, use another verifiable rate and write down the source.

**Do I include joint accounts in the FBAR maximum value calculation?**
Yes. Each US person with a financial interest in a joint account must report the full balance — not a share. If two US persons share a joint account, both file separate FBARs with the full value. See our guide on [FBAR joint account reporting rules](/blog/fbar-joint-account-reporting-rules) for more details.

**What happens if I report the wrong maximum account value on my FBAR?**
An incorrect FBAR can lead to non-willful fines of up to $12,909 per violation under 31 USC 5321(a)(5). Willful errors carry higher fines. If you find a mistake, consider filing an amended or delinquent FBAR through the [FinCEN BSA E-Filing system](https://www.fincen.gov/report-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts). See our [FBAR penalties guide](/blog/fbar-penalties-what-happens-if-you-dont-file) for the full breakdown.

**Does the $10,000 threshold apply per account or in aggregate?**
The threshold applies in aggregate. You add the peak values of all your foreign accounts together. Two accounts that peaked at $6,000 and $5,000 produce an $11,000 total. That exceeds the $10,000 limit and triggers a filing.

**Are crypto exchange accounts included in the FBAR calculation?**
Accounts at foreign-based crypto exchanges are reportable. Include them in your total. See our guide on [FBAR and cryptocurrency exchanges](/blog/fbar-cryptocurrency-foreign-exchanges) for details on how to value crypto holdings.

## Key Takeaways

Here are the key takeaways when you calculate maximum account value FBAR, based on FinCEN guidance and the Bank Secrecy Act. Review them before you gather your foreign financial account statements each year so you report each account correctly on FinCEN Form 114.

1. **Maximum value = peak balance at any point during the year.** It is not the year-end balance.
2. **Use the Treasury Department December 31 exchange rate.** Do not use Google, your bank's rate, or the rate from the peak date.
3. **Joint accounts: report the full value.** Each US person files separately with the full balance.
4. **Once you cross $10,000 in total**, report every foreign account no matter the size.
5. **Closed accounts still count** if they existed at any point during the year.
6. **Document your method.** Keep statements that show how you found each peak value.
