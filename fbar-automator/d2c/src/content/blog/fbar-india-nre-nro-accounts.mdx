# FBAR India NRE NRO Accounts: What US Persons Must Report

More than four million Indian-Americans live in the United States. Many hold accounts in India — NRE savings accounts, NRO accounts, FCNR deposits, Public Provident Fund accounts, fixed deposits, and demat accounts for Indian equities. If the combined value of all your foreign accounts exceeded $10,000 at any point during the year, you must file the Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR) with FinCEN under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350).

FBAR India NRE NRO accounts is the phrase many Indian-Americans search when they discover this rule. NRE, NRO, and FCNR accounts are all foreign financial accounts under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA). Every account type discussed in this article is reportable. The only question is whether your total foreign account values cross the $10,000 threshold.

India signed a FATCA Model 1 Intergovernmental Agreement with the United States. Under this agreement, Indian banks report US-person account data to India's Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT), which then shares it with the IRS. If you skip the FBAR, the IRS may already have records of your Indian accounts.

## Which FBAR India NRE NRO Accounts Are Reportable?

All standard Indian bank accounts held by a US person are reportable under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350). This includes NRE accounts, NRO accounts, FCNR accounts, fixed deposits, PPF accounts where you have a financial interest, and demat accounts. The account name and any Indian tax treatment do not reduce your US reporting duty. You report each account separately and list the peak value in USD.

Here is a summary of the most common Indian account types and their FBAR status.

| Account Type | Full Name | FBAR Reportable? |
|---|---|---|
| NRE | Non-Resident External | Yes |
| NRO | Non-Resident Ordinary | Yes |
| FCNR | Foreign Currency Non-Resident | Yes |
| PPF | Public Provident Fund | Yes (if US person has financial interest) |
| Fixed Deposit | Fixed-term bank deposit | Yes |
| Demat Account | Dematerialized securities account | Yes |
| NPS | National Pension System | Yes |
| SCSS | Senior Citizens Savings Scheme | Yes |

### NRE Accounts (Non-Resident External)

An NRE account is a Non-Resident External account. It holds Indian rupees funded from earnings outside India. Interest on NRE accounts is tax-free under Indian law, and you can move the principal back to the US at any time.

NRE account status under Indian law does not affect US reporting duties. Under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314) and the Bank Secrecy Act, you must report it on the FBAR. An NRE savings account with a peak balance of ₹900,000 (about $10,800 at ₹83 per USD) clears the $10,000 threshold on its own.

When you return to India, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) requires you to convert your NRE account to a resident account. If you are a US person after that change, the account is still a foreign account and still reportable.

### NRO Accounts (Non-Resident Ordinary)

An NRO account is a Non-Resident Ordinary account. It holds Indian income — rental income, dividends, interest, and pension payments. NRO accounts are subject to Indian tax and have RBI repatriation limits.

NRO accounts are reportable on the FBAR regardless of those repatriation restrictions. The RBI limit on moving money out of India does not change your US reporting obligation. Many Indian-Americans hold NRO accounts to receive rent from Indian properties. A rental property generating ₹50,000 per month adds ₹600,000 ($7,229) to the NRO balance each year. Therefore, combined with an NRE account, the aggregate often exceeds $10,000.

### FCNR Accounts (Foreign Currency Non-Resident)

An FCNR account is a Foreign Currency Non-Resident deposit account. Unlike NRE and NRO accounts, an FCNR account holds a foreign currency — USD, GBP, EUR, AUD, JPY, or other RBI-approved currencies. FCNR accounts are time deposits with fixed maturities.

If the account holds USD, the peak balance is the reportable amount directly. If it holds a different currency, apply the US Treasury year-end exchange rate. An FCNR deposit held in USD at SBI or HDFC Bank is a foreign financial account under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350) because the bank is located outside the United States.

### Fixed Deposits at Indian Banks

Fixed deposits (FDs) at Indian banks are reportable foreign financial accounts. FDs are time-bound deposits paying a fixed interest rate. Common FD providers include State Bank of India, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, and Kotak Mahindra Bank.

Report the peak balance of each FD during the year. If you reinvest a matured FD into a new FD, the new FD is a separate account. A laddered FD portfolio worth ₹2,000,000 converts to about $24,096 at ₹83 per USD — well above the $10,000 threshold under [31 CFR 1010.306](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306).

### Public Provident Fund (PPF)

A PPF account is a government-backed long-term savings scheme with a 15-year lock-in period. PPF accounts are reportable if you have a financial interest. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(e)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), you have a financial interest if you own the account. A PPF account in your own name gives you a direct financial interest.

The RBI restricts NRIs from opening new PPF accounts. However, NRIs who opened PPF accounts before becoming NRIs can continue contributing for the existing term. Those accounts remain reportable each year. A PPF balance of ₹1,500,000 ($18,072 at ₹83/USD) is reportable. PPF accounts are missed because filers assume the Indian tax exemption extends to US reporting — it does not.

### Demat Accounts for Indian Securities

A demat (dematerialized) account holds Indian stocks, mutual funds, bonds, and exchange-traded funds. If you have a financial interest in or signature authority over a demat account at HDFC Securities, Zerodha, or ICICI Direct, you must report it on the FBAR.

Demat accounts are securities accounts under [31 CFR 1010.350(c)(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350). Report the peak value of all holdings in the account during the year, converted to USD using the Treasury year-end rate. A demat account holding Nifty 50 ETFs worth ₹800,000 ($9,639) does not trigger reporting alone. However, combined with an NRE savings account, the aggregate crosses $10,000.

## How Do You Convert INR to USD for FBAR Reporting?

To convert Indian rupees to USD for FBAR reporting, use the US Treasury's end-of-year exchange rate. Apply the December 31 rate to the peak balance in INR for each account. Do not use the spot rate on the date of the peak or a yearly average. The FBAR instructions require the Treasury year-end rate. For more detail, see our guide on [FBAR exchange rates and the Treasury Department](/blog/fbar-exchange-rates-treasury-department).

The Treasury publishes rates including the Indian rupee on its [Treasury Reporting Rates of Exchange page](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/treasury-reporting-rates-of-exchange).

### Step-by-Step INR Conversion

1. Find the peak INR balance of each Indian account during the calendar year.
2. Look up the Treasury's December 31 rate for INR/USD at [fiscal.treasury.gov](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/treasury-reporting-rates-of-exchange).
3. Divide the INR balance by the INR-per-USD rate (or multiply if quoted as USD-per-INR).
4. Sum the converted USD values for all foreign accounts worldwide.
5. If the total exceeds $10,000, file the FBAR.

### Conversion Example: Three Indian Accounts

Priya is a US green card holder who moved from Chennai to California in 2020. She holds three Indian accounts:

- NRE savings account at SBI: peak balance ₹720,000
- NRO account at HDFC Bank: peak balance ₹380,000
- PPF account (opened before becoming NRI): balance ₹1,200,000

Using the Treasury December 31, 2025 rate of 1 USD = ₹85.50:

- NRE: ₹720,000 ÷ 85.50 = $8,421
- NRO: ₹380,000 ÷ 85.50 = $4,444
- PPF: ₹1,200,000 ÷ 85.50 = $14,035

Aggregate maximum value: **$26,900**. Priya must file an FBAR for 2025 under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), reporting all three accounts. She should also check [Form 8938 (FATCA)](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938) — if she files jointly, the domestic FATCA threshold is $100,000.

## What Are the FBAR Penalties for Unreported Indian Accounts?

The IRS can assess civil penalties for each year you fail to file the FBAR. For non-willful failures, the penalty is up to **$16,117 per violation** under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321) (2026 inflation-adjusted amount). The Supreme Court ruled in *United States v. Bittner* (2023) that non-willful penalties apply per report, not per account. One missed year equals one violation.

Willful violations carry higher penalties. A willful failure to file results in a penalty of the greater of **$100,000** or **50% of the unreported account balance** under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). Criminal penalties under [31 USC 5322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5322) can reach **$250,000** and five years in prison.

Additionally, India's FATCA Model 1 IGA increases detection risk. Indian banks report US-person account data to the CBDT, which shares it with the IRS. The IRS often holds records of NRE and NRO account balances before it checks FBAR compliance.

For a full breakdown of the penalty structure, see our article on [FBAR penalties and what happens if you do not file](/blog/fbar-penalties-what-happens-if-you-dont-file).

## Does the India-US Tax Treaty Reduce FBAR Obligations?

No. The India-US Tax Treaty does not reduce FBAR reporting obligations. The FBAR is a Bank Secrecy Act requirement under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314), not an income tax rule. The treaty covers income tax credits, reduced withholding rates, and tie-breaker residency rules. It does not override BSA reporting duties.

Many Indian-Americans use treaty positions to reduce US tax on NRO interest income. That treaty election is separate from FBAR. You can claim a treaty benefit on your income tax return and still need to file the FBAR for the same accounts.

The treaty tiebreaker residency rule also does not help. If you claim non-resident status under the treaty tiebreaker, you are treated as a non-resident for income tax. However, the FBAR is based on your status as a "US person" under the Bank Secrecy Act. That definition covers green card holders and citizens regardless of treaty elections. See our article on [FBAR rules for green card holders](/blog/fbar-green-card-holders) for more.

These examples show how FBAR requirements apply to common Indian-American situations. Each example uses approximate INR-to-USD conversions at ₹85 per USD.

### Scenario 1: Software Engineer With NRE and Fixed Deposits

Arjun is a US citizen who moved from Hyderabad to Seattle in 2018. He keeps his State Bank of India NRE savings account and a ladder of fixed deposits at HDFC Bank.

- SBI NRE savings account: peak ₹1,500,000 ($17,647)
- HDFC Bank FD #1 (3-year): ₹2,000,000 ($23,529)
- HDFC Bank FD #2 (5-year): ₹3,500,000 ($41,176)

Aggregate maximum: **$82,352**. Arjun must file an FBAR for 2025 under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350) and report all three accounts. He should also verify whether his total foreign assets exceed the FATCA threshold under [26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D), which triggers Form 8938.

If your situation is similar but you missed prior years, the [Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures) may let you catch up with reduced penalties.

### Scenario 2: Retiree With NRO Rental Income Account

Meena is a US citizen who retired in Florida. Her parents left her a house in Bangalore that she rents out. The rent flows into her NRO account at Axis Bank. She also holds an old PPF account opened before she immigrated.

- Axis Bank NRO account: peak ₹650,000 ($7,647)
- PPF account: balance ₹2,200,000 ($25,882)

Aggregate maximum: **$33,529**. Meena must file an FBAR. The NRO account alone falls below $10,000, but the aggregate with the PPF account clears the threshold. She must also report the NRO rental income on her US tax return.

### Scenario 3: Green Card Holder With Demat Account and NRE Account

Vikram received his US green card in 2023. He has an HDFC Securities demat account holding Nifty 50 index funds and an ICICI Bank NRE savings account.

- HDFC Securities demat account: peak value ₹950,000 ($11,176)
- ICICI Bank NRE savings: peak ₹820,000 ($9,647)

Aggregate maximum: **$20,823**. Vikram must file an FBAR for 2025. His filing obligation began the year he received his green card under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350). ## What Is the FBAR Deadline for Indian Account Holders?

The FBAR for the 2025 tax year is due on **April 15, 2026** under [31 CFR 1010.306(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.306). FinCEN grants an automatic extension to **October 15, 2026** — you do not need to request it. No penalties apply between April 15 and October 15. After October 15, the IRS may assess penalties for late filing. Indian account holders who have never filed should act before that date.

If you have Indian accounts and have never filed an FBAR, three compliance paths exist:

1. **Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures**: For non-willful filers, you must file six years of FBARs and three years of tax returns under [IRS Rev. Proc. 2014-55](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures), with reduced or zero penalties.
2. **Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures**: If you have no unreported income and no active IRS examination, file past FBARs with a statement explaining the late filing. Penalties are often waived under IRS delinquent filing procedures.
3. **Voluntary Disclosure Practice**: For willful cases, the formal IRS program that reduces criminal exposure.

Many Indian-Americans missed prior years because they assumed NRE accounts were tax-exempt under US law. The NRE interest exemption is an Indian tax rule — it does not create a US reporting exemption. Therefore, act before the IRS contacts you. Penalty reduction programs close once an examination begins.

## FBAR vs. Form 8938 for Indian Accounts

Many US persons with Indian accounts must file both the FBAR and [IRS Form 8938](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938) under IRC 6038D. The two reports have different thresholds, different filing destinations, and different penalties. Filing one does not satisfy the other. NRIs with large FD portfolios, PPF accounts, and NRE savings frequently exceed both thresholds.

| | FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) | Form 8938 |
|---|---|---|
| **Filed with** | FinCEN via BSA E-Filing | IRS (attached to tax return) |
| **Threshold (domestic, single)** | $10,000 aggregate | $50,000 on last day or $75,000 any time |
| **Threshold (domestic, joint)** | $10,000 aggregate | $100,000 on last day or $150,000 any time |
| **Threshold (expat, single)** | $10,000 aggregate | $200,000 on last day or $300,000 any time |
| **Covers PPF?** | Yes | Yes |
| **Covers Demat accounts?** | Yes | Yes |
| **Non-willful penalty** | Up to $16,117 per violation ([31 USC 5321](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321)) | Up to $10,000 per failure ([26 USC 6038D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6038D)) |

For a full comparison, see our article on [FBAR vs. Form 8938 differences](/blog/fbar-vs-fatca-form-8938-differences).

## Frequently Asked Questions About FBAR India NRE NRO Accounts

These frequently asked questions cover the most common FBAR India NRE NRO accounts issues. If your Indian accounts exceeded $10,000 in combined peak value at any point during the year — whether NRE, NRO, FCNR, PPF, fixed deposits, or demat — you must file the FBAR under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350). The answers below address specific Indian account types and Indian-American situations.

**Do I report my NRE account on the FBAR if interest is tax-free in India?**

Yes. The NRE account interest exemption is an Indian income tax rule under the Income Tax Act, 1961. It has no effect on US FBAR reporting. Under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314) and [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), the account is a foreign financial account because a bank outside the United States holds it. The Indian tax treatment of the interest does not change your FBAR duty.

**Is a PPF account reportable if I cannot withdraw for 15 years?**

Yes. The 15-year lock-in period does not remove the FBAR obligation. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.350), you must report accounts where you have a financial interest, even if access is restricted. The lock-in period is a withdrawal restriction, not a bar on financial interest. Report the full PPF balance each year.

**What if my Indian accounts are in my spouse's name?**

If your spouse holds the account and you have no financial interest and no signature authority, you do not report it on your own FBAR. However, if you are a joint holder, or your spouse holds the account as your agent, you must report it. See our article on [FBAR joint account reporting rules](/blog/fbar-joint-account-reporting-rules) for how joint ownership works.

**Do I need to report an Indian account I closed during the year?**

Yes. If you held the account at any point during the calendar year and your aggregate foreign accounts exceeded $10,000, you must report the closed account. Use the maximum balance it held before you closed it. Closed accounts are reported the same way as open ones — enter the bank name, account number, and peak value.

**Can the India-US FATCA IGA affect my FBAR compliance?**

Yes. Under India's FATCA Model 1 IGA, Indian banks report US-person account information to the CBDT, which shares it with the IRS. The IRS often holds records of your NRE, NRO, and FCNR account balances before you file anything. Not filing the FBAR when the IRS holds matching data increases your penalty risk.

**Are mutual funds in an Indian demat account separately reportable?**

No. You report mutual funds held inside a demat account as part of the total demat account value, not as separate line items. Report the demat account's peak total value — including all funds, stocks, and bonds within it. If you hold mutual funds directly through CAMS or KFintech outside a demat account, those are separate reportable accounts.
