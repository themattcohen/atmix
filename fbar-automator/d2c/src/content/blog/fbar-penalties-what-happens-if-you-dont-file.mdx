## What Are FBAR Penalties and How Severe Are They?

FBAR penalties are among the harshest in US tax enforcement. The Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR) carries strict civil and criminal penalties for non-filers. A single unreported foreign financial account with $50,000 can trigger a non-willful penalty of $12,909. Willful violations can reach $100,000 or 50% of the account balance — whichever is greater. These penalties apply per year. They can quickly exceed the account balance itself.

The IRS and Department of Justice have stepped up FBAR enforcement since the 2008 UBS scandal. Knowing the penalty structure is the first step toward protecting yourself. Voluntary disclosure before IRS contact can reduce what you owe.

## What Are the Three Tiers of FBAR Penalties?

FBAR penalties fall into three tiers based on the taxpayer's level of intent. The key factor is **willfulness** — whether you knew about the FBAR requirement and chose not to file. Non-willful violations carry lower civil penalties. Willful violations face much larger civil fines. Serious cases can lead to criminal prosecution. The tier you fall into can mean the difference between a $12,909 penalty and a $200,000+ penalty for the same unreported account.

### Tier 1: Non-Willful Civil Penalties

**Statute**: [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)(i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321)

**Maximum penalty**: **$12,909 per violation** (2026 inflation-adjusted amount; the base of $10,000 adjusts each year under the Federal Civil Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act of 2015)

**What counts as "non-willful"**: In other words, you failed to file because you did not know about the requirement, misunderstood it, or made a genuine mistake. There was no deliberate effort to hide accounts.

**How the IRS counts violations**: The Supreme Court clarified this in *United States v. Bittner* (2023). For non-willful violations, the penalty applies **per report (per year)**, not per account. Miss one FBAR covering five accounts? That is one violation — up to $12,909 — not five. Miss FBARs for three years? That is three violations: up to **$38,727** total.

### Tier 2: Willful Civil Penalties

**Statute**: [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321)

**Maximum penalty** — the greater of:
- **$100,000** per violation, or
- **50% of the account balance** at the time of the violation

**What counts as "willful"**: You knew — or should have known — about the FBAR requirement and chose not to file. Courts also apply "reckless disregard." This includes:
- Actual knowledge of the FBAR rule plus deliberate non-filing
- Hearing about the requirement from a CPA and ignoring it
- Active concealment of foreign accounts
- Lying to your tax preparer about foreign accounts

**How the IRS counts violations**: Unlike non-willful violations, the IRS assesses willful FBAR penalties **per account, per year**. Five accounts unreported for three years equals 15 violations.

**Example**: David has a Swiss bank account with $400,000. His CPA asked him each year if he had foreign accounts, and he said no. When the IRS discovers the account:

| Year | Penalty Basis | Annual Willful Penalty |
|------|--------------|----------------------|
| Year 1 | 50% × $400,000 | $200,000 |
| Year 2 | 50% × $400,000 | $200,000 |
| Year 3 | 50% × $400,000 | $200,000 |
| **Total** | | **$600,000** |

As a result, that total exceeds the account balance itself — before adding back taxes and interest.

### Tier 3: Criminal Penalties

**Statute**: [31 USC 5322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5322)

**Maximum penalty**:
- Up to **$250,000** in fines, and/or
- Up to **5 years** imprisonment

Additionally, for violations connected to other crimes — such as tax evasion under 26 USC 7201 — penalties increase to **$500,000** and **10 years**.

**When criminal prosecution occurs**: IRS Criminal Investigation (CI) targets cases involving:
- Large undisclosed foreign accounts (often $1,000,000+)
- Active concealment through nominee accounts or shell companies
- Obstruction, such as destroying records or lying to agents
- Patterns of evasion across multiple years
- Connection to money laundering or fraud

Between 2010 and 2024, courts gave average prison terms of 2-3 years in FBAR criminal cases. Judges also imposed fines and civil penalties on top of prison time.

## What Makes an FBAR Violation "Willful"?

The willfulness question is the most important in any FBAR case. It is the difference between a manageable civil penalty and a devastating one. Courts apply the *Safeco Insurance Co. v. Burr* standard. Willfulness requires a "knowing violation of a known legal duty" or "reckless disregard" of that duty. Reckless disregard means taking risks far beyond normal carelessness. In other words, the IRS does not need to prove you intended to break the law — only that you ignored it.

### Factors That Point Toward Willfulness

- **Checking "No" on Schedule B**: Form 1040 Schedule B asks: did you have a financial interest or signature authority over a foreign financial account? Answering "No" while holding foreign accounts signals awareness — and a deliberate misrepresentation.
- **Ignored professional advice**: Your CPA told you about the FBAR requirement and you did not file.
- **Large account balances**: The IRS argues it is harder to forget a $500,000 account than a $5,000 one.
- **Skilled taxpayer**: People in finance, law, or accounting face a higher awareness standard.
- **Active hiding**: Using nominee names, structuring deposits, or keeping accounts in secrecy havens.
- **Selective reporting**: Filing FBARs for some accounts but not others suggests knowledge of the rule.

### Factors That Point Against Willfulness

- **First-time filer**: No prior FBAR history, especially for recent immigrants or new green card holders.
- **No foreign income**: If accounts earned little interest and there was no tax benefit to concealment, willfulness is harder to prove.
- **Reliance on professional advice**: If your CPA did not ask about foreign accounts and you relied on them for compliance, this fact supports a non-willful finding.
- **Prompt disclosure**: Coming forward before the IRS contacts you demonstrates good faith.
- **Cultural factors**: Immigrants from countries without similar reporting rules may not be aware.

### Penalty Comparison: Willful vs. Non-Willful

| Factor | Non-Willful | Willful |
|--------|------------|---------|
| Maximum civil penalty | $12,909 per year | $100,000 or 50% of balance (per account, per year) |
| Counted per | Annual report | Account and year |
| Criminal risk | No | Possible |
| Statute | 31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B) | 31 USC 5321(a)(5)(C) |
| IRS must prove | Mistake or ignorance | Knowledge or reckless disregard |

## What Is the FBAR Statute of Limitations?

The IRS has **6 years** from the date of the violation to assess FBAR penalties. The clock starts on the FBAR filing deadline for that year. For most filers, the formula is simple: FBAR due date + 6 years. A 2019 FBAR (due April 15, 2020) expires on April 15, 2026. In contrast, there is no fraud exception that removes the FBAR statute — unlike income tax, where fraud removes the deadline.

| FBAR Year | Due Date | Statute Expires |
|-----------|----------|----------------|
| 2019 | April 15, 2020 | April 15, 2026 |
| 2020 | April 15, 2021 | April 15, 2027 |
| 2021 | April 15, 2022 | April 15, 2028 |
| 2025 | April 15, 2026 | April 15, 2032 |

Six years is a long window. [FATCA](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/corporations/foreign-account-tax-compliance-act-fatca) sends account data from over 300,000 foreign institutions to the IRS each year. Consequently, the chance of discovery grows every year you wait. See the [IRS FBAR enforcement page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) for current guidance on international compliance priorities.

## Real-World FBAR Penalty Cases

### Case 1: *United States v. Zwerner* (2014)

Carl Zwerner held a Swiss UBS account worth about $1.69 million and argued his failure to file was non-willful. A jury disagreed. The court assessed a penalty of **$2.24 million** — about 133% of the account balance. The parties later settled for a reduced amount. The lesson: once a court finds willfulness, stacked annual penalties can exceed the total account value.

### Case 2: *United States v. Bittner* (2023, Supreme Court)

Alexandru Bittner is a dual Romanian-US citizen who missed FBARs for five years covering 272 foreign accounts. The government claimed $2.72 million, assessing penalties per account. Bittner argued the penalty should be per report — $50,000 total. The Supreme Court ruled 5-4 in his favor. For **non-willful** violations, the maximum is $12,909 per annual FBAR report, not per account. However, this ruling does **not** apply to willful violations.

### Case 3: Streamlined Filing vs. Enforcement Penalties

Elena is a green card holder from Brazil. She had three accounts there with a peak aggregate value of $180,000 over six years. She reported all interest income on her tax returns but did not know about the FBAR form.

| Path | Calculation | Total Cost |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| Streamlined (voluntary) | 5% × $180,000 | **$9,000** |
| IRS enforcement (non-willful) | $12,909 × 6 years | **$77,454** |
| IRS enforcement (willful) | 50% × $180,000 × 6 years | **$540,000** |

Voluntary disclosure through Streamlined procedures saves real money. The gap between a $9,000 Streamlined penalty and $77,454 in enforcement penalties speaks for itself.

## How Can You Fix Past FBAR Non-Filing?

If you have unreported foreign financial accounts, you can fix past non-filing through three IRS-approved paths. The right path depends on whether your failure was willful and whether you reported the income. Acting before the IRS contacts you is critical — it keeps your options open and reduces your penalty exposure. Early action is the better choice.

### Path 1: Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures

**Who qualifies**: Filers who reported all foreign income on their tax returns but missed the FBAR filing.

**Steps**:
1. File delinquent FBARs through [FinCEN's BSA E-Filing system](https://bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov) for all missed years
2. Include a statement explaining why each FBAR is late
3. The IRS will not often assess penalties when you reported all income on your tax returns

**Likely cost**: Zero penalty if you reported all income and the IRS has not contacted you about an examination. This is the best outcome available.

### Path 2: Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures

**Who qualifies**: Non-willful violators who may also have unreported foreign income.

**Steps**:
1. File 3 years of amended or delinquent income tax returns
2. File 6 years of delinquent FBARs
3. Pay all tax and interest on unreported income
4. Pay the 5% miscellaneous offshore penalty (Domestic) or zero penalty (Foreign)
5. Certify non-willful conduct under penalty of perjury

**Penalty math**: The 5% applies to the highest total foreign account value in the 6-year look-back period. For example, for a $250,000 peak value, the Streamlined Domestic penalty is $12,500 — flat. This covers all accounts and all years in one payment.

**The Foreign option**: If you lived outside the US for at least 330 days in at least one of the three covered tax years, you qualify for Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures. The penalty is zero. You pay only back taxes and interest.

For more on who qualifies, see our guide to [first-time filer options](/blog/fbar-first-time-filer-guide) and our comparison of [FBAR vs. FATCA Form 8938](/blog/fbar-vs-fatca-form-8938-differences).

### Path 3: IRS Voluntary Disclosure Practice

**Who qualifies**: Filers whose non-compliance was willful — or who cannot certify non-willfulness under penalty of perjury.

**Process**: Submit a preclearance request to IRS Criminal Investigation, then provide full disclosure. This is not a self-service program. You need a tax attorney.

**Outcome**: You avoid criminal prosecution. In exchange, you pay civil penalties — often 75% of total tax liability, plus other civil fines. It is expensive, but far better than prosecution.

## What Are the Current IRS FBAR Enforcement Trends?

IRS enforcement of FBAR penalties has grown since FATCA took effect in 2010. The IRS now gets foreign account data from over 300,000 banks worldwide. The agency can find non-filers without an audit. The chance of discovery rises every year. If you have an unreported foreign account at a FATCA-compliant bank, the IRS already has your data. Key enforcement methods include:

- **FATCA reporting**: Over 300,000 foreign banks in 110+ countries send US account holder data to the IRS each year.
- **Common Reporting Standard (CRS)**: The US is not a CRS participant, but data flows through bilateral agreements and tax treaties are increasing.
- **John Doe summonses**: The IRS has used summonses to obtain account holder records from banks and cryptocurrency exchanges.
- **Data analytics**: The IRS cross-references FBAR filings against FATCA data, foreign tax credit claims, and Schedule B disclosures.

The era of hidden foreign accounts is over. Data exchange is automatic and growing. Filing your FBAR is not optional — the IRS will get the data sooner or later.

## Does the IRS Waive FBAR Penalties If All Income Was Reported?

The IRS does not waive FBAR penalties just because you reported all income on your tax return. FBAR filing is a separate legal duty under the Bank Secrecy Act — not part of income tax reporting ([31 U.S.C. 5321](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321)).

Nevertheless, the IRS may consider "reasonable cause" when it decides whether to assess penalties. If you can show that your failure to file was not willful neglect — and that you reported all foreign income — this can work in your favor during the penalty review process.

The [IRS Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures) offer a formal path for filers who can certify their non-compliance was non-willful. Under these procedures, the IRS may reduce or drop the penalty for late FBARs. Filers who lived outside the US for 330+ days in a covered tax year may owe zero penalty under the Streamlined Foreign Offshore option.

## Frequently Asked Questions About FBAR Penalties

These questions cover the most common concerns about FBAR penalty amounts, timing, and options for late filers. If your situation is complex — if willfulness is a concern — consult a tax attorney before filing anything.

**What is the minimum FBAR penalty?**

There is no guaranteed minimum for civil FBAR penalties. The IRS may impose no penalty for non-willful violations through the Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures — if you reported all income and the IRS has not contacted you. However, once the IRS opens an examination, the minimum non-willful penalty is $12,909 per annual report.

**Can the IRS assess FBAR penalties and income tax penalties at the same time?**

Yes. FBAR penalties are separate from income tax penalties. Both can run at the same time. If you had unreported foreign interest income, the IRS can also assess: income tax on the unreported amount, accuracy-related penalties of 20–40% of the tax owed, and interest on unpaid tax. All of these stack on top of the FBAR civil penalty under 31 USC 5321.

**Does filing a late FBAR trigger a penalty?**

No. Filing a late FBAR does not trigger a penalty on its own. The IRS can assess penalties, but it uses discretion. Factors favoring no penalty include: no prior FBAR violation history, all income reported on tax returns, voluntary filing before IRS contact, and a reasonable cause for the late filing.

**What happens if I joint-file an FBAR with my spouse?**

Joint account holders can file a single FBAR if both spouses sign. However, each person with signature authority or financial interest in a foreign financial account must file their own FBAR. See our detailed guide on [joint account FBAR reporting rules](/blog/fbar-joint-account-reporting-rules) for full requirements.

**How does the FBAR filing deadline work for 2025 accounts?**

The 2025 FBAR covers foreign financial accounts open at any point during calendar year 2025. The deadline is April 15, 2026, with an automatic extension to October 15, 2026. You do not need to request an extension. See our complete [FBAR filing deadline guide for 2026](/blog/fbar-filing-deadline-2026) for details on the extension and late-filing rules.

**Is the IRS waiving penalties for late FBAR if all income is reported?**

Yes, in practice. The IRS Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures allow late filers to submit overdue FBARs without penalties — provided they reported all foreign income on their tax returns and the IRS has not contacted them about the accounts. To use this path, you file the late FBAR form through [BSA E-Filing](https://bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov) with a statement explaining the delay. The IRS will not often assess FBAR penalties in this situation. This is not a formal statutory waiver. Instead, the IRS exercises its discretion under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)(B)(ii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321) to waive penalties when reasonable cause exists.

**Is there an FBAR penalty waiver for first-time filers?**

The IRS has discretion to waive penalties for first-time non-willful violators. The Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures provide a penalty waiver when filers reported all income on their tax returns. However, there is no formal statutory waiver program — the IRS decides case by case.
