# FBAR Cryptocurrency Foreign Exchanges: Complete Reporting Guide

If you hold crypto on a foreign exchange, you may need to file an FBAR. The FBAR — the Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts — covers crypto accounts, not just bank accounts. This guide covers FBAR cryptocurrency foreign exchanges reporting: which platforms are foreign, how to find your maximum account value, and what to do if you missed past filings.

## Do Foreign Crypto Exchange Accounts Require FBAR Filing?

Yes. If you hold cryptocurrency on a foreign exchange and your total foreign financial accounts topped $10,000 at any point during the year, you must file an FBAR. This covers crypto accounts — not just standard bank accounts.

This was not always clear. For years, filers debated whether crypto accounts fell under [31 USC 5314](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5314) and the Bank Secrecy Act. However, FinCEN settled this in 2020. The FBAR now covers foreign crypto exchange accounts. So if you hold crypto abroad, you must report it.

## What Is FinCEN's Ruling on FBAR Cryptocurrency Foreign Exchanges?

FinCEN issued a clear ruling on FBAR cryptocurrency foreign exchanges in 2020. Two key documents define the rule: FinCEN Notice 2020-2 and a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) from December 2020. Both confirmed that you must report virtual currency at foreign firms under [31 CFR 1010.350](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350). The pause during rulemaking did not create a lasting waiver. Today, the BSA E-Filing system accepts crypto exchange filings.

For more context, see [FinCEN Notice 2020-2](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2020-12/Notice2020-2.pdf) and the [IRS virtual currency guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/virtual-currencies). If you fail to file, FinCEN can fine you under [31 USC 5321(a)(5)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5321). Fines reach up to $12,909 per non-willful breach.

## Which Crypto Exchanges Are Considered "Foreign" for FBAR?

A crypto exchange counts as "foreign" for FBAR based on where it sits — not where you log in from. Seven major exchanges are foreign: Binance (global), Crypto.com, Bybit, KuCoin, OKX, Bitfinex, and Bitstamp. US-based exchanges like Coinbase, Kraken, and Gemini are domestic and not reportable. ### What About Gray Areas?

Some exchanges use complex setups with entities in many countries. To find out which entity holds your account, check the Terms of Service. That document names the legal party. If your tie is with the foreign entity, the account is foreign.

## How Do You Calculate Maximum Value for Crypto FBAR Accounts?

Finding the max value for a crypto FBAR account works like any other foreign account. But crypto prices move a lot, so this takes more care. Under [31 CFR 1010.350(g)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350), you report the highest account value at any point during the year — not the year-end figure.

Follow these steps:

### Step 1: Find Your Peak Portfolio Value

Review your exchange's account history. Most exchanges provide daily portfolio records. Find the single day — or moment — when your total holdings hit their highest USD value. This includes all assets: BTC, ETH, stablecoins, altcoins, and fiat balances.

### Step 2: Convert to USD

If your exchange shows values in USD, use that figure. If it shows values in another currency, convert to USD. Use the [Treasury Department's December 31 exchange rate](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/treasury-reporting-rates-exchange/treasury-reporting-rates-of-exchange) for this. Most exchanges show USD values, so this step is often simple.

### Step 3: Report the Peak — Not the Year-End Balance

The FBAR requires the maximum value during the year — not the December 31 balance. Crypto is volatile. Your year-end balance may be far lower than your peak.

**Example — Alex's Binance Account:**

Alex, a US citizen, holds an account on Binance (international) with this 2025 activity:

- **January**: Portfolio value ~$15,000 (0.3 BTC, 5 ETH, plus altcoins)
- **March**: Added $20,000; portfolio grew to ~$45,000
- **May**: Market peak pushes portfolio to **$85,000**
- **August**: Correction drops portfolio to ~$52,000
- **December 31**: Portfolio at $61,000

Alex's maximum account value for FBAR is **$85,000** — the May peak, not the year-end figure of $61,000.

## How Do Multiple Foreign Crypto Accounts Work on the FBAR?

Each exchange is a separate foreign account. But multiple sub-accounts on the same exchange count as one account. So you report each exchange as its own line item when you file.

**Example — Taylor's Multi-Exchange Portfolio:**

Taylor holds crypto on three foreign exchanges in 2025:

1. **Binance**: Peak value **$85,000** in May
2. **Crypto.com**: Peak value **$12,000** in September
3. **Bybit**: Peak value **$3,200** in November

Taylor also has Coinbase ($45,000) and a Chase account ($8,000). Those are domestic — not reportable. For FBAR, Taylor reports three foreign accounts with a total peak value of **$100,200**. Each account's peak value appears on the form. Even if Binance alone tops $10,000, you must list all three foreign accounts once you cross the line.

## Does the $10,000 FBAR Threshold Include Both Crypto and Bank Accounts?

Yes. The $10,000 total threshold applies across all foreign financial accounts — crypto and bank accounts combined. You add up the peak values of all your foreign accounts. If the total tops $10,000 at any point during the year, you must file — and report every foreign account. Most crypto holders assume the limit applies per account. It does not.

For example: a foreign bank account with £5,000 ($6,350 USD) plus a Binance account that peaked at $4,500 equals $10,850 in total — over the limit.

**Example — Jamie's Mixed Portfolio:**

- **Bank of Ireland savings account**: Peak balance €6,000 ($6,600 USD)
- **Revolut account** (London-based): Peak balance £2,500 ($3,175 USD)
- **Binance account**: Peak value $4,000
- **Coinbase account**: Peak value $50,000 (domestic — excluded)

Total foreign peak values: $6,600 + $3,175 + $4,000 = **$13,775**

Jamie tops $10,000 and must file, reporting all three foreign accounts. The $50,000 on Coinbase does not matter — Coinbase is a US firm. ## What Are the FBAR Rules for DeFi Protocols and Foreign Platforms?

The FBAR rules for DeFi protocols and foreign platforms are still unclear. FinCEN has not given final guidance on whether DeFi counts as a "financial institution." Therefore, the right treatment depends on the type of platform you use and how it holds your assets.

Here is what we know:

- **Centralized exchanges with DeFi features** (Binance DeFi staking, Crypto.com DeFi wallet): You should report these. The account belongs to a foreign firm.
- **Pure DeFi protocols** (Uniswap, Aave, Compound): These are smart contracts, not banks or brokers. FinCEN has not yet given clear rules on DeFi lending or liquidity spots.
- **Self-custody wallets** (MetaMask, Ledger, Trezor): You hold the private keys. You do not need to report these under current rules.

**The safe approach:** Most tax experts advise reporting large DeFi positions held through foreign platforms. Over-reporting carries no extra penalty. But under-reporting can cost $12,909 or more per breach.

## What Are the Most Common Crypto FBAR Mistakes?

### Mistake 1: Thinking "I Already Pay Taxes on My Crypto"

The FBAR is not a tax form. It is a Bank Secrecy Act report under 31 USC 5314. Even if you report every dollar of crypto gains on Form 8949, you still file the FBAR too. IRS crypto reporting on your tax return does not replace the FBAR. The two filings serve different goals and go to different agencies.

### Mistake 2: Using December 31 Balance Instead of Maximum Value

Crypto is volatile. Your December 31 balance may be 40% below your peak. The FBAR requires the max value during the calendar year. If your Binance portfolio peaked at $120,000 in April but ended at $72,000, you report $120,000.

### Mistake 3: Not Knowing Which Exchange Version You Use

"I use Binance" is not enough detail. Are you on Binance (global) or Binance.US? They are different entities based in different places. Check your account agreement to confirm.

### Mistake 4: Forgetting About Stablecoins

Stablecoins count toward your account value. For example, if you hold $30,000 in USDT on Binance, that is $30,000 in a foreign account — even though stablecoins do not feel like typical crypto.

### Mistake 5: Ignoring Fiat Balances on Foreign Exchanges

If you deposited $15,000 into your Binance account and have not yet converted it, that $15,000 sits at a foreign firm. It counts toward your FBAR value.

### Mistake 6: Treating NFTs Differently

If you hold NFTs on a foreign exchange, their value is part of your account total. An NFT worth $8,000 on a foreign platform adds to your total foreign account balance.

## What Triggers an FBAR Audit for Crypto Holders?

The IRS and FinCEN can flag your account for an FBAR audit in several ways. For instance, common triggers include large wire transfers to foreign exchanges, reporting crypto gains on your tax return without a matching FBAR, and tips from foreign tax bodies under data-sharing treaties. In addition, exchanges like Binance have faced US enforcement actions. Those actions gave regulators access to user data. If you hold crypto on a foreign exchange, the safest path is to file on time and keep records for at least five years.

## What Should You Do If You Haven't Been Reporting Crypto Accounts on the FBAR?

If you held crypto on foreign exchanges without filing FBARs, fix this now. You have three main paths. The FBAR is separate from FATCA — see our [FBAR vs FATCA Form 8938 differences](/blog/fbar-vs-fatca-form-8938-differences) guide for details.

**Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures**: If you have no unreported income — you held crypto but never sold — you can file late FBARs with a cause letter. The IRS does not often charge fines when there is no tax gap.

**Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures**: If you have unreported crypto income (sales gains, staking rewards, DeFi yields), this program lets you file 3 years of amended tax returns and 6 years of FBARs at lower rates. The domestic fine is 5% of the highest total balance. On a $100,000 peak balance, that comes to $5,000.

**Quiet Disclosure** (filing without a formal program): This is risky. If the IRS finds your accounts on its own, full fines apply. Specifically, non-willful fines reach $12,909 per breach. Willful fines reach the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the balance under 31 USC 5321(a)(5). See our guide on [FBAR penalties](/blog/fbar-penalties-what-happens-if-you-dont-file).

## Key Takeaways for Crypto Holders

These are the key points for crypto holders who use foreign exchanges and need to file an FBAR. Each one covers a core part of the filing rules, from which accounts count to how you stay in line.

1. **Foreign crypto exchange accounts are FBAR-reportable** under current FinCEN guidance and proposed rules.
2. **"Foreign" depends on where the exchange is domiciled** — not where you access it. Binance (international) is foreign; Coinbase is domestic.
3. **Max value means peak portfolio value** during the year — not the year-end balance. Crypto swings make this very important.
4. **Crypto and bank accounts add up together** for the $10,000 threshold. A $6,000 foreign bank account plus a $5,000 foreign crypto account requires filing.
5. **Stablecoins, fiat balances, and NFTs on foreign exchanges** all count toward account value.
6. **Self-custody wallets are often not reportable**, but centralized exchange accounts are.
7. **DeFi remains a gray area** — the safe approach is to report positions on foreign-based platforms.
8. **Come into compliance on your own** through Streamlined or Delinquent FBAR procedures. Voluntary disclosure costs far less than enforcement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

These are the most common questions about FBAR cryptocurrency foreign exchanges filing. Each answer covers the core rules for crypto holders who use foreign exchanges. For questions about your own accounts, talk to a tax expert or call the IRS FBAR hotline at 1-866-270-0733.

**Does cryptocurrency on foreign exchanges need to be reported on the FBAR?**

Yes. If your total foreign financial accounts — including crypto on foreign exchanges — topped $10,000 at any point during the year, you must file the FBAR (FinCEN Form 114). This covers crypto exchange accounts at foreign firms, not just standard bank accounts.

**Is Binance considered a foreign exchange for FBAR purposes?**

Binance (the global platform at binance.com) operates from the Cayman Islands. It counts as a foreign exchange for FBAR purposes. Binance.US is a separate US-based entity and does not require FBAR reporting. Check your account agreement to confirm which entity holds your account.

**Do stablecoins count toward the FBAR threshold?**

Yes. Stablecoins such as USDT or USDC held on a foreign exchange count toward your account value. The FBAR limit covers the total value of all assets in the account — including stablecoins, fiat balances, NFTs, and all types of crypto.

**What is the penalty for not reporting a foreign crypto exchange on the FBAR?**

FinCEN can charge up to $12,909 per non-willful breach. For willful breaches, the fine rises to the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the account balance. As a result, voluntary disclosure through the Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures cuts these amounts by a large margin.

**Does DeFi need to be reported on the FBAR?**

FinCEN has not given clear guidance on pure DeFi protocols like Uniswap or Aave. However, you should report centralized exchanges with DeFi features. Tax experts advise reporting DeFi positions on foreign platforms as a safe step.

**How do I calculate the maximum account value for a crypto exchange?**

Find the highest total USD value of your account at any point during the year — not the December 31 balance. Check your exchange's portfolio history for the peak value. Include all assets: crypto, stablecoins, and fiat balances.
