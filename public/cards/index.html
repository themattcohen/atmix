03 — Critical Discovery

eBay Sold Data: The Structural Constraint

A major API deprecation changes the approach to sold comparables — but not the strategy.

⚠
eBay Finding API — Deprecated and Decommissioned
findCompletedItems was deprecated 2020-10-15 and fully decommissioned 2025-02-05. This was the primary mechanism for accessing sold listing data through the standard developer program.
🔒
Marketplace Insights API — Restricted Access
The official replacement (formerly Terapeak) is "Limited Release" — restricted to approved business partners only. Individual developers cannot access it through standard registration. This affects features A1 (Sold Comp Engine), A2 (Deal Score Badges), and D2b (Historical Archive query).
✓
Strategy: Passive Accumulation + Pluggable Architecture
Build with passive accumulation first (fully compliant). Record price and status changes on every sync — when a card disappears from watchlist after a price drop, that's a likely sale. Architecture designed to plug in any data source (130point.com export, future API access) without rebuilding core logic. Every day of accumulation is a permanent data moat advantage.

What 130point.com Proves

130point.com has comprehensive sold data and it's free — proving the data is accessible via alternative means. The question is not "can we get sold data" but "which approach fits the architecture and compliance posture best at each stage."

Data Moat Insight

Every day without an archive is data permanently lost. The historical archive (D2) earns its high score precisely because it is time-sensitive — start passive accumulation immediately even if active querying is blocked.

07 — Competitive Defensibility

Why eBay Will Never Build This

eBay's seller-revenue model creates a structural conflict with every buyer-intelligence feature. This is not a capability gap — it is a strategic impossibility.

✕
Sold Comp Badges
Showing "this card is overpriced vs recent sales" actively discourages bidding and reduces auction revenue. eBay's business model depends on buyers not having this information at point of decision.
✕
Budget Optimizer
Portfolio thinking (optimize across all watchlist items) conflicts with item-by-item impulse buying. eBay benefits from siloed decisions, not integrated portfolio management.
✕
"Should I Wait?" Advisor
Recommending patience is anti-auction psychology. Every "wait" recommendation is a lost sale for eBay. They cannot build a feature that increases buyer patience.
✕
Seller Intelligence
Surfacing seller reputation patterns, pricing strategies, and account behavior would undermine seller trust in the platform and reduce seller-side revenue.
✕
Player Performance Signals
eBay is a general marketplace — building sports-specific intelligence requires domain focus that conflicts with their horizontal market position. They won't build it for cards when they have 100 other categories.
✕
Price Target Alerts
Encouraging buyers to wait for specific price points reduces impulse purchases. eBay's auction design is optimized for urgency and FOMO, not patient price targeting.

The Structural Gap

Every competitor is either (a) a marketplace optimized for sellers, (b) a passive data viewer with no decision-making layer, or (c) a narrow tool solving one piece of the problem. No competitor is building an active buyer decision engine that combines live eBay data, sports signals, portfolio tracking, and sold comp intelligence in a single interface. That is the gap.
