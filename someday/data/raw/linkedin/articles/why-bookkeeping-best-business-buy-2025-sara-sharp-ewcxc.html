Why Bookkeeping Is the Best Business to Buy in 2025

Created on 2025-08-24 15:58

Published on 2025-08-29 14:00

If you’ve been watching the small business acquisition space, you’ve likely heard a dozen theories on the “best business to buy” this year. Car washes. Laundromats. Boat storage. But if you ask me—the deal attorney who sees real buyer behavior every single day—the top business to buy in 2025 is clear:

It’s Bookkeeping.

And Here’s Why.

Whether you're a first-time buyer or an investor looking for a smart bolt-on acquisition, bookkeeping checks more boxes than almost any other industry. Let’s break down what makes it so powerful (and surprisingly hard to find).

1. High Demand, Low Supply

If you've ever tried to find a new bookkeeper, you know the script:

“Let me check if mine is accepting new clients.”

The answer? Often, they’re not. Bookkeepers and accountants are in incredibly high demand—and there's a massive shortage of quality firms. That makes the businesses that do exist more valuable and stickier.

2. Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)

Most bookkeeping practices operate on a monthly retainer model—meaning predictable, recurring income. That’s rare in professional services, and it creates stability that resembles software businesses more than traditional service firms.

You can model cash flow, plan staffing, and make better acquisition financing decisions thanks to this MRR predictability.

3. SBA 7(a) Loans = 100% Financing

Thanks to the SBA’s 7(a) loan program, many qualified buyers can purchase a bookkeeping firm with little or no money down.

The SBA backs up to 90% of the loan, meaning banks are more willing to finance deals that they wouldn’t touch otherwise—especially for “mushy” assets like client lists and goodwill. 

4. No CPA Required

Unlike audit or tax practices that often require licensed CPAs or EAs, pure bookkeeping firms usually don’t. That opens up a wider pool of potential buyers and makes the business more scalable (and more sellable) on the backend.

5. Easy Profit Margin Growth

A million-dollar bookkeeping practice might have a $300K profit when you buy it—but it’s not uncommon to bump that to $500K in year one without even adding clients. How?

  • Offshoring

  • Remote workforce

  • AI-based tools

  • Standardized systems

  • Rate increases (the market often supports it)

The levers are quick to spot and not too hard to pull.

6. Year-Round Cash Flow

Tax practices tend to be seasonal—you either buy before or after tax season, and you float capital in between. But bookkeeping is year-round. That smooths out cash flow and simplifies your projections.

7. It’s the Perfect Complement Business

Already own another business? Bookkeeping fits in beautifully. It's operationally aligned with:

  • E-commerce

  • Agencies

  • Consulting firms

  • Other service-based businesses

  • It can also be brought in-house to support your portfolio (and cut external costs).

The Only Catch? They're Hard to Find.

Bookkeeping businesses with clean MRR and no tax prep are rare. Owners hold onto them tightly for a reason: they’re lifestyle-friendly, stable, and profitable.

If you find one, don’t wait. Do your diligence, run the numbers, and make your move.
